Anatomy sample questions

Q: A 45 year old man got a fracture in skull. X-ray is done they found a diploe structure in the bone . Which kind of bone can it be ?

  1. flat (Correct)
  2. sesamoid
  3. irregular
  4. large
  5. small

Explanation: Diploe is the layer of spongy, cancellous bone sandwiched between the inner and outer tables of compact bone in flat bones of the skull. It contains red marrow and diploic veins. Sesamoid, irregular and other bone types do not show this named sandwich arrangement.

Q: A person is rushed to the hospital after he was stung by a bee in the arm. After a while his skin start to rash and he feels severe pain. Which of the following cells is responsible for the general release of histamine, heparin and leukotrienes related substances??

  1. plasma cells
  2. mast cells (Correct)
  3. neutrophils
  4. macrophages
  5. eosinophils

Explanation: Mast cells in connective tissue store histamine and heparin in granules and synthesize leukotrienes, which are released in allergy and bee-sting reactions causing rash, pain and vasodilation. Plasma cells make antibodies, neutrophils phagocytose bacteria, and eosinophils mainly counter parasites and modulate allergy rather than being the primary source.

Q: A tumour in the posterior mediastinum compresses which structure:

  1. Esophagus (Correct)
  2. Arch of aorta
  3. IVC
  4. Pulmonary trunk
  5. Trachea

Explanation: The esophagus descends through the posterior mediastinum behind the trachea and heart, so a posterior mediastinal tumor compresses it and causes dysphagia. The arch of aorta and pulmonary trunk are in the middle/superior mediastinum, and the trachea is mainly in the superior mediastinum.

Physiology sample questions

Q: A carpenter suffering from diabetes showed increased triglyceride and is hypertensive. There is an increased risk of his suffering from cardiovascular disorders due to:

  1. Age and profession
  2. Triglyceride and hypertension
  3. Diabetes and hypertension (Correct)

Explanation: Diabetes mellitus and hypertension are each major independent cardiovascular risk factors, and together with his raised triglycerides they multiply his risk of coronary and cerebrovascular disease. Age and occupation are non-modifiable/background factors and do not explain the risk as directly as the diabetes-hypertension combination.

Q: People with Limited socio economic status increase the risk of CVD due to:

  1. Limited access to the health care centre
  2. Obesity
  3. Increase exposure to pollutants
  4. Poor diet and smoking (Correct)
  5. Stressful Life and smoking

Explanation: Low socioeconomic status raises cardiovascular risk mainly through behavioural and dietary pathways — an energy-dense poor-quality diet and higher smoking prevalence — together with less preventive care. Limited healthcare access contributes, but the best single answer combines poor diet and smoking as the principal mediators.

Q: A 11 years old male child has extreme discomfort in playing with friends and has poor performance in studies as well. His CBC report shows:

  1. Sickle cell anemia- Hemolysis
  2. Sideroblastic anemia - Iron chelation
  3. Thalassemia minor - Microcytic anemia (Correct)
  4. Occult blood loss- Macrocytic anemia

Explanation: The pairing marked correct is thalassemia minor with microcytic anaemia: defective globin-chain synthesis produces small, poorly haemoglobinised red cells and a mild microcytic anaemia that limits exercise tolerance. Sickle cell anaemia causes haemolysis but thalassemia minor is specifically a microcytic index picture, while occult blood loss would give iron-deficiency indices rather than a globin-synthesis defect.

Biochemistry sample questions

Q: "Euchromatin" and "heterochromatin" represent different states of DNA organization. Which feature is characteristic of euchromatin compared to heterochromatin?

  1. It is highly condensed and transcriptionally inactive.
  2. It lacks histones entirely.
  3. It is relatively decondensed and contains actively transcribed genes. (Correct)
  4. It consists only of circular mitochondrial DNA.
  5. It is found only in the centromere region.

Explanation: Euchromatin is relatively decondensed, lightly staining chromatin containing actively transcribed genes. Heterochromatin is highly condensed and transcriptionally inactive, so it is the opposite state.

Q: A "suicide inhibitor" is a type of irreversible inhibitor that:

  1. Binds noncovalently to the allosteric site.
  2. Is converted by the enzyme itself into a reactive form that kills the enzyme. (Correct)
  3. Increases the Vmax of the reaction.
  4. Only binds to the apoenzyme.
  5. Disappears after the substrate concentration is increased.

Explanation: A suicide inhibitor is first processed by the target enzyme's own catalytic mechanism into a reactive form that then irreversibly inactivates the enzyme. It is therefore mechanism-based and covalent, not a reversible allosteric inhibitor.

Q: A 12-year-old child exhibits signs of accelerated aging and severe atherosclerosis. Genetic testing confirms a mutation in the LMNA gene leading to an unstable nuclear envelope. Which abnormal protein is accumulating in this child's cells?

  1. Phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase
  2. Progerin (Correct)
  3. Dopamine
  4. Cardiolipin
  5. GlcNAc phosphotransferase

Explanation: The accumulating abnormal protein is progerin, a permanently farnesylated truncated lamin A produced by the LMNA mutation in Hutchinson-Gilford progeria. It makes the nuclear envelope unstable and nuclei abnormally shaped.

Why past papers matter for the UHS first professional exam

Paper pattern (MCQ/SEQ split)

The UHS first professional exam combines multiple-choice questions with short essay questions (SEQs), plus practical and viva stations. Past papers show you the real topic weightings, so you study what is actually asked.

Block exams vs send-ups

Under the UHS modular system from 2023, send-ups were replaced by block exams plus internal assessment. This quiz covers block-wise topics so you can revise for the block in front of you.

Frequently asked questions

Is the FMU Past Papers Quiz really free?

What is the difference between a send-up and a proff?

A send-up was the college's internal exam that qualified a student for the annual professional exam (“proff”). Under the UHS modular system from 2023, send-ups were replaced by block exams plus internal assessment — but students still use the word “send-up” for pre-prof preparation.

Can I use this for other universities or NUMS?

The questions follow the UHS 1st-year syllabus, so students at other UHS-affiliated colleges will find them relevant. NUMS has a different board pattern, so a NUMS-specific version is not promised.
