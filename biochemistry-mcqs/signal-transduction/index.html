Signal Transduction MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Signal Transduction

Signal transduction is the process by which a cell converts an external message, such as a hormone, into an internal response. The message binds a receptor on the cell surface or inside the cell, and the receptor then activates a chain of signalling molecules. These chains often amplify the signal, so a small stimulus can produce a large cellular effect.

  1. A heterotrimeric G-protein is composed of which specific subunits?

    • A. Alpha, beta, and delta
    • B. Alpha, beta, and gamma
    • C. Alpha, sigma, and rho
    • D. Beta, gamma, and epsilon
    • E. Two alpha and two beta units
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Alpha, beta, and gamma

    Heterotrimeric G proteins have three different subunits: alpha (which binds GDP/GTP), beta, and gamma, with beta and gamma acting as a tight dimer. Activation occurs when the alpha subunit exchanges GDP for GTP and dissociates from beta-gamma.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  2. A patient is administered a drug that acts as an agonist for alpha-1 adrenergic receptors. This leads to the activation of Phospholipase C and the cleavage of PIP₂. Which product triggers the release of sequestered calcium?

    • A. Diacylglycerol (DAG)
    • B. Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP₃)
    • C. Cyclic AMP (cAMP)
    • D. Phosphatidylcholine
    • E. Phosphatidic acid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP₃)

    Alpha-1 adrenergic receptors act through Gq to activate phospholipase C, which splits PIP2 into IP3 and DAG. IP3 is water-soluble, diffuses to the endoplasmic reticulum, opens IP3-gated Ca2+ channels, and releases sequestered calcium. DAG stays in the membrane and helps activate protein kinase C.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  3. A patient with a neurological disorder is treated with a drug that modifies signal transmission at specialized junctions between nerve cells and their target cells. This form of intercellular communication is known as:

    • A. Endocrine signaling
    • B. Paracrine signaling
    • C. Synaptic signaling
    • D. Autocrine signaling
    • E. Direct contact signaling
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Synaptic signaling

    Communication by neurotransmitters released at specialized nerve-target junctions is synaptic signaling. Endocrine signaling uses blood-borne hormones, paracrine acts on nearby cells, and autocrine acts on the secreting cell itself.

  4. A pharmacology student is researching a new drug that targets a receptor characterized by seven transmembrane alpha-helices. Which type of receptor is the primary target of this research?

    • A. Tyrosine kinase receptor
    • B. Ionotropic receptor
    • C. Nuclear receptor

    • E. Intracellular receptor

    Answer: D. G protein-coupled receptor

    Receptors with seven transmembrane alpha-helices are G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs, serpentine receptors). They signal through heterotrimeric G proteins, unlike single-pass tyrosine kinase receptors or intracellular nuclear receptors.

  5. Binding of Gs to adenylyl cyclase results in:

    • A. Increase ATP production
    • B. Cholesterol accumulation
    • C. Increased CAMP production
    • D. Receptor internalization
    • E. Decrease CAMP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Increased CAMP production

    Gs is the stimulatory G protein: activated Gs alpha stimulates adenylyl cyclase to convert ATP to cyclic AMP (increased cAMP production). Gi inhibits adenylyl cyclase and lowers cAMP.

  6. Calcium mobility from intracellular sources is increased by:

    • A. GP3
    • B. IP₃
    • C. LP3
    • D. CAMP
    • E. DAG
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. IP₃

    Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3), generated from PIP2 by phospholipase C, opens IP3 receptors on the endoplasmic reticulum and increases Ca2+ release from intracellular stores. DAG remains in the membrane and, with Ca2+, activates protein kinase C; cAMP acts mainly through protein kinase A.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  7. Cyclic nucleotides like cAMP and cGMP play critical roles in cellular physiology. What is their primary function in the context of cell signaling?

    • A. Acting as structural components of the ribosome
    • B. Serving as second messengers in signal transduction
    • C. Functioning as high-energy phosphate donors for glycolysis
    • D. Acting as coenzymes for the TCA cycle
    • E. Facilitating the splicing of pre-mRNA in the nucleus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Serving as second messengers in signal transduction

    Cyclic nucleotides cAMP and cGMP act as intracellular second messengers that relay signals from membrane receptors to effectors such as protein kinase A and protein kinase G. They are signaling molecules, not structural ribosomal components or energy donors for glycolysis.

  8. During a laboratory study, a patient's hepatocytes are treated with epinephrine, which binds to alpha-1 receptors. Activation of Protein Kinase C (PKC) is observed. Which membrane-associated molecule directly assists in activating PKC?

    • A. Inositol trisphosphate (IP₃)
    • B. Cyclic AMP (cAMP)
    • C. Diacylglycerol (DAG)
    • D. Phosphatidylinositol
    • E. Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Diacylglycerol (DAG)

    Phospholipase C splits PIP2 into IP3 and diacylglycerol (DAG). DAG remains in the membrane and, together with Ca2+ released by IP3, directly activates protein kinase C (PKC). cAMP activates protein kinase A instead.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  9. G-protein coupled receptors (GPCR). are bound to G-proteins on the cytoplasmic aspect of the cell membrane. What binds with G protein after interaction with GPCR, leading to dissociation of the α subunit from the βγ subunit?

    • A. ADP
    • B. ATP
    • C. AMP
    • D. GDP
    • E. GTP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. GTP

    Activation of a GPCR acts as a guanine nucleotide exchange factor: GDP bound to the G-alpha subunit is replaced by GTP. GTP binding causes the alpha subunit to dissociate from the beta-gamma dimer so both can regulate effectors.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  10. G-Protein coupled receptors:

    • A. Have seven transmembrane domains
    • B. Have single transmembrane domain
    • C. Are located exclusively in the cytoplasm
    • D. Directly interacts with DNA
    • E. A and D
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Have seven transmembrane domains

    G protein-coupled receptors are seven-transmembrane (7TM, serpentine) receptors that cross the membrane seven times and couple to heterotrimeric G proteins on the cytoplasmic side. They do not interact directly with DNA.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  11. Hormones and neurotransmitters are often referred to as "signals", while their membrane-bound detectors are called:

    • A. Effectors
    • B. Second messengers
    • C. Signal detectors or receptors
    • D. Adapters
    • E. Transcription factors
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Signal detectors or receptors

    Hormones and neurotransmitters are first-messenger signals, and the membrane (or intracellular) proteins that detect them are receptors (signal detectors). Binding of signal to receptor initiates intracellular second-messenger cascades.

  12. Hormones secreted into the blood to integrate metabolism in distant tissues represent which form of communication?

    • A. Synaptic signaling
    • B. Endocrine signaling
    • C. Direct contact
    • D. Intracellular communication
    • E. Autocrine signaling
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Endocrine signaling

    Hormones secreted into the bloodstream to act on distant target tissues are endocrine signaling. Synaptic signaling is across a synapse, autocrine acts on the same cell, and direct contact requires cell-cell junctions.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  13. How does cyclic AMP (cAMP) activate Protein Kinase A (PKA)?

    • A. By phosphorylating the catalytic subunits
    • B. By binding to the regulatory subunits and releasing catalytic subunits
    • C. By acting as a cofactor for the kinase active site
    • D. By inducing the synthesis of new PKA molecules
    • E. By inhibiting the phosphoprotein phosphatase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. By binding to the regulatory subunits and releasing catalytic subunits

    Protein kinase A is normally inactive as a regulatory2-catalytic2 tetramer. cAMP binds to the regulatory subunits, causing them to release the catalytic subunits, which then phosphorylate target proteins. cAMP does not phosphorylate PKA itself.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  14. In the Gq signaling pathway, which molecule diffuses through the cytosol to trigger the release of calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum?

    • A. Diacylglycerol (DAG)
    • B. Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP₃)
    • C. Protein kinase C
    • D. cAMP
    • E. GTP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP₃)

    In the Gq pathway, phospholipase C generates IP3 and DAG from PIP2. IP3 is soluble and diffuses through the cytosol to open IP3 receptors on the endoplasmic reticulum, releasing Ca2+. DAG stays in the membrane and activates protein kinase C.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  15. Nitric oxide causes vasodilation by activating guanylyl cyclase, which leads to the rise of which second messenger?

    • A. cAMP
    • B. cGMP
    • C. IP₃
    • D. DAG
    • E. Calcium
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. cGMP

    Nitric oxide diffuses into vascular smooth muscle and activates soluble guanylyl cyclase, which converts GTP to cyclic GMP (cGMP). Raised cGMP lowers calcium and relaxes the muscle, causing vasodilation — cAMP belongs to the adenylyl cyclase pathway.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  16. Pertussis toxin leads to a rise in cAMP in the respiratory tract by specifically inactivating which G-protein?

    • A. Gs
    • B. Gi
    • C. Gq
    • D. Gt
    • E. G12
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Gi

    Pertussis toxin ADP-ribosylates and inactivates Gi, the inhibitory G protein that normally restrains adenylyl cyclase. With Gi switched off, cyclase runs unchecked and cAMP rises; cholera toxin locks Gs on by a different modification.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  17. Phospholipase C catalyzes the cleavage of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP₂) into which two second messengers?

    • A. cAMP and cGMP
    • B. IP₃ and diacylglycerol (DAG)
    • C. ATP and ADP
    • D. Tyrosine and serine
    • E. Inositol and phosphate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. IP₃ and diacylglycerol (DAG)

    Phospholipase C splits the membrane lipid PIP2 into two second messengers: IP3, which releases Ca2+ from the endoplasmic reticulum, and diacylglycerol (DAG), which stays in the membrane and activates protein kinase C. Together they form the classic Gq pathway output.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  18. Protein Kinase C (PKC) activation typically requires the presence of which two components?

    • A. cAMP and PKA
    • B. DAG and Calcium
    • C. IP₃ and GTP
    • D. Insulin and receptor tyrosine kinase
    • E. ATP and adenylyl cyclase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. DAG and Calcium

    Protein kinase C needs both diacylglycerol (DAG) in the membrane and Ca2+ (released via IP3) for full activation in the classic isoforms. This is why the PLC pathway, which produces DAG and raises Ca2+, is its upstream activator — not cAMP/PKA.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  19. The activation of Phospholipase C (PLC) is a hallmark of which G-protein signaling pathway?

    • A. Gs
    • B. Gi
    • C. Gq
    • D. Tyrosine kinase
    • E. Ionotropic
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Gq

    Gq is the G protein whose alpha subunit activates phospholipase C, generating IP3 and DAG. Gs stimulates adenylyl cyclase and Gi inhibits it, so PLC activation is the signature of the Gq pathway.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  20. The Gs protein is named "stimulatory" because it performs which action?

    • A. Stimulates the ligand to bind
    • B. Stimulates the synthesis of GTP
    • C. Stimulates the enzyme adenylyl cyclase
    • D. Stimulates the degradation of cAMP
    • E. Stimulates the opening of potassium channels
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Stimulates the enzyme adenylyl cyclase

    Gs is named stimulatory because its alpha subunit (Gs-alpha-GTP) stimulates adenylyl cyclase to convert ATP to cAMP. Gi does the opposite (inhibits cyclase); Gs neither makes GTP nor degrades cAMP.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  21. The inherent GTPase activity of the G-alpha subunit is crucial for which of the following processes?

    • A. Activation of adenylyl cyclase
    • B. Termination of the signal
    • C. Phosphorylation of protein kinase A
    • D. Binding of the ligand to the receptor
    • E. Synthesis of PIP₂
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Termination of the signal

    G-alpha has built-in GTPase activity that hydrolyses its bound GTP to GDP, switching itself off and ending effector activation. This self-timing is how the signal is terminated; cholera toxin blocks this step and locks the signal on.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  22. The toxin from Vibrio cholerae causes excess cAMP production in intestinal cells by which mechanism?

    • A. Inactivating the Gi protein
    • B. Inhibiting the GTPase activity of Gs-alpha
    • C. Directly stimulating Protein Kinase A
    • D. Cleaving PIP₂ into IP₃ and DAG
    • E. Opening voltage-gated calcium channels
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Inhibiting the GTPase activity of Gs-alpha

    Cholera toxin ADP-ribosylates Gs-alpha so it cannot hydrolyse GTP, locking Gs on and adenylyl cyclase into continuous cAMP production. The resulting chloride and water secretion causes profuse watery diarrhoea; pertussis toxin instead inactivates Gi.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  23. What happens to the G-alpha subunit after it hydrolyzes its bound GTP to GDP?

    • A. It remains bound to the effector enzyme
    • B. It is degraded by proteasomes
    • C. It reassociates with the beta-gamma dimer
    • D. It moves to the nucleus to affect transcription
    • E. It binds another molecule of ATP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. It reassociates with the beta-gamma dimer

    When G-alpha hydrolyses GTP to GDP it switches off, leaves the effector (e.g. adenylyl cyclase), and reassociates with the beta-gamma dimer to reform the inactive heterotrimer. The protein is reused, not degraded, each signalling cycle.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  24. What is the function of cAMP in signal transduction?

    • A. Activation of Gi subunit
    • B. Inhibition of protein synthesis
    • C. Activation of protein kinases
    • D. Stimulation of DNA replication
    • E. Blockade of ion channels
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Activation of protein kinases

    cAMP is a second messenger that binds the regulatory subunits of protein kinase A (PKA), freeing its catalytic subunits to phosphorylate target proteins. It does not directly replicate DNA or block ion channels; its job is kinase activation.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  25. What is the outcome of Gi protein activation in hepatocytes?

    • A. Increased PKA activity
    • B. Inhibition of adenylyl cyclase and decreased cAMP
    • C. Rapid release of calcium from internal stores
    • D. Synthesis of glycogen from DAG
    • E. Activation of the MAP kinase pathway
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Inhibition of adenylyl cyclase and decreased cAMP

    Gi activation inhibits adenylyl cyclase, so ATP conversion to cAMP falls and PKA activity drops in hepatocytes and other cells. This is the mirror image of Gs, which stimulates cyclase and raises cAMP.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  26. Which enzyme is responsible for converting ATP to the second messenger cyclic AMP (cAMP)?

    • A. Phospholipase C
    • B. Protein kinase A
    • C. Adenylyl cyclase
    • D. GTPase
    • E. Phosphodiesterase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Adenylyl cyclase

    Adenylyl cyclase, activated by Gs, converts ATP into the second messenger 3',5'-cyclic AMP (cAMP). Phosphodiesterase does the opposite (breaks cAMP down), and protein kinase A is activated by cAMP rather than making it.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  27. Which event occurs immediately after a ligand binds to a G protein-coupled receptor?

    • A. Hydrolysis of GTP to GDP
    • B. Release of cAMP from the membrane
    • C. Replacement of GDP with GTP on the alpha subunit
    • D. Phosphorylation of the receptor’s tyrosine residues
    • E. Dissociation of the regulatory subunits of PKA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Replacement of GDP with GTP on the alpha subunit

    Immediately after ligand binding, the activated GPCR acts as a guanine-nucleotide exchange factor: GDP leaves the G-alpha subunit and GTP binds in its place, switching G-alpha on. GTP hydrolysis and cAMP changes come later in the cycle.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  28. Which form of signaling involves neurotransmitters being released at specialized junctions between nerve cells and target cells?

    • A. Paracrine
    • B. Endocrine
    • C. Synaptic
    • D. Autocrine
    • E. Direct contact
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Synaptic

    Synaptic signalling is the specialised junctional form where a neuron releases neurotransmitter across a synapse onto a specific target cell. It is faster and more targeted than endocrine (blood-borne) or general paracrine signalling.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Signal Transduction

  29. Adenylyl cyclase activity is increased by which G-protein subunit?

    • A. Alpha s (stimulatory)
    • B. Alpha i (inhibitory)
    • C. Alpha q only
    • D. Beta-gamma inhibitory complex
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Alpha s (stimulatory)

    The Gs alpha subunit (stimulatory) activates adenylyl cyclase and increases cAMP formation. Gi alpha does the opposite and inhibits the enzyme, reducing cAMP.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Signal Transduction
