DNA Structure MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › DNA Structure

DNA is made of two strands wound around each other in a double helix. Each strand consists of a sugar-phosphate backbone with one of four bases attached to each sugar: adenine, thymine, guanine, or cytosine. The strands are complementary, so adenine always pairs with thymine and guanine with cytosine.

  1. A clinician explains to a patient that their DNA is stabilized by base pairing and the stacking of hydrophobic bases inside the molecule. Stacking primarily serves to:

    • A. Increase water interaction
    • B. Minimize water interaction
    • C. Form peptide bonds
    • D. Neutralize phosphate charge
    • E. Shorten the genome
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Minimize water interaction

    Base stacking places hydrophobic bases in the interior of the double helix to minimise their interaction with water, stabilising DNA. Hydrogen bonding between base pairs gives specificity, while stacking gives much of the stability.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  2. In the Watson-Crick model, the helical axis passes through which part of the DNA molecule?

    • A. The major groove
    • B. The minor groove
    • C. The center of the base pairs
    • D. The phosphate groups
    • E. The 5' terminal phosphate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. The center of the base pairs

    In B-DNA the two antiparallel strands wind around a common central axis that runs through the middle of the stacked base pairs. The sugar-phosphate backbones lie on the outside, while the flat bases face inward toward this helical axis.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  3. Nucleic acid strands possess a distinct polarity that dictates the direction of synthesis. Which chemical group is found at the 5' end of a mature DNA strand?

    • A. Free hydroxyl group
    • B. Free phosphate group
    • C. Nitrogenous base
    • D. Methyl group
    • E. N-glycosidic bond
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Free phosphate group

    A nucleic-acid strand has polarity: the 5' end carries a free phosphate group on the 5' carbon, and the 3' end carries a free 3'-OH. New nucleotides are always added to the 3'-OH, so synthesis proceeds 5' to 3'.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  4. Nucleotides are joined together in a linear polymer to form nucleic acids. What is the precise chemical nature and orientation of the bond that links these successive monomers?

    • A. 2'-to-5' phosphodiester bond
    • B. 5'-to-3' N-glycosidic bond
    • C. 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bond
    • D. 1'-to-9' ester linkage
    • E. 3'-to-3' hydrogen bond
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bond

    Successive nucleotides are joined by 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bonds: the 3'-OH of one sugar links through phosphate to the 5' carbon of the next. The N-glycosidic bond instead joins base to sugar within a single nucleotide.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  5. The 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bond connects which two specific atoms?

    • A. 3'-carbon of ribose to 5'-carbon of next ribose
    • B. 3'-OH of deoxyribose to 5'-phosphate of next nucleotide
    • C. Nitrogen-9 of purine to Carbon-1' of sugar
    • D. Oxygen of phosphate to the nitrogenous base
    • E. Two nitrogenous bases on opposite strands
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. 3'-OH of deoxyribose to 5'-phosphate of next nucleotide

    A phosphodiester bond joins nucleotides through phosphate: the 3'-OH of one (deoxy)ribose connects via the phosphate to the 5' position of the next nucleotide — described as 3'-to-5'. The base-to-sugar link is a separate N-glycosidic bond.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  6. The A-form of DNA is characterized by which of the following features?

    • A. Left-handedness
    • B. 12 base pairs per turn
    • C. Base pairs tilted 20 degrees from the perpendicular
    • D. Zigzag phosphate backbone
    • E. Primary form found in chromosomes
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Base pairs tilted 20 degrees from the perpendicular

    A-DNA is a right-handed helix, more compact than B-DNA, with about 11 base pairs per turn and base pairs tilted about 20 degrees from the axis and displaced from it. B-DNA (the usual form in cells) has ~10.5 bp/turn with bases nearly perpendicular.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  7. The pairing of nitrogenous bases in DNA is:

    • A. Guanine and cytosine
    • B. Thymine and guanine
    • C. Adenine and thymine
    • D. Adenine and uracil
    • E. A and C (both A-T and G-C pairing)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. A and C (both A-T and G-C pairing)

    DNA follows Chargaff pairing: adenine pairs with thymine (2 hydrogen bonds) and guanine pairs with cytosine (3 hydrogen bonds). Since options A (G-C) and C (A-T) are each individually correct, the combined option E (both A and C) is the right answer.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  8. The sequence of linked dNMPs in a single strand of DNA is referred to as which level of organization?

    • A. Primary structure
    • B. Secondary structure
    • C. Tertiary structure
    • D. Quaternary structure
    • E. Supercoiling
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Primary structure

    The ordered sequence of deoxyribonucleoside monophosphates (dNMPs) along one strand is the primary structure of DNA — its first level of organisation. The double helix formed by two strands is the secondary structure, and supercoiling is tertiary.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  9. The stacking of hydrophobic bases in the interior of the DNA molecule serves what primary purpose?

    • A. Providing energy for replication
    • B. Creating the major groove
    • C. Minimizing interaction with water
    • D. Forming the 3' hydroxyl group
    • E. Neutralizing phosphate charges
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Minimizing interaction with water

    The flat nitrogenous bases are hydrophobic, so stacking them inside the helix (with the sugar-phosphate backbone outside) minimises their contact with water. This base stacking also stabilises the helix through van der Waals interactions.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  10. The temperature at which one half of the helical structure of a DNA sample is lost and single-stranded regions are formed is called the:

    • A. Denaturation point
    • B. Renaturation temperature
    • C. Melting temperature (Tm)
    • D. Annealing point
    • E. Critical temperature
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Melting temperature (Tm)

    The melting temperature (Tm) is the temperature at which half the DNA duplex has separated into single strands (half the helical structure lost). It rises with GC content because G-C pairs have three hydrogen bonds; it is measured by increased UV absorbance (hyperchromicity).

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  11. The zigzag appearance of the Z-DNA backbone is primarily a result of:

    • A. 10 base pairs per turn
    • B. The left-handed coiling of the helix
    • C. Dehydration of the deoxyribose
    • D. Triple-stranded pairing
    • E. Binding of regulatory proteins
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The left-handed coiling of the helix

    Z-DNA is a left-handed helix with a zigzag sugar-phosphate backbone caused by alternating purine-pyrimidine bases adopting syn/anti conformations. B-DNA and A-DNA are right-handed with smooth backbones.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  12. Under what conditions is the A-form of DNA typically produced from the standard B-form?

    • A. High temperature
    • B. Moderate dehydration
    • C. Presence of alternating purines and pyrimidines
    • D. Exposure to UV radiation
    • E. Binding of histone H1
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Moderate dehydration

    Removing water (moderate dehydration, low humidity or high salt) converts right-handed B-DNA into the shorter, wider A-form. In cells, DNA-RNA hybrids and double-stranded RNA naturally adopt an A-like conformation.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  13. Which component of the DNA double helix is positioned on the exterior of the molecule to interact with the aqueous environment?

    • A. Hydrophobic nitrogenous bases
    • B. Deoxyribose-phosphate backbone
    • C. Hydrogen bonds
    • D. Purine and pyrimidine rings
    • E. Stacked base pairs
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Deoxyribose-phosphate backbone

    The deoxyribose-phosphate backbone is hydrophilic (charged phosphates) and faces outward to interact with water, while the hydrophobic bases are stacked inside away from water. This outside-backbone/inside-bases arrangement defines the double helix surface.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure

  14. Which form of DNA can occur naturally in regions with alternating purines and pyrimidines, such as poly GC sequences?

    • A. A-DNA
    • B. B-DNA
    • C. Z-DNA
    • D. cDNA
    • E. mtDNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Z-DNA

    Left-handed Z-DNA forms in stretches of alternating purines and pyrimidines, classically alternating C-G sequences (poly GC), where bases adopt alternating syn/anti conformations. Ordinary cellular DNA is mainly B-DNA, with A-DNA in dehydrated or hybrid contexts.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure
