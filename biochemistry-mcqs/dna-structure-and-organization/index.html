DNA Structure and Organization MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › DNA Structure and Organization

In cells, DNA is organised with proteins into a compact structure called chromatin. The basic unit of chromatin is the nucleosome, in which DNA winds around histone proteins, and further folding packs long DNA molecules into chromosomes. This organisation protects DNA, fits it inside the nucleus, and controls which genes can be read.

  1. "Euchromatin" and "heterochromatin" represent different states of DNA organization. Which feature is characteristic of euchromatin compared to heterochromatin?

    • A. It is highly condensed and transcriptionally inactive.
    • B. It lacks histones entirely.
    • C. It is relatively decondensed and contains actively transcribed genes.
    • D. It consists only of circular mitochondrial DNA.
    • E. It is found only in the centromere region.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. It is relatively decondensed and contains actively transcribed genes.

    Euchromatin is relatively decondensed, lightly staining chromatin containing actively transcribed genes. Heterochromatin is highly condensed and transcriptionally inactive, so it is the opposite state.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  2. Which level of DNA organization is formed when DNA is wound nearly twice around an octameric core of histone proteins?

    • A. Nucleofilament
    • B. 30-nm fiber
    • C. Nucleosome
    • D. Chromosome
    • E. Plasmid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Nucleosome

    A nucleosome is the first level of chromatin packing: about 146 base pairs of DNA wrap nearly twice around an octamer of histones H2A, H2B, H3 and H4. Further coiling of nucleosomes forms the 30-nm fibre and then chromosomes. A plasmid is separate circular DNA and is not this histone-based unit.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  3. Which of the following base pairs is held together by three hydrogen bonds, contributing to higher structural stability?

    • A. Adenine and Thymine
    • B. Adenine and Uracil
    • C. Guanine and Cytosine
    • D. Guanine and Thymine
    • E. Cytosine and Thymine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Guanine and Cytosine

    Guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds, whereas adenine pairs with thymine (or uracil in RNA) through only two. The extra bond makes G-C-rich DNA more thermally stable. G-T and C-T are not normal Watson-Crick pairs.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  4. Which of the following describes the covalent linkage that joins the deoxyribose of one nucleotide to the deoxyribose of an adjacent nucleotide?

    • A. 5'-to-5' phosphodiester bond
    • B. 3'-to-5' N-glycosidic bond
    • C. 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bond
    • D. 2'-to-5' phosphodiester bond
    • E. Hydrogen bond
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bond

    Adjacent nucleotides are joined by a 3'-to-5' phosphodiester bond between the 3'-hydroxyl of one deoxyribose and the 5'-phosphate of the next. This bond forms the sugar-phosphate backbone. Hydrogen bonds join the bases, not the sugars.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  5. Which of the following interactions is a major factor in stabilizing the double helix by occurring between the planes of the stacked rings?

    • A. Phosphodiester linkages
    • B. Hydrophobic interactions
    • C. Covalent bonds
    • D. Ionic bonds
    • E. Disulfide bridges
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Hydrophobic interactions

    Inside the double helix the flat bases stack on each other, and hydrophobic/stacking interactions between their planes are a major stabilising force. Hydrogen bonds give pairing specificity between bases. Phosphodiester bonds form the backbone but do not stabilise base stacking.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  6. Which of the following is true regarding the structural differences between A-DNA and B-DNA?

    • A. A-DNA is left-handed
    • B. A-DNA has 10 base pairs per turn
    • C. A-DNA base pairs are tilted 20 degrees
    • D. B-DNA is produced by dehydration
    • E. B-DNA base pairs are tilted 40 degrees
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. A-DNA base pairs are tilted 20 degrees

    A-DNA is a right-handed, dehydrated form in which base pairs are tilted about 20 degrees from the helix axis and lie off-centre. B-DNA is the common physiological form with base pairs nearly perpendicular to the axis. A-DNA is not left-handed; Z-DNA is.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  7. Which of the following is true regarding the total amount of purines and pyrimidines in any double-stranded DNA sample?

    • A. Purines always exceed pyrimidines
    • B. Pyrimidines always exceed purines
    • C. Purines (A+G) equal pyrimidines (T+C)
    • D. Adenine equals Guanine
    • E. Thymine equals Cytosine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Purines (A+G) equal pyrimidines (T+C)

    By Chargaff's rules A equals T and G equals C in double-stranded DNA, so total purines (A+G) must equal total pyrimidines (T+C). Adenine does not equal guanine, nor thymine cytosine, unless the base composition happens to be equal.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  8. Which of the following statements about DNA B form is correct:

    • A. DNA B form has a zigzag backbone
    • B. DNA B form is a left-handed helix
    • C. DNA B form is obtained by dehydration
    • D. DNA B form has approximately 10 base pairs per turn
    • E. DNA B form is less stable than DNA A form
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. DNA B form has approximately 10 base pairs per turn

    B-DNA is the right-handed double helix found under normal physiological conditions, with about 10.5 base pairs per complete turn. The zigzag backbone and left-handed helix describe Z-DNA, while dehydration favours A-DNA.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  9. Which specific histone protein is not part of the nucleosome core but binds to the linker DNA between "beads"?

    • A. H2A
    • B. H2B
    • C. H3
    • D. H4
    • E. H1
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. H1

    Histone H1 is the linker histone: it binds DNA at the entry and exit of the nucleosome and to the linker DNA between nucleosomes, helping compact chromatin into the 30-nm fibre. The core octamer contains two copies each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4, not H1.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  10. Which statement regarding Z-DNA is correct?

    • A. It is right-handed
    • B. It contains 11 base pairs per turn
    • C. It occurs in alternating purine/pyrimidine sequences
    • D. Its bases are perpendicular to the axis
    • E. It is the form described by Watson and Crick
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. It occurs in alternating purine/pyrimidine sequences

    Z-DNA is a left-handed helix that forms most readily in alternating purine-pyrimidine sequences, such as CG repeats, and has a zigzag backbone. It is not the Watson-Crick B form and its bases are not arranged like those of right-handed B-DNA.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  11. Which structural form of DNA is a left-handed helix that contains 12 base pairs per turn and a zigzag phosphate backbone?

    • A. A-DNA
    • B. B-DNA
    • C. Z-DNA
    • D. dsRNA
    • E. Poly-A DNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Z-DNA

    Z-DNA is identified by its left-handed helix, about 12 base pairs per turn and a zigzag sugar-phosphate backbone. A-DNA and B-DNA are both right-handed, with B-DNA having about 10 base pairs per turn.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  12. Which structural form of DNA is a right-handed helix with 10 base pairs per 360-degree turn and bases perpendicular to the helical axis?

    • A. A-form
    • B. B-form
    • C. Z-form
    • D. mRNA-DNA hybrid
    • E. Left-handed form
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. B-form

    B-DNA (B-form) is the standard right-handed helix with roughly 10 base pairs per 360-degree turn and base pairs perpendicular to the axis. A-form is shorter/wider with tilted bases, and Z-form is left-handed.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  13. While chromosomal DNA is typically linear in eukaryotes, where is circular double-stranded DNA found in human cells?

    • A. Nucleolus
    • B. Mitochondria
    • C. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
    • D. Golgi apparatus
    • E. Lysosomes
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Mitochondria

    Human mitochondria contain their own small, closed circular double-stranded DNA, inherited maternally, encoding some mitochondrial proteins and RNAs. Nuclear chromosomal DNA is linear. The nucleolus, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi and lysosomes contain no DNA.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  14. While documenting a patient’s genetic sequence, a laboratory technician writes the bases from left to right. By convention, the sequence of a DNA strand is always written in which direction?

    • A. 3′ to 5′
    • B. 5′ to 3′
    • C. N to C terminus
    • D. C to N terminus
    • E. Carboxyl to amino
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. 5′ to 3′

    By convention a nucleic-acid sequence is always written in the 5' to 3' direction, left to right, for both DNA and RNA. N-to-C terminology applies to protein sequences, not nucleotides.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  15. Why does DNA with a high concentration of Guanine and Cytosine require a higher temperature to denature compared to Adenine-Thymine rich DNA?

    • A. GC pairs have more hydrophobic interactions
    • B. GC pairs are joined by three hydrogen bonds
    • C. AT pairs are on the outside of the helix
    • D. GC pairs are larger than AT pairs
    • E. AT pairs have a zigzag backbone
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. GC pairs are joined by three hydrogen bonds

    G-C pairs are held by three hydrogen bonds and also stack strongly, so more heat is needed to separate (melt) GC-rich DNA than AT-rich DNA, which has only two hydrogen bonds per pair. This is why GC content raises the melting temperature, Tm.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization

  16. Why does single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) show a higher relative absorbance at 260 nm than double-stranded DNA (dsDNA)?

    • A. ssDNA has more phosphate groups
    • B. The bases in dsDNA are "unstacked"
    • C. The bases are more exposed in ssDNA
    • D. dsDNA contains uracil
    • E. ssDNA is longer than dsDNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. The bases are more exposed in ssDNA

    When DNA denatures, the bases become unstacked and more exposed to ultraviolet light, so absorbance at 260 nm increases — the hyperchromic effect. Double-stranded DNA therefore absorbs less than the same amount of single-stranded DNA; uracil content and phosphate number do not explain this.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Structure and Organization
