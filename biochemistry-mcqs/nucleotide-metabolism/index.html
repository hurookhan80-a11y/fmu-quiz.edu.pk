Nucleotide Metabolism MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Nucleotide Metabolism

Nucleotide metabolism covers the synthesis and breakdown of purines and pyrimidines, the building blocks of DNA and RNA. Purines are built on a ribose phosphate framework, while pyrimidines are assembled as a ring that is then attached to ribose. Disorders of these pathways can raise uric acid levels or disturb the supply of nucleotides for cell division.

  1. A 25-year-old female with sickle cell anemia is treated with hydroxyurea. In addition to its effects on gene expression, this drug acts as an inhibitor of which enzyme?

    • A. Dihydrofolate reductase
    • B. Thymidylate synthase
    • C. Ribonucleotide reductase
    • D. IMP dehydrogenase
    • E. Adenosine deaminase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Ribonucleotide reductase

    Hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, blocking conversion of ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides needed for DNA synthesis. In sickle cell disease it also raises fetal haemoglobin, but its direct enzyme target is ribonucleotide reductase.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  2. A fundamental distinction between purine and pyrimidine biosynthesis is the timing of attachment to PRPP. How is the pyrimidine ring initially processed?

    • A. Built atom-by-atom directly on PRPP
    • B. Synthesized completely before attachment to PRPP
    • C. Salvaged from extracellular DNA only
    • D. Formed by the degradation of adenine
    • E. Attached to ribose 1-phosphate first
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Synthesized completely before attachment to PRPP

    Pyrimidine synthesis first builds the complete pyrimidine ring from carbamoyl phosphate and aspartate, and only then attaches it to PRPP to form orotidine monophosphate. Purine synthesis is the opposite strategy: the purine ring is built atom by atom directly on PRPP.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  3. A male patient is diagnosed with Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, leading to an overproduction of double-ringed purine bases. During the de novo synthesis of these purines, which amino acid provides the C4, C5, and N7 atoms to the heterocyclic skeleton?

    • A. Aspartate
    • B. Glutamine
    • C. Glycine
    • D. Serine
    • E. Proline
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Glycine

    In de novo purine synthesis, glycine contributes C4, C5, and N7 of the purine ring — the largest single amino-acid contribution. Aspartate, glutamine, CO2, and formyl-THF supply the remaining atoms. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is complete HGPRT deficiency with purine overproduction.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  4. A methotrexate-resistant tumor shows increased dihydrofolate reductase amount. This is an example of:

    • A. Covalent modification by phosphorylation
    • B. Allosteric inhibition by end product
    • C. Increase in gene copy number (gene amplification)
    • D. Competitive binding of a trans-acting factor to the promoter
    • E. Zymogen activation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Increase in gene copy number (gene amplification)

    Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR). Resistant tumor cells often amplify the DHFR gene, increasing gene copy number and enzyme amount so more drug is required for inhibition. This is gene amplification, not covalent modification or zymogen activation.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  5. A patient with a herpes simplex virus infection is treated with acyclovir. once activated by viral and cellular kinases, this purine analog specifically inhibits which process?

    • A. Viral protein translation
    • B. Viral DNA polymerase activity
    • C. Host cell RNA transcription
    • D. Addition of the 5' cap to mRNA
    • E. Removal of introns during splicing
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Viral DNA polymerase activity

    Acyclovir is a guanosine analog that is phosphorylated first by viral thymidine kinase and then by cellular kinases. The active triphosphate inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates viral DNA chain synthesis because it lacks a proper 3'-OH. It does not primarily block translation or splicing.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  6. A patient with cancer is treated with 5-fluorouracil, which inhibits the conversion of dUMP to dTMP. This enzymatic step involves adding a methyl group to the number 5 carbon of the pyrimidine ring. Which base is the methylated product?

    • A. Adenine
    • B. Guanine
    • C. Cytosine
    • D. Thymine
    • E. Uracil
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Thymine

    Thymidylate synthase methylates dUMP at carbon 5 to form dTMP, whose base is thymine (5-methyluracil). 5-Fluorouracil blocks this step, depriving cells of thymidine for DNA synthesis.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  7. A patient with HIV is treated with AZT (Zidovudine). This pyrimidine analog arrests viral replication because its structure specifically lacks which functional group required for DNA chain elongation?

    • A. 5′-phosphate group
    • B. 3′-hydroxyl group
    • C. 2′-hydroxyl group
    • D. Nitrogenous base
    • E. N-glycosidic bond
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. 3′-hydroxyl group

    AZT (zidovudine) is a nucleoside analog whose sugar lacks a 3'-hydroxyl group. Once incorporated into growing viral DNA it acts as a chain terminator, because the next phosphodiester bond cannot form.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  8. A patient with leukemia is treated with methotrexate to limit the amount of THF available for purine synthesis. This drug acts as a structural analog of which essential vitamin?

    • A. Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)
    • B. Folic acid (Folate)
    • C. Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid)
    • D. Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)
    • E. Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Folic acid (Folate)

    Methotrexate is a structural analog of folic acid (folate) and competitively inhibits dihydrofolate reductase. This reduces tetrahydrofolate (THF) available for purine and thymidylate synthesis, limiting DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  9. A patient with painful joints, kidney stones and self-mutilation (lip/finger biting) has high uric acid and complete HGPRT deficiency. The diagnosis is:

    • A. Adrenoleukodystrophy
    • B. Fabry disease
    • C. Kabuki syndrome
    • D. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome
    • E. Rickets
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome

    Complete deficiency of HGPRT, the purine salvage enzyme, causes Lesch-Nyhan syndrome. Failed salvage increases purine degradation to uric acid (gout, kidney stones) and produces the characteristic self-mutilation such as lip and finger biting.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  10. Acyclovir is a synthetic analog used to treat herpes simplex virus infections. What is its mechanism of action once it is converted to its active form?

    • A. Inhibition of the viral RNA polymerase
    • B. Termination of the host cell’s protein synthesis
    • C. Inhibition of the viral DNA polymerase
    • D. Prevention of the 3' poly-A tail addition
    • E. Degradation of the viral envelope lipids
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Inhibition of the viral DNA polymerase

    After activation to acyclovir triphosphate, acyclovir inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates viral DNA synthesis. It is selective because viral thymidine kinase performs the first activating phosphorylation much more efficiently than host enzymes.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  11. Allopurinol is putinol analog what is metabolism of allopurinol

    • A. competitive inhibitor of xanthine oxidase
    • B. competitive inhibitor of glucose 6-phosphate
    • C. competitive inhibitor of lactate dehydrogenase
    • D. competitive inhibitor of DNA hydrogenase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. competitive inhibitor of xanthine oxidase

    Allopurinol is a structural analog of hypoxanthine (a purine analog) and competitively inhibits xanthine oxidase. Blocking xanthine oxidase reduces conversion of hypoxanthine/xanthine to uric acid and is used in gout.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  12. Allopurinol lowers urate by inhibiting xanthine oxidase. How does it affect the enzyme?

    • A. Binds an allosteric site to decrease Vmax
    • B. Acts as a structural analog competing for the active site
    • C. Binds only to the ES complex to lower Km
    • D. Permanently denatures the apoenzyme
    • E. Prevents association of the metal cofactor
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Acts as a structural analog competing for the active site

    Allopurinol acts as a structural analog of the natural substrate and competes at the active site of xanthine oxidase; its metabolite oxypurinol also inhibits the enzyme. This competitive/active-site inhibition reduces uric acid production rather than denaturing the enzyme or acting at a separate allosteric site.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  13. Certain nucleoside analogs like AZT (Zidovudine) and dideoxyinosine (ddI) are used to treat viral infections. How do these analogs specifically arrest the process of viral replication?

    • A. By inhibiting the synthesis of the 5' cap
    • B. By acting as DNA chain terminators due to the lack of a 3'-OH group
    • C. By preventing the transport of mRNA out of the nucleus
    • D. By stabilizing the DNA double helix during heat denaturation
    • E. By irreversibly binding to the large ribosomal subunit
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. By acting as DNA chain terminators due to the lack of a 3'-OH group

    Nucleoside analogs such as AZT and ddI lack a 3'-hydroxyl group on their sugar. After incorporation into growing DNA they act as chain terminators because no next phosphodiester bond can be formed.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  14. Chemically, the pyrimidine thymine differs from uracil by the presence of a specific functional group at the number 5 carbon. Which of the following identifies this group?

    • A. Hydroxyl group
    • B. Methyl group
    • C. Amino group
    • D. Carboxyl group
    • E. Phosphate group
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Methyl group

    Thymine is chemically 5-methyluracil: it differs from uracil only by a methyl group at carbon 5 of the pyrimidine ring. Thymine is used in DNA, uracil in RNA.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  15. During the de novo synthesis of the purine ring, multiple components contribute individual atoms. Which of the following molecules provides the largest single contribution to the purine skeleton?

    • A. Aspartate
    • B. Glutamine
    • C. Glycine
    • D. Carbon dioxide
    • E. N10-formyl-tetrahydrofolate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Glycine

    Glycine provides three atoms of the purine ring — C4, C5, and N7 — the largest single contribution by one molecule. Other atoms come from aspartate (N1), glutamine (N3, N9), CO2 (C6), and N10-formyl-THF (C2, C8).

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  16. Following a kidney transplant, a patient is prescribed mycophenolic acid to prevent graft rejection. Which enzyme does this drug inhibit to deprive proliferating T cells of GMP?

    • A. PRPP synthetase
    • B. Adenylosuccinate synthetase
    • C. IMP dehydrogenase
    • D. Xanthine oxidase
    • E. Ribonucleotide reductase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. IMP dehydrogenase

    Mycophenolic acid reversibly inhibits IMP dehydrogenase, the rate-limiting enzyme for de novo synthesis of guanine nucleotides (GMP). Proliferating T and B lymphocytes depend on this pathway, so they are selectively deprived of GMP and graft rejection is prevented.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  17. In prokaryotes, which group of synthetic analogs inhibits bacterial growth by competing with para- aminobenzoic acid (PABA) during folic acid synthesis?

    • A. Aminoglycosides
    • B. Sulfonamides
    • C. Tetracyclines
    • D. Fluoroquinolones
    • E. Cephalosporins
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Sulfonamides

    Sulfonamides are structural analogs of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and competitively inhibit bacterial synthesis of folic acid, which bacteria must make themselves. Human cells take up preformed folate from the diet, so they are relatively spared.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  18. In transplant surgery, which drug is administered to lower the risk of organ rejection?

    • A. Penicillin
    • B. Warfarin
    • C. Azathioprine
    • D. Paracetamol
    • E. Analgesics
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Azathioprine

    Azathioprine is a purine antimetabolite converted to 6-mercaptopurine, which blocks purine synthesis and lymphocyte proliferation. This immunosuppression lowers the risk of graft rejection; antibiotics and analgesics do not suppress the immune response.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  19. Methotrexate is a potent anticancer drug that interferes with nucleotide metabolism. Which enzyme is its primary target, leading to tetrahydrofolate deficiency?

    • A. Thymidylate synthase
    • B. IMP dehydrogenase
    • C. Dihydrofolate reductase
    • D. PRPP synthetase
    • E. Adenosine deaminase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Dihydrofolate reductase

    Methotrexate tightly inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, blocking regeneration of tetrahydrofolate (THF). Without THF one-carbon units, thymidylate and purine synthesis fail, so rapidly dividing cancer and immune cells cannot make DNA.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  20. Mycophenolic acid is used as an immunosuppressant to prevent organ transplant rejection. Which enzyme does it inhibit to deprive T and B cells of necessary GMP?

    • A. Adenylosuccinate synthetase
    • B. PRPP amidotransferase
    • C. IMP dehydrogenase
    • D. Xanthine oxidase
    • E. Nucleoside diphosphate kinase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. IMP dehydrogenase

    Mycophenolic acid reversibly inhibits IMP dehydrogenase, the rate-limiting step for de novo GMP (guanine nucleotide) synthesis. Lymphocytes depend heavily on this pathway, so T and B cells are selectively starved of GMP and cannot proliferate.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  21. Nucleotides are components of several essential coenzymes. Which of the following coenzymes contains an adenine nucleotide as part of its structure?

    • A. Thiamine pyrophosphate
    • B. Pyridoxal phosphate
    • C. Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺)
    • D. Tetrahydrobiopterin
    • E. Ascorbic acid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺)

    NAD+ is nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — it literally contains an adenine nucleotide (AMP) joined to nicotinamide mononucleotide. FAD and coenzyme A also contain adenine nucleotides; thiamine pyrophosphate and pyridoxal phosphate do not.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  22. Orotic aciduria is treated by:

    • A. Adenine
    • B. Guanosine
    • C. Uridine
    • D. Cytosine
    • E. Inosine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Uridine

    In orotic aciduria the de novo pyrimidine pathway is blocked (UMP synthase defect); giving uridine provides ready-made pyrimidines by salvage, bypassing the block. Uridine also feeds back to reduce orotate production, so it both replaces and corrects.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  23. PRPP (5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate) is an "activated pentose" essential for nucleotide pathways. Which condition activates the enzyme PRPP synthetase?

    • A. High levels of purine ribonucleotides
    • B. Presence of inorganic phosphate
    • C. Accumulation of uric acid
    • D. Inhibition by 5-fluorouracil
    • E. Low concentrations of ATP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Presence of inorganic phosphate

    PRPP synthetase makes PRPP from ribose-5-phosphate and ATP and is activated by inorganic phosphate (Pi), signalling plenty of raw material. It is feedback-inhibited by purine ribonucleotides (AMP, GMP), the end products of the pathways PRPP feeds.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  24. Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID) can be caused by a deficiency of adenosine deaminase (ADA). The toxicity in this condition is primarily due to the accumulation of which compound?

    • A. Uric acid
    • B. cAMP
    • C. dATP
    • D. Hypoxanthine
    • E. PRPP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. dATP

    Without adenosine deaminase (ADA), deoxyadenosine is converted to dATP, which accumulates and inhibits ribonucleotide reductase. Lymphocytes then cannot make other deoxyribonucleotides for DNA, fail to develop, and severe combined immunodeficiency results.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  25. The catabolism of purines will produce:

    • A. Amino acids
    • B. Nucleotides
    • C. Nucleic acids
    • D. Uric acid
    • E. All of the above
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Uric acid

    Humans lack uricase, so purine catabolism ends at uric acid: AMP/GMP are broken down through hypoxanthine and xanthine, with xanthine oxidase making uric acid for excretion. Excess uric acid precipitates as gout.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  26. The conversion of ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides is essential for DNA synthesis. Which drug acts as a specific inhibitor of the enzyme ribonucleotide reductase?

    • A. 5-Fluorouracil
    • B. Methotrexate
    • C. Hydroxyurea
    • D. Cytarabine
    • E. Acyclovir
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Hydroxyurea

    Hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, the enzyme that converts ribonucleotides (NDPs) to deoxyribonucleotides (dNDPs) for DNA synthesis. Blocking it starves dividing cells of DNA precursors; methotrexate and 5-FU act on folate/thymidylate instead.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  27. The drug cytarabine (araC) is used in the treatment of certain leukemias. What structural modification allows it to interfere with DNA synthesis?

    • A. A fluorine atom at the 5-position of the base
    • B. The substitution of ribose with the sugar arabinose
    • C. The removal of the 3'-hydroxyl group
    • D. The addition of a methyl group to the phosphate
    • E. The use of a sulfur atom in the purine ring
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The substitution of ribose with the sugar arabinose

    Cytarabine (ara-C) is cytosine joined to arabinose instead of ribose — the 2'-OH is in the opposite orientation. Once phosphorylated it inhibits DNA polymerase and terminates the chain, which is why it works in rapidly dividing leukaemias.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  28. The regulation of ribonucleotide reductase is complex. Which molecule acts as an overall inhibitor of the enzyme’s catalytic activity at its "activity site"?

    • A. ATP
    • B. dGTP
    • C. dTTP
    • D. dATP
    • E. dCTP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. dATP

    Ribonucleotide reductase is switched off overall when dATP binds its allosteric activity site, signalling that deoxyribonucleotides are plentiful. ATP at the same site activates it; dGTP/dTTP at the specificity site only change which substrate is reduced, not overall activity.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  29. The synthetic analog 5-fluorouracil is frequently used in cancer chemotherapy. What is the precise biochemical mechanism by which this drug inhibits DNA synthesis?

    • A. It inhibits ribonucleotide reductase directly.
    • B. It acts as a suicide inhibitor of thymidylate synthase.
    • C. It prevents the formation of the N-glycosidic bond.
    • D. It competitively inhibits dihydrofolate reductase.
    • E. It blocks the salvage of hypoxanthine by HGPRT.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. It acts as a suicide inhibitor of thymidylate synthase.

    5-Fluorouracil is converted to 5-FdUMP, which forms a permanent (covalent) dead-end complex with thymidylate synthase and its folate cofactor — a suicide inhibition. Without thymidylate (dTMP), DNA synthesis stops in rapidly dividing cancer cells.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  30. Which synthetic analog is used to treat gout by inhibiting xanthine oxidase, thereby reducing the production of uric acid?

    • A. Probenecid
    • B. Allopurinol
    • C. Colchicine
    • D. Sulfinpyrazone
    • E. Methotrexate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Allopurinol

    Allopurinol, a hypoxanthine analogue, inhibits xanthine oxidase so less uric acid is formed and the more soluble precursors hypoxanthine and xanthine are excreted instead. Probenecid and sulfinpyrazone increase uric-acid excretion, colchicine treats acute gout inflammation, and methotrexate is not a gout drug.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism

  31. The rate-limiting, regulated step of de novo pyrimidine synthesis in mammals is catalysed by cytosolic:

    • A. Orotidylic acid decarboxylase
    • B. Dihydrofolate reductase
    • C. HGPRT
    • D. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase-II (CPS-II)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase-II (CPS-II)

    Cytosolic CPS-II (glutamine-dependent, activated by PRPP and inhibited by UTP) is the committed, rate-limiting step of pyrimidine synthesis — unlike mitochondrial CPS-I of the urea cycle, it is not activated by N-acetylglutamate. HGPRT is a purine salvage enzyme, and dihydrofolate reductase supplies folate for thymidylate/purine synthesis.

    Source: Lippincott Biochemistry — Pyrimidine Synthesis; Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Nucleotide Metabolism
