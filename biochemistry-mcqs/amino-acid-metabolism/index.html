Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Amino Acid Metabolism

Amino acid metabolism covers how the body builds, breaks down, and interconverts amino acids. Amino acids not used for protein synthesis lose their nitrogen as ammonia, which the liver converts to urea for excretion. Their remaining carbon skeletons can be used for energy or to make glucose and fats.

  1. A patient with jaundice and RUQ pain has highly elevated ALT. ALT catalyzes:

    • A. Oxidative deamination of glutamate
    • B. Transfer of an amino group from alanine to alpha-ketoglutarate
    • C. Hydrolysis of C-terminal peptide bonds
    • D. Phosphorylation of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate
    • E. Decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Transfer of an amino group from alanine to alpha-ketoglutarate

    Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) reversibly transfers the amino group of alanine to alpha-ketoglutarate, forming pyruvate and glutamate. ALT is abundant in hepatocytes, so leakage into plasma indicates hepatocellular injury. Oxidative deamination of glutamate is by glutamate dehydrogenase, not ALT.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  2. Age-related neurodegeneration involves mitochondrial damage and the aggregation of specific proteins. Which protein's misfolding and subsequent accumulation into Lewy bodies is a central hallmark of this pathology?

    • A. Beta-amyloid
    • B. Tau protein
    • C. Alpha-synuclein
    • D. Progerin
    • E. Prelamin A
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Alpha-synuclein

    Lewy bodies in Parkinson disease are neuronal inclusions composed mainly of misfolded, aggregated alpha-synuclein. Beta-amyloid plaques and tau tangles are characteristic of Alzheimer disease; progerin is associated with progeria.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  3. Amino acids having both gluccogenic and ketogenic prop .

    • A. Phenylalanine
    • B. Leucine
    • C. Lysine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Phenylalanine

    Phenylalanine is both glucogenic and ketogenic: its degradation yields fumarate (glucogenic) and acetoacetate (ketogenic). Leucine and lysine are purely ketogenic and cannot give net glucose.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  4. An elderly patient with resting tremors and muscular rigidity is diagnosed with Parkinson's disease. The clinician explains that the condition is linked to the aggregation of alpha-synuclein. These aggregates are known as which of the following?

    • A. Inclusion bodies
    • B. Lewy bodies
    • C. Residual bodies
    • D. Dictyosomes
    • E. Caveolae
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Lewy bodies

    Aggregates of misfolded alpha-synuclein inside neurons are called Lewy bodies, the pathological hallmark of Parkinson disease. They are associated with loss of dopaminergic neurons, resting tremor, and rigidity.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  5. At a pH below its isoelectric point (pI), an amino acid exists predominantly as:

    • A. A cation (positively charged)
    • B. An anion (negatively charged)
    • C. A zwitterion (net zero charge)
    • D. A fully undissociated neutral molecule
    • E. A weak acid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. A cation (positively charged)

    At pH below its isoelectric point (pI), an amino acid gains protons: the carboxyl group is -COOH and the amino group is -NH3+, giving a net positive charge. It therefore exists predominantly as a cation; above the pI it is an anion, and at the pI it is a zwitterion with net zero charge.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  6. Elevated levels of Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) in the blood plasma are most indicative of damage to which organ?

    • A. Heart
    • B. Pancreas
    • C. Liver
    • D. Skeletal muscle
    • E. Brain
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Liver

    Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) is concentrated in hepatocytes, so raised plasma ALT is most indicative of liver (hepatocellular) damage. AST is less specific and also rises with heart and skeletal muscle injury.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  7. Which molecule serves as the source of nitrogen for the synthesis of nitric oxide, a local mediator in the vascular endothelium?

    • A. Lysine
    • B. Arginine
    • C. Glutamine
    • D. Asparagine
    • E. Glycine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Arginine

    Nitric oxide synthase converts L-arginine, using oxygen and NADPH, into nitric oxide plus citrulline. Endothelial NO then diffuses to vascular smooth muscle and causes vasodilatation. The other amino acids listed are not the substrate for this reaction.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism

  8. Transaminases (aminotransferases) require which coenzyme?

    • A. Biotin
    • B. Cobalamin (vitamin B12)
    • C. Thiamine pyrophosphate
    • D. Pyridoxine (pyridoxal phosphate, vitamin B6)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Pyridoxine (pyridoxal phosphate, vitamin B6)

    Aminotransferases use pyridoxal phosphate, the active form of vitamin B6, to transfer amino groups. Biotin is used by carboxylases, not by transaminases.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Amino Acid Metabolism
