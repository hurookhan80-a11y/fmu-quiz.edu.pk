Protein Synthesis MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Protein Synthesis

Protein synthesis is the process by which the information in a gene is used to build a protein. Messenger RNA carries the gene's code to the ribosome, where transfer RNA molecules deliver amino acids in the correct order. The amino acids are linked into a chain that folds into a working protein.

  1. A laboratory technician examines a sample from a patient's abscess and identifies a microorganism where RNA and protein synthesis occur in the same compartment. Which type of cell is the technician most likely observing?

    • A. Fungal cell
    • B. Animal cell
    • C. Plant cell
    • D. Prokaryotic cell
    • E. Protist
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Prokaryotic cell

    Prokaryotic cells have no membrane-bound nucleus, so transcription and translation occur in the same compartment and are coupled. In eukaryotes, transcription occurs in the nucleus and translation in the cytoplasm, so they are separated in space and time.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  2. A modified base, dihydrouridine, is often found in tRNA at:

    • A. Acceptor arm
    • B. D arm
    • C. Variable loop
    • D. Anticodon arm
    • E. TΨC arm
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. D arm

    The D arm (dihydrouridine arm) of tRNA is named for its dihydrouridine residues, a modified base characteristic of tRNA. The acceptor arm carries the amino acid at the 3' CCA end, and the anticodon arm pairs with mRNA.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  3. A patient with a bacterial infection is treated with an antibiotic that targets the ribozyme activity of the large ribosomal subunit. Which RNA species in prokaryotes possesses this catalytic peptidyl transferase activity?

    • A. 5S rRNA
    • B. 16S rRNA
    • C. 23S rRNA
    • D. 18S rRNA
    • E. 28S rRNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 23S rRNA

    In prokaryotes the peptidyl transferase activity that forms peptide bonds resides in the 23S rRNA of the large (50S) ribosomal subunit — a ribozyme. The 16S rRNA is in the small subunit and helps position mRNA; 18S and 28S are eukaryotic.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  4. A researcher identifying a ribozyme that catalyzes peptide bond formation during translation in a prokaryote is most likely studying which specific RNA species?

    • A. 16S rRNA
    • B. 5S rRNA
    • C. 23S rRNA
    • D. 28S rRNA
    • E. 18S rRNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 23S rRNA

    The ribozyme with peptidyl transferase activity in prokaryotes is the 23S rRNA of the large (50S) subunit; ribosomal RNA, not protein, catalyzes peptide bond formation. The 16S rRNA belongs to the small subunit, while 18S/28S are eukaryotic.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  5. During a laboratory study of a patient's protein synthesis, a specific amino acid is observed attaching to a tRNA. To which sequence at the 3′ end of the tRNA is the amino acid covalently linked?

    • A. AAUAAA
    • B. Shine-Dalgarno
    • C. TATA box
    • D. –CCA
    • E. AUG
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. –CCA

    Every tRNA ends at its 3' end with the sequence -CCA, and the amino acid is covalently attached (ester bond) to the adenosine (A) of this CCA. AAUAAA is a polyadenylation signal and AUG is the start codon.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  6. In prokaryotes, the 16S rRNA of the small ribosomal subunit facilitates translation initiation by base-pairing with which mRNA sequence?

    • A. TATA box
    • B. Pribnow box
    • C. Shine–Dalgarno sequence
    • D. Polyadenylation signal
    • E. 5′ Cap structure
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Shine–Dalgarno sequence

    In prokaryotes the Shine-Dalgarno sequence, a purine-rich sequence upstream of the start codon, base-pairs with the complementary 3' end of 16S rRNA in the small ribosomal subunit. This positions the ribosome correctly for initiation of translation. TATA/Pribnow boxes are promoter elements for transcription, not ribosome binding.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  7. In the structural organization of transfer RNA (tRNA), where is the specific amino acid covalently attached, and what is the nature of the chemical linkage?

    • A. 5′ terminus via a phosphodiester bond
    • B. Anticodon loop via hydrogen bonding
    • C. 3′ terminus hydroxyl group via an ester linkage
    • D. D-loop via a peptide bond
    • E. Variable loop via a disulfide bridge
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 3′ terminus hydroxyl group via an ester linkage

    Every tRNA ends in -CCA at its 3' end, and the amino acid is joined to the 3'-OH of the terminal adenosine. The link is a high-energy ester bond made by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase; the anticodon at the opposite end only reads the mRNA codon and does not carry the amino acid.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  8. Ribosomes contain three binding sites for tRNA molecules. Which site is occupied by the peptidyl-tRNA, which carries the growing chain of amino acids?

    • A. A site
    • B. P site
    • C. E site
    • D. I site
    • E. Promoter site
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. P site

    During elongation the growing polypeptide chain sits on the peptidyl-tRNA in the P (peptidyl) site. The incoming aminoacyl-tRNA enters the A site, the chain is transferred to it, and the empty tRNA leaves via the E site.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  9. The enzyme aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase ensures high fidelity in translation by performing which of the following tasks?

    • A. Aligning the mRNA on the 16S rRNA
    • B. Catalyzing the formation of the 5′-to-5′ triphosphate cap
    • C. Covalently attaching a specific amino acid to its cognate tRNA
    • D. Excising introns from the primary transcript
    • E. Adding the –CCA sequence to the 3′ end of tRNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Covalently attaching a specific amino acid to its cognate tRNA

    Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases attach each amino acid to its correct (cognate) tRNA, forming aminoacyl-tRNA, and proofread to keep translation accurate. This charging step, not the ribosome, decides which amino acid matches each tRNA anticodon.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  10. The tertiary structure of tRNA is often described as being in which of the following shapes?

    • A. Cloverleaf
    • B. Linear
    • C. Circular
    • D. L-shaped
    • E. Triple helix
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. L-shaped

    tRNA folds from its cloverleaf secondary structure into a compact L-shaped tertiary structure by additional base pairing and stacking. The anticodon sits at one end of the L and the amino-acid-bearing CCA at the other, fitting the ribosome's sites.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  11. Transfer RNA molecules are known for containing a high percentage of "unusual" bases. Which of the following is a modified base commonly found in tRNA?

    • A. 7-methylguanosine
    • B. Pseudouracil
    • C. Adenosine triphosphate
    • D. Deoxythymidine
    • E. 5-methylcytosine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Pseudouracil

    tRNAs contain many post-transcriptionally modified (unusual) bases, classically pseudouridine (psi, in the TpsiC arm) and dihydrouridine. These modifications fine-tune tRNA folding and ribosome interaction; 7-methylguanosine is instead the mRNA cap.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  12. What is the function of the –CCA sequence located at the 3′ end of all mature tRNA molecules?

    • A. It serves as the anticodon for mRNA binding.
    • B. It provides the site for amino acid attachment.
    • C. It is the recognition site for the ribosome small subunit.
    • D. It initiates the splicing of pre-tRNA introns.
    • E. It acts as a termination signal for translation.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. It provides the site for amino acid attachment.

    The universal -CCA at the 3' end of mature tRNA is the amino-acid attachment site — the terminal adenosine's 3'-OH forms the ester bond with the amino acid. The anticodon is at the opposite end of the molecule (middle loop), not at the CCA.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  13. What is the sedimentation coefficient (S value) of a complete eukaryotic ribosome?

    • A. 50S
    • B. 60S
    • C. 70S
    • D. 80S
    • E. 100S
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. 80S

    The complete eukaryotic ribosome is 80S, assembled from a 60S large and a 40S small subunit. Prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S (50S + 30S) — a classic distinction and the basis for selective antibiotic action.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  14. What is the sedimentation coefficient of the large ribosomal subunit in eukaryotic cells, and which rRNA species does it contain?

    • A. 50S; contains 23S and 5S rRNA
    • B. 60S; contains 28S, 5.8S, and 5S rRNA
    • C. 40S; contains 18S rRNA
    • D. 80S; contains 28S and 18S rRNA
    • E. 30S; contains 16S rRNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. 60S; contains 28S, 5.8S, and 5S rRNA

    The eukaryotic large subunit is 60S and contains three rRNAs: 28S, 5.8S and 5S (plus many proteins). The 50S/23S combination belongs to prokaryotes, and 18S rRNA is in the eukaryotic small (40S) subunit.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  15. Which of the following describes the relationship between the tRNA anticodon and the mRNA codon during translation?

    • A. Parallel and complementary
    • B. Antiparallel and non-complementary
    • C. Antiparallel and complementary
    • D. Parallel and identical
    • E. Random and non-specific
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Antiparallel and complementary

    The tRNA anticodon binds the mRNA codon in an antiparallel and complementary manner, following base-pairing rules with wobble allowed at the third codon position. It is neither parallel nor identical in sequence. This alignment ensures the correct amino acid is added during translation.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis

  16. Which statement accurately describes the "Wobble Hypothesis" regarding the interaction between the tRNA anticodon and the mRNA codon?

    • A. The first base of the codon pairs strictly with the third base of the anticodon.
    • B. The 5′ base of the anticodon can form nontraditional pairs with the 3′ base of the codon.
    • C. All three bases of the codon must follow traditional Watson-Crick rules.
    • D. The 3′ base of the anticodon determines the specificity of the amino acid.
    • E. Wobble allows one codon to be recognized by multiple different tRNA species.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The 5′ base of the anticodon can form nontraditional pairs with the 3′ base of the codon.

    The wobble hypothesis states that the first (5') base of the tRNA anticodon can pair loosely/non-standardly with the third (3') base of the codon. This relaxed pairing explains how one tRNA can recognise more than one codon for the same amino acid. The first two codon bases still pair strictly.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Protein Synthesis
