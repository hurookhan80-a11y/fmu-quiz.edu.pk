Vitamins MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Vitamins

Vitamins are organic compounds needed in small amounts for normal growth and metabolism, which the body cannot make in sufficient quantity. Fat-soluble vitamins are stored in the body, while water-soluble vitamins are not stored and must be supplied regularly in the diet. Each vitamin has specific roles, and deficiency produces characteristic disorders.

  1. A man with loose teeth and bleeding gums has failure of prolyl hydroxylase. Vitamin C's role classifies it as:

    • A. A prosthetic group
    • B. A zymogen
    • C. A reducing agent keeping the cofactor iron as Fe2+
    • D. An allosteric activator increasing O2 affinity
    • E. A competitive inhibitor of lysyl oxidase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. A reducing agent keeping the cofactor iron as Fe2+

    Prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases need Fe2+ at their active site, and ascorbate (vitamin C) keeps that iron reduced as Fe2+. Without vitamin C, collagen is under-hydroxylated and weak, causing scurvy features such as bleeding gums and loose teeth. Vitamin C is acting as a reducing agent, not as a permanently bound prosthetic group.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  2. A patient on anti-tuberculous therapy for 5 months is most at risk of deficiency of which vitamin?

    • A. Thiamine, B1
    • B. Riboflavin, B2
    • C. Pyridoxine, B6
    • D. Biotin, B7
    • E. Folic acid, B9
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Pyridoxine, B6

    Isoniazid (anti-tuberculous therapy) interferes with pyridoxal phosphate formation and increases vitamin B6 loss, causing pyridoxine (B6) deficiency. This classically produces peripheral neuropathy (and sideroblastic anemia), preventable by giving pyridoxine with isoniazid.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  3. A pregnant woman prescribed isotretinoin delivers an infant with cleft palate and congenital heart defects. Teratogenicity is best explained by:

    • A. Competitive inhibition of retinal dehydrogenase
    • B. Excess retinyl phosphate disrupting glycoprotein synthesis
    • C. Excess retinoic acid binding nuclear transcription factors, altering gene expression
    • D. Oxidative stress from retinol ester accumulation
    • E. Inhibition of hepatic retinol-binding protein synthesis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Excess retinoic acid binding nuclear transcription factors, altering gene expression

    Retinoic acid binds nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RAR/RXR), which act as transcription factors controlling developmental (e.g., Hox) gene expression. Excess isotretinoin in pregnancy therefore disrupts embryonic patterning, causing craniofacial/cleft palate and congenital heart defects — it is a teratogen.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  4. All of the following are correct regarding vitamin D EXCEPT:

    • A. Vitamin D helps correct hypocalcaemia in renal failure
    • B. 1,25-dihydroxy D3 is the active form of vitamin D
    • C. Vitamin D opposes the effects of parathyroid hormone
    • D. Sunlight exposure is essential for vitamin D synthesis
    • E. Hydroxylation of vitamin D takes place in liver and kidney
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Vitamin D opposes the effects of parathyroid hormone

    Calcitriol [1,25-(OH)2 D3], the active vitamin D formed by hydroxylation in liver then kidney, works together with parathyroid hormone to raise plasma calcium by increasing gut absorption and bone resorption. The statement that vitamin D opposes PTH is therefore the incorrect one. Sunlight is needed for cutaneous vitamin D synthesis.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  5. Biotin acts as a co-enzyme in which reaction?

    • A. Carboxylation
    • B. Deamination
    • C. Hydroxylation
    • D. Methylation
    • E. Transamination
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Carboxylation

    Biotin is the coenzyme/prosthetic group of carboxylases such as pyruvate carboxylase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase, and propionyl-CoA carboxylase. It carries activated CO2 (carboxybiotin) for carboxylation reactions.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  6. Vitamin B12 deficiency typically produces which type of anaemia?

    • A. Microcytic anaemia
    • B. Haemolytic anaemia only
    • C. Sideroblastic anaemia
    • D. Macrocytic (megaloblastic) anaemia
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Macrocytic (megaloblastic) anaemia

    Vitamin B12 deficiency impairs DNA synthesis, producing large immature red cell precursors and a macrocytic/megaloblastic anaemia. Iron deficiency is the classic microcytic cause.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  7. Maturation failure (megaloblastic) anaemia can be caused by deficiency of:

    • A. Folic acid
    • B. Vitamin C
    • C. Copper only
    • D. Iron
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Folic acid

    Folate deficiency impairs DNA synthesis and red cell maturation, producing a megaloblastic anaemia. Vitamin B12 deficiency gives a similar picture, while iron deficiency gives microcytic anaemia.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Vitamins

  8. Vitamin K is required for the post-translational gamma-carboxylation of glutamate residues in clotting factors:

    • A. I, V, VIII and XIII
    • B. XI and XII only
    • C. II, VII, IX and X (and proteins C and S)
    • D. Fibrinogen and thrombin receptor only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. II, VII, IX and X (and proteins C and S)

    Gamma-carboxylation creates Gla residues that bind Ca2+ and phospholipid, activating factors II (prothrombin), VII, IX, X and anticoagulant proteins C and S — hence warfarin (vitamin K antagonist) and neonatal vitamin K prophylaxis. Factors V, VIII, XI, XII and fibrinogen are not vitamin K-dependent.

    Source: Lippincott Biochemistry — Fat-Soluble Vitamins (Vitamin K); Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Vitamins

  9. Both folate and vitamin B12 deficiency raise plasma homocysteine, but only vitamin B12 deficiency characteristically raises:

    • A. Methionine
    • B. Cystathionine
    • C. Methylmalonic acid
    • D. Formiminoglutamic acid (FIGLU)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Methylmalonic acid

    B12 is cofactor for both methionine synthase (homocysteine to methionine) and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase; its deficiency therefore accumulates homocysteine AND methylmalonic acid, whereas folate deficiency raises homocysteine (and FIGLU) with normal methylmalonic acid — a key laboratory distinction. This also explains why folate can correct the anaemia of B12 deficiency while neurology worsens.

    Source: Lippincott Biochemistry — Folate and Vitamin B12; Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Vitamins
