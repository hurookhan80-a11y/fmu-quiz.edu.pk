RNA Synthesis and Processing MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › RNA Synthesis and Processing

RNA synthesis, or transcription, copies one strand of a gene into a messenger RNA molecule. The new RNA is then processed: a cap is added at one end, a tail at the other, and non-coding segments are removed by splicing. The finished messenger RNA leaves the nucleus and carries the code for protein synthesis.

  1. A 30-year-old female presents with a butterfly rash and joint pain. Laboratory tests detect antibodies against small nuclear ribonucleoprotein particles (snRNPs). What is the primary function of these particles?

    • A. Adding the 3′ poly-A tail
    • B. Facilitating base modifications in tRNA
    • C. Removing introns and joining exons
    • D. Catalyzing peptide bond formation
    • E. Transporting amino acids to the ribosome
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Removing introns and joining exons

    Small nuclear ribonucleoproteins form the spliceosome that removes introns and joins exons in pre-mRNA splicing. Antibodies to snRNPs occur in systemic lupus erythematosus and mixed connective tissue disease.

  2. A fetus is identified with a mutation in the AAUAAA consensus sequence near the 3′ end of a specific gene. Which process in the maturation of the patient’s messenger RNA will be most directly affected?

    • A. Splicing of the first intron
    • B. Addition of the 5' cap
    • C. Cleavage and polyadenylation
    • D. Transcription initiation
    • E. RNA editing
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Cleavage and polyadenylation

    The AAUAAA sequence is the polyadenylation signal near the 3' end of pre-mRNA. It directs endonucleolytic cleavage downstream and addition of the poly-A tail by poly-A polymerase. The 5' cap and splicing use different signals and machinery.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  3. A mutation that alters the invariant GU or AG sequences at the ends of an intron is most likely to interfere with which process?

    • A. Transcription initiation
    • B. 5′ Capping
    • C. Splicing
    • D. Polyadenylation
    • E. RNA editing
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Splicing

    Almost all introns begin with GU (5' donor site) and end with AG (3' acceptor site); these invariant sequences are essential for recognition by the spliceosome. Mutating them interferes with splicing, not with capping, polyadenylation, or transcription initiation.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  4. A polycistronic messenger RNA is a characteristic feature of which of the following biological systems?

    • A. Eukaryotic nuclear genome
    • B. Prokaryotic organisms
    • C. Mature human cytosolic mRNA
    • D. Histone gene transcripts
    • E. Spliced hnRNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Prokaryotic organisms

    A polycistronic mRNA carries coding information for several proteins from more than one gene and is characteristic of prokaryotes (operons). Eukaryotic nuclear mRNAs are normally monocistronic, encoding one protein.

  5. A researcher examines the lipid profile of a patient and notes the production of apolipoprotein B-48 in the intestine. This shorter protein isoform is the result of which posttranscriptional process?

    • A. Alternative polyadenylation
    • B. Exon skipping
    • C. Deamination of cytosine to uracil (RNA editing)
    • D. Addition of a 5' cap
    • E. RNA interference
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Deamination of cytosine to uracil (RNA editing)

    In the intestine, apolipoprotein B mRNA undergoes RNA editing: cytosine deamination (C to U) creates a stop codon. Translation therefore stops early, producing the shorter apo B-48 used in chylomicrons instead of full-length apo B-100.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  6. A researcher is studying a patient's messenger RNA stability. They observe a structural modification at the 5′ end consisting of 7-methylguanosine. Which unusual linkage connects this molecule to the transcript?

    • A. 3′-to-5′ phosphodiester bond
    • B. 5′-to-3′ phosphodiester bond
    • C. 5′-to-5′ triphosphate linkage
    • D. 3′-to-3′ methylguanosine bridge
    • E. 2′-to-5′ glycosidic bond
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 5′-to-5′ triphosphate linkage

    Eukaryotic mRNA has a 5' cap of 7-methylguanosine joined to the first nucleotide by an unusual 5'-to-5' triphosphate linkage. The cap protects mRNA from degradation and helps ribosome binding for translation.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  7. A scientist investigating a patient's nucleolar function identifies small nucleolar RNA (snoRNA). What is the primary role of these molecules in the maturation of ribosomal RNA?

    • A. Removing the 5' cap
    • B. Facilitating base and sugar modifications
    • C. Transporting rRNA to the cytoplasm
    • D. Degrading misfolded rRNA
    • E. Serving as a template for translation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Facilitating base and sugar modifications

    Small nucleolar RNAs (snoRNAs), located in the nucleolus, guide chemical modifications of nascent rRNA — mainly 2'-O-methylation of ribose and pseudouridylation of bases. These modifications are needed for rRNA processing and ribosome maturation.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  8. During the synthesis of the 3′ poly-A tail of eukaryotic mRNA, which enzyme is responsible for adding the adenine nucleotides, and what is the template used?

    • A. RNA polymerase II using a DNA template
    • B. Polyadenylate polymerase using a DNA template
    • C. Polyadenylate polymerase using no template
    • D. RNA polymerase III using an RNA template
    • E. Primase using a poly-T template
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Polyadenylate polymerase using no template

    The poly-A tail is added after cleavage of the pre-mRNA by polyadenylate (poly-A) polymerase, which adds AMP units from ATP one at a time without any template. RNA polymerases, by contrast, synthesize RNA using a DNA template.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  9. Eukaryotic cells exhibit a high degree of compartmentalization. Which of the following processes is spatially separated in eukaryotes but occurs in the same compartment in prokaryotes?

    • A. DNA replication and coiling
    • B. Aerobic and anaerobic respiration
    • C. RNA synthesis and protein synthesis
    • D. Lipid synthesis and transport
    • E. Fission and mitosis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. RNA synthesis and protein synthesis

    In eukaryotes RNA synthesis (transcription) occurs in the nucleus and protein synthesis in the cytoplasm, so they are spatially separated. In prokaryotes, lacking a nucleus, transcription and translation occur in the same compartment and can be coupled.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  10. In a patient with dysregulated cell proliferation, a specific micro-RNA (miRNA) is found to repress the translation of a target messenger RNA. Where does the guide strand of this miRNA typically hybridize?

    • A. 5′ 7-methylguanosine cap
    • B. 5′-untranslated region (5′-UTR)
    • C. Poly-A tail
    • D. 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR)
    • E. First exon of the coding region
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR)

    MicroRNAs loaded into the RISC complex usually bind by partial complementarity to the 3'-untranslated region (3'-UTR) of target mRNAs. This represses translation and/or promotes mRNA degradation, reducing protein output.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing

  11. Which of the following structures is specifically present in eukaryotic cells and serves as the major site where ribosomal RNA is synthesized and ribosomal sub-units are assembled?

    • A. Nucleoid
    • B. Nucleolus
    • C. Chromatin
    • D. Nuclear pore
    • E. Porin
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Nucleolus

    The nucleolus, a non-membrane-bound region inside the eukaryotic nucleus, is where ribosomal RNA is transcribed and processed and ribosomal subunits begin assembly. Prokaryotes have a nucleoid instead, and nuclear pores only transport material across the nuclear envelope.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — RNA Synthesis and Processing
