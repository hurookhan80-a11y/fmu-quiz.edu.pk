DNA Structure and Replication MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › DNA Structure and Replication

DNA is a double-stranded molecule that stores the genetic instructions of the cell. Each strand is a chain of nucleotides, and the two strands are held together by base pairing: adenine with thymine, and guanine with cytosine. During replication, the strands separate and each serves as a template for a new strand, producing two identical copies.

  1. A DNA molecule in which both strands have radioactive thymidine is permitted to replicate in environment that contains non-radioactive thymidine. What is the right number of DNA molecules which possess some radioactive thymidine post three duplications?

    • A. Four such molecules
    • B. Eight such molecules
    • C. One such molecule
    • D. Ten such molecules
    • E. Two such molecules
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. Two such molecules

    DNA replication is semiconservative, so the two original radioactive strands are conserved and each ends up in a different daughter molecule. After three rounds there are eight DNA molecules in total, but only the two molecules containing an original strand contain radioactive thymidine.

  2. A DNA solution is heated until it denatures. When the solution is cooled, the complementary strands reform a double helix. This is called:

    • A. Transcription
    • B. Translation
    • C. Renaturation
    • D. Transformation
    • E. Transduction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Renaturation

    Heating separates DNA strands by breaking hydrogen bonds, which is denaturation. Slow cooling lets complementary strands find each other and reform the double helix, called renaturation (or reannealing). Transcription and translation are information-transfer processes, not strand re-pairing.
