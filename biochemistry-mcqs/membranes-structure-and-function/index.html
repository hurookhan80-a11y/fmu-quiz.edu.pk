Biological membranes are thin barriers that separate the inside of a cell, and its compartments, from the surroundings. They are built mainly from a double layer of phospholipids with proteins embedded in it. Membranes control what passes in and out, allow cells to communicate, and provide surfaces for chemical reactions.

  1. A patient with a chronic inflammatory condition has a significantly thinned glycocalyx on their vascular endothelial cells. Which component of the cell membrane is primarily responsible for forming this protective hydrophilic layer?

    • A. Cholesterol
    • B. Integral proteins
    • C. Phospholipids
    • D. Oligosaccharide chains
    • E. Cardiolipin
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Oligosaccharide chains

    The glycocalyx is the hydrophilic carbohydrate-rich layer on the outer surface of the plasma membrane, formed mainly by oligosaccharide chains of glycolipids and glycoproteins. It protects the cell and participates in cell recognition. Cholesterol and phospholipids form the bilayer itself, not the surface sugar coat.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  2. A patient with a metabolic disorder has a high ratio of saturated to unsaturated fatty acids in their cell membranes, leading to decreased fluidity. Which structural feature of cis-unsaturated fatty acids normally prevents tight packing?

    • A. Long straight tails
    • B. Kinked tails providing more spacing
    • C. Trans double bonds
    • D. Hydrophilic ring systems
    • E. Amide linkages to sphingosine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Kinked tails providing more spacing

    Cis double bonds put a kink in unsaturated fatty-acid tails, preventing tight packing of membrane lipids. This increases spacing and membrane fluidity. A membrane rich in saturated fatty acids packs tightly and becomes less fluid.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  3. Animal membrane lipids predominantly contain unsaturated fatty acids in the cis configuration. What is the biochemical significance of this specific geometric isomerism for membrane function?

    • A. It allows for tighter packing and higher gel-state stability
    • B. It removes the necessity for a polar head group
    • C. It creates kinked tails that increase spacing and room for movement
    • D. It prevents the lateral diffusion of integral proteins
    • E. It ensures the membrane is virtually impermeable to water
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. It creates kinked tails that increase spacing and room for movement

    Cis-unsaturated fatty acids have kinked tails that prevent tight packing of membrane phospholipids. This increases spacing between molecules and maintains membrane fluidity, whereas saturated straight chains pack tightly and stiffen the membrane.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  4. Biological membranes are described as a "fluid mosaic" where proteins float in a lipid sea. Which factor is primarily responsible for the "fluid" quality of these membranes at a physiological temperature?

    • A. Rigid association with the cytoskeleton
    • B. Strong covalent bonds between phospholipids
    • C. Weak Van der Waals forces and hydrophobic effects between lipids
    • D. Presence of fixed carbohydrate chains on the inner leaflet
    • E. Irreversible "flip-flop" of integral proteins
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Weak Van der Waals forces and hydrophobic effects between lipids

    Membrane lipids are held together only by weak non-covalent forces — hydrophobic interactions and van der Waals forces — so lipids and many proteins can move laterally. This mobility gives the membrane its fluid character in the fluid mosaic model.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  5. Biological membranes exhibit a significant transverse asymmetry regarding their phospholipid distribution. Which phospholipid is preferentially located in the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane?

    • A. Phosphatidylcholine
    • B. Sphingomyelin
    • C. Phosphatidylserine
    • D. Galactosyl-ceramide
    • E. Ganglioside GM1
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Phosphatidylserine

    Plasma membrane phospholipids are asymmetrically distributed: phosphatidylserine (and phosphatidylethanolamine) are kept mainly in the inner (cytoplasmic) leaflet, while phosphatidylcholine and sphingomyelin predominate in the outer leaflet. Externalization of phosphatidylserine is a signal in apoptosis and clotting.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  6. Cardiolipin is present in:

    • A. Inner mitochondrial mem
    • B. Outer Mitochondrial mem
    • C. Outer cell mem
    • D. Inner cell mem
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Inner mitochondrial mem

    Cardiolipin (diphosphatidylglycerol) is the characteristic phospholipid of the inner mitochondrial membrane, where it supports oxidative phosphorylation complexes. It is not a major phospholipid of the plasma membrane.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  7. Glycolipids on cell membranes has carbohydrate:

    • A. Monosaccharide
    • B. Oligosaccharide
    • C. Non reducing sugars
    • D. Reducing sugars
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Oligosaccharide

    Membrane glycolipids carry short oligosaccharide chains that project from the cell surface as part of the glycocalyx. These sugar chains act as cell-recognition and antigenic determinants (e.g., blood group antigens).

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  8. In a patient with a rare cytoskeleton defect, certain membrane proteins that are usually fixed begin to diffuse laterally within the lipid sea. This lateral mobility is a core tenet of which membrane model?

    • A. Rigid lattice
    • B. Solid crystal
    • C. Fluid mosaic
    • D. Static bilayer
    • E. Protein scaffold
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Fluid mosaic

    The fluid mosaic model describes the membrane as phospholipids forming a fluid bilayer in which proteins float and can diffuse laterally. Lateral mobility of lipids and many proteins is a central feature; a rigid lattice or static bilayer would not allow this movement.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  9. In the context of cell surface recognition, which component acts as the "antigenic determinant" in a glycolipid?

    • A. The fatty acid tail
    • B. The glycerol backbone
    • C. The carbohydrate portion
    • D. The phosphate group
    • E. The sphingosine base
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. The carbohydrate portion

    In glycolipids (especially glycosphingolipids) the carbohydrate portion projects from the membrane surface and serves as the antigenic determinant and cell-recognition marker, as in ABO blood group antigens. The lipid portion only anchors the molecule in the membrane.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function

  10. The cell-surface receptors and antigens on the outer membrane are mainly composed of:

    • A. Integral channel proteins only
    • B. Cholesterol
    • C. Phosphatidylserine
    • D. Glycoproteins and glycolipids
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Glycoproteins and glycolipids

    Carbohydrate chains of glycoproteins and glycolipids project outward forming the glycocalyx, which carries ABO antigens and acts in cell recognition. Cholesterol mainly stabilizes the bilayer rather than acting as a surface receptor.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Membranes: Structure and Function
