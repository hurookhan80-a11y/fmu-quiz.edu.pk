DNA Organization and Replication MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › DNA Organization and Replication

DNA is organised in the cell by winding around histone proteins to form chromatin, which folds further into chromosomes during cell division. When a cell prepares to divide, its DNA is replicated: the two strands separate and each directs the synthesis of a new partner strand. The result is two identical DNA molecules, one for each daughter cell.

  1. In the structural hierarchy of a mitotic chromosome, the 30-nm fibers are organized into large loops. These loops are physically anchored to which specific cellular component?

    • A. The nucleolus
    • B. A nuclear scaffold protein
    • C. The nuclear pore complex
    • D. The mitochondrial matrix
    • E. Histone H1 octamers
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. A nuclear scaffold protein

    After the 10-nm beads-on-a-string form the 30-nm fibre, the fibre is folded into large looped domains. These loops are anchored at their bases to a protein framework called the nuclear scaffold (matrix), which organises chromosomes and helps regulate genes.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  2. The "beads on a string" appearance of chromatin is created by nucleosomes joined by short segments of DNA. What is the name and approximate length of these segments?

    • A. Core DNA; 147 bp
    • B. Linker DNA; 50 bp
    • C. Satellite DNA; 1000 bp
    • D. Palindromic DNA; 4 bp
    • E. Telomeric DNA; 250 bp
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Linker DNA; 50 bp

    In the beads-on-a-string (10-nm) fibre, each nucleosome core (~147 bp wrapped around histones) is joined to the next by linker DNA of roughly 20-60 bp (about 50 bp). Histone H1 binds this linker DNA and helps fold the fibre into the 30-nm form.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  3. The complex of DNA and protein found inside the nuclei of eukaryotic cells, which serves to condense and organize the long DNA molecules, is collectively termed:

    • A. The genome
    • B. The transcriptome
    • C. Chromatin
    • D. The plasmid
    • E. The proteome
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Chromatin

    Inside the eukaryotic nucleus, DNA wound around histone proteins forms chromatin, which condenses further into chromosomes at division. The genome is the total DNA content; chromatin is the actual DNA-protein complex that packages it.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  4. The higher-order organization of DNA involves the formation of a 30-nm fiber. This specific structural arrangement, characterized by the tight stacking of nucleosomes, is also known as a:

    • A. Double helix
    • B. Nucleofilament
    • C. Transcription bubble
    • D. Nuclear scaffold
    • E. Sister chromatid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Nucleofilament

    Stacking/coiling of nucleosomes (with histone H1) compacts the 10-nm beads-on-a-string into the thicker 30-nm fibre, also called the nucleofilament (solenoid). The nuclear scaffold is a higher level of organisation that anchors loops of this fibre.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  5. The process by which the double helix of a chromosome crosses over on itself to relieve torsional strain is known as:

    • A. Denaturation
    • B. Supercoiling
    • C. Splicing
    • D. Translation
    • E. Transcription
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Supercoiling

    Supercoiling is further twisting of the double helix on itself (like over-twisting a rope) to relieve torsional strain and compact DNA. Topoisomerases control it; denaturation instead separates the two strands.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  6. What is the fundamental unit of eukaryotic chromatin structure?

    • A. The gene
    • B. The chromosome
    • C. The nucleosome
    • D. The plasmid
    • E. The histone octamer
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. The nucleosome

    The nucleosome — about 147 bp of DNA wound around a histone octamer (H2A, H2B, H3, H4 x2) — is the fundamental repeating unit of eukaryotic chromatin. Nucleosomes fold further into the 30-nm fibre and chromosomes.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  7. When DNA is wound nearly twice around the histone octamer to form a nucleosome, it results in a specific change in the helical turns. This process directly causes:

    • A. Positive supercoiling
    • B. Negative supercoiling
    • C. Denaturation of the A-T rich regions
    • D. Formation of a triple helix
    • E. Methylation of cytosine bases
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Negative supercoiling

    Wrapping DNA nearly twice (left-handed) around the histone octamer removes about one helical turn from the DNA, introducing negative supercoiling into the rest of the molecule. This underwinding also helps strand separation during replication and transcription.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  8. Which class of histone proteins is described as being the most tissue-specific and species-specific, playing a vital role in facilitating the packing of nucleosomes into compact structures?

    • A. H2A
    • B. H2B
    • C. H1
    • D. H3
    • E. H4
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. H1

    Histone H1 is the linker histone that binds the DNA between nucleosomes and is the most variable, tissue-specific and species-specific histone. By sealing the linker DNA it enables packing of nucleosomes into the compact 30-nm fibre.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication

  9. Which level of DNA organization corresponds to the "30-nm fiber" formed by the stacking of nucleosomes?

    • A. Primary structure
    • B. Nucleofilament
    • C. Double helix
    • D. Nucleoid
    • E. Nuclear scaffold
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Nucleofilament

    The 30-nm fibre formed by coiling/stacking of nucleosomes (with H1) is termed the nucleofilament (solenoid), the level above the nucleosome itself. The double helix is only the secondary structure, far below this packaging level.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization and Replication
