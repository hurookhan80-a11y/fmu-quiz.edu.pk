DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

DNA in the cell is organised into chromatin and, during division, into chromosomes, so that very long molecules fit inside the nucleus. Before a cell divides, its DNA is copied by replication, giving each daughter cell a complete set. Repair systems constantly scan DNA for damage and correct errors, protecting the genetic information.

  1. A mutation in the LMNA gene at position 1824 results in the production of progerin. This mutant protein remains anchored to which specific cellular component?

    • A. Outer mitochondrial membrane
    • B. Inner nuclear membrane
    • C. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
    • D. Golgi medial cisternae
    • E. Plasma membrane lipid rafts
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Inner nuclear membrane

    Progerin is a truncated, permanently farnesylated prelamin A produced in Hutchinson-Gilford progeria. Because the farnesyl anchor is not removed, progerin stays attached to the inner nuclear membrane and makes the nuclear lamina unstable.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  2. A researcher discovers a microscopic organism that lacks a defined nucleus and instead contains only a nuclear zone with DNA. This organism should be classified as which of the following?

    • A. Protist
    • B. Fungus
    • C. Archaeo-bacteria
    • D. Plant cell
    • E. Animal cell
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Archaeo-bacteria

    Cells without a membrane-bound, well-defined nucleus, having DNA only in a nuclear zone (nucleoid), are prokaryotic cells — including archaeo-bacteria (archaea) and eubacteria. Protists, fungi, plant and animal cells are eukaryotes with a true nucleus.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  3. A researcher finds that a patient’s gene is silenced due to high histone deacetylase activity. Removing the acetyl group from the N-terminal lysine residues causes which of the following?

    • A. Immediate degradation of the DNA strand
    • B. Restoration of the positive charge on the histone
    • C. Elimination of the negative charge on the DNA
    • D. Conversion of the B-DNA to Z-DNA
    • E. Permanent breakage of phosphodiester bonds
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Restoration of the positive charge on the histone

    Histone lysines are positively charged and bind negatively charged DNA. Deacetylation by histone deacetylases (HDACs) removes the acetyl group and restores the positive charge on lysine, tightening histone-DNA binding and repressing transcription. Acetylation does the opposite and opens chromatin.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  4. A student uses an electron microscope to examine a patient's biopsy and identifies stacked nucleosomes forming a coil-shaped structure. This level of DNA organization is called the:

    • A. Primary structure
    • B. Double helix
    • C. 30-nm fiber
    • D. Nuclear scaffold
    • E. Triple helix
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 30-nm fiber

    Nucleosomes ('beads on a string', ~10-nm) coil and pack into the thicker 30-nm fiber/chromatin fiber. Further looping and condensation produce metaphase chromosomes.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  5. All animal tissues, including those in humans, are organized from collections of cells, which represent the fundamental unit of life. Which of the following best describes the hierarchical level immediately following the coordination of different specialized tissues

    • A. Organelle
    • B. Tissue
    • C. Organ
    • D. Organ System
    • E. Cytoplasm
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Organ

    Different specialized tissues combine to form an organ, which performs a coordinated function (e.g., muscle, nerve, and connective tissue in the heart). Cells form tissues, organs form organ systems. Organelles are structures inside a cell, below the tissue level.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  6. Children with progeria typically die in their teens. What is the most common clinical cause of death associated with this accelerated aging syndrome?

    • A. Respiratory failure due to muscular rigidity
    • B. Blindness and sensorineural hearing loss
    • C. Severe psychomotor impairment
    • D. Atherosclerosis
    • E. Liver failure due to lipid imbalance
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Atherosclerosis

    Children with Hutchinson-Gilford progeria develop premature, accelerated atherosclerosis and usually die in their teens from myocardial infarction or stroke. Respiratory failure, blindness, and liver failure are not the typical cause of death.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  7. During the process of chromatin remodeling, histone acetyltransferases (HATs) modify lysine residues. What is the primary biochemical consequence of this acetylation on the histone-DNA interaction?

    • A. It increases the positive charge, tightening the bond.
    • B. It replaces the phosphate groups on the DNA.
    • C. It eliminates the positive charge, weakening the bond.
    • D. It triggers the immediate degradation of the DNA.
    • E. It converts B-DNA into Z-DNA.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. It eliminates the positive charge, weakening the bond.

    Histone acetyltransferases add acetyl groups to lysine residues, neutralizing their positive charge. This weakens histone binding to negatively charged DNA, loosens chromatin, and favors transcription. Deacetylation restores the positive charge and compacts chromatin.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  8. Epigenetics involves heritable changes in gene expression without altering the nucleotide sequence. Which of the following is an example of an epigenetic modification mentioned in higher DNA organization?

    • A. Phosphodiester bond cleavage
    • B. Semiconservative replication
    • C. Reversible acetylation of histone N-terminal ends
    • D. Formation of Okazaki fragments
    • E. Hydrogen bonding between A and T
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Reversible acetylation of histone N-terminal ends

    Reversible acetylation of histone N-terminal tails changes gene expression without changing the DNA nucleotide sequence, which is the definition of an epigenetic modification. Replication, Okazaki fragments, and A-T hydrogen bonding are normal DNA processes, not epigenetic marks.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  9. Eukaryotic DNA is distinct from prokaryotic DNA because it is coiled and associated with specific proteins that are characteristically absent in prokaryotes. These proteins are:

    • A. Porins
    • B. ATPase
    • C. RNA polymerase
    • D. Histones
    • E. Cardiolipin
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Histones

    Eukaryotic DNA is coiled around histone proteins to form nucleosomes and chromatin. Prokaryotic DNA is organized in a nucleoid without true membrane-bound nucleus and characteristically lacks histone-based nucleosomes.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  10. Eukaryotic DNA is packaged with such high efficiency that the final mitotic chromosome represents a linear compaction of approximately how many times compared to naked DNA?

    • A. 10-fold
    • B. 100-fold
    • C. 1,000-fold
    • D. 10,000-fold
    • E. 100,000-fold
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. 10,000-fold

    Packaging from naked DNA through nucleosomes, the 30-nm fiber, loops, and chromatids compacts DNA about 10,000-fold into a mitotic chromosome. This allows roughly 2 meters of human DNA to fit into a microscopic nucleus/chromosome.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  11. Histones are able to form strong ionic bonds with DNA because they contain a high concentration of which amino acids?

    • A. Glutamate and Aspartate
    • B. Valine and Leucine
    • C. Lysine and Arginine
    • D. Serine and Threonine
    • E. Proline and Glycine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Lysine and Arginine

    Histones are rich in the basic amino acids lysine and arginine, giving them a strong positive charge at physiological pH. This allows ionic binding to the negatively charged phosphate backbone of DNA in nucleosomes.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  12. In a prenatal screening, a clinician identifies a replicated chromosome consisting of two identical DNA molecules associated with histones and joined at a centromere. Each identical copy is a:

    • A. Nucleosome
    • B. Histone H1
    • C. Sister chromatid
    • D. Plasmid
    • E. 30-nm fiber
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Sister chromatid

    After replication, two identical DNA molecules (each with histones) remain joined at the centromere as sister chromatids. Each individual copy is a sister chromatid; a nucleosome is DNA wrapped around a histone octamer, a much smaller unit.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair

  13. In the hierarchical organization of multicellular organisms, how are the various membrane complexes and organelles involved in protein synthesis and secretion categorized?

    • A. They represent the "System" level
    • B. They are components of the "Cell" level
    • C. They are equivalent to the "Tissue" level
    • D. They represent the "Organ" level
    • E. They are considered independent organisms
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. They are components of the "Cell" level

    Membrane complexes and organelles such as the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi that synthesize and secrete proteins are parts of a single cell — the cell level of organization. Tissues, organs, and systems are higher levels made of many cells.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — DNA Organization, Replication, and Repair
