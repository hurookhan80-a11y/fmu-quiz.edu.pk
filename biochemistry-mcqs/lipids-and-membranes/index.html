Lipids and Membranes MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Lipids and Membranes

Lipids are a varied group of molecules that do not dissolve in water, including fats, phospholipids, and steroids. Phospholipids form the double-layered membranes that enclose cells and their compartments, while stored fats provide energy and insulation. Cholesterol, a lipid, is built into membranes and is also the starting material for steroid hormones.

  1. Laboratory analysis of a patient's cell membranes shows an inability to regulate fluidity during temperature changes. Which amphipathic molecule is most likely deficient if the fluid membranes lack stabilization?

    • A. Cephalin
    • B. Lecithin
    • C. Sphingomyelin
    • D. Cholesterol
    • E. Phosphatidic acid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Cholesterol

    Cholesterol buffers membrane fluidity: it restrains movement of fatty-acid chains at high temperature and prevents tight packing at low temperature. A deficiency therefore impairs the membrane's ability to keep a stable, workable fluidity when temperature changes.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  2. Lipid rafts are specialized microdomains that act as floating platforms within the cell membrane. Which combination of components is most characteristic of these thicker, more tightly packed regions?

    • A. Phosphatidic acid and short-chain saturated fatty acids
    • B. Sphingolipids, cholesterol, and GPI-linked proteins
    • C. Phosphatidylserine and peripheral proteins only
    • D. High concentration of cis-unsaturated lipids and no sterols
    • E. Glycophorin and large amounts of phosphatidylcholine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Sphingolipids, cholesterol, and GPI-linked proteins

    Lipid rafts are ordered, thicker membrane patches enriched in sphingolipids, cholesterol and GPI-anchored proteins with saturated tails that pack tightly. They act as platforms for signalling proteins, unlike the more fluid phosphatidylcholine-rich bulk membrane.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  3. Membrane proteins are classified based on their association with the lipid bilayer. What is a defining characteristic of peripheral membrane proteins compared to integral proteins?

    • A. They are deeply embedded in the hydrophobic central core
    • B. They can be removed from the membrane without disrupting its structure
    • C. They always span the entire breadth of the lipid bilayer
    • D. They are held in place exclusively by covalent bonds to lipid tails
    • E. They lack any hydrophilic regions for aqueous interaction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. They can be removed from the membrane without disrupting its structure

    Peripheral (extrinsic) membrane proteins sit on the membrane surface held by weak non-covalent interactions and can be washed off without breaking the bilayer. Integral proteins are embedded in the hydrophobic core and need detergents to be removed.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  4. Some integral proteins span the entire breadth of the lipid bilayer and are categorized as transmembrane proteins. What structural feature allows these proteins to remain stable within the central core of the membrane?

    • A. Highly hydrophilic side chains exposed to the lipid tails
    • B. Covalent bonding to the oligosaccharide chains of the outer leaflet
    • C. Hydrophobic side chains of amino acids embedded in the core
    • D. Exclusive reliance on peripheral protein anchors
    • E. Lack of any interaction with Van der Waals forces
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Hydrophobic side chains of amino acids embedded in the core

    Transmembrane proteins stay stable in the bilayer because their membrane-spanning segments present hydrophobic amino-acid side chains outward to the lipid tails. Their hydrophilic ends face the water on each side; exposing hydrophilic chains to the core would be energetically impossible.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  5. Specific proteins are attached to the cell membrane via which type of anchor that allows for high lateral mobility?

    • A. Disulfide bridge
    • B. Glycosyl phosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor
    • C. Cholesterol ester
    • D. Peptide bond to lipids
    • E. Transmembrane alpha-helix
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Glycosyl phosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor

    GPI-anchored proteins are attached by a glycosyl phosphatidylinositol lipid anchor in the outer leaflet, not by crossing the membrane. Because the anchor sits in one leaflet, these proteins show high lateral mobility and can be released by phospholipase C.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  6. The "mosaic" aspect of the Fluid Mosaic Model refers to the heterogeneous distribution of components. Which statement accurately describes the asymmetric distribution of sugar moieties in the plasma membrane?

    • A. They are equally distributed between the inner and outer faces
    • B. They are exclusively found protruding inward into the cytoplasm
    • C. They are absent from the outer face but rich on the inner face
    • D. They protrude outward from the plasma membrane and are absent from the inner face
    • E. They are only present in the mitochondria and absent from the cell membrane
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. They protrude outward from the plasma membrane and are absent from the inner face

    Membrane asymmetry places all carbohydrate (sugar) groups of glycoproteins and glycolipids on the outer (extracellular) face, forming the glycocalyx, with none facing the cytosol. This orientation is set during synthesis in the ER/Golgi and is never flipped.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  7. The fluidity of the animal cell membrane is critically regulated by the presence of cholesterol. How does cholesterol uniquely influence a membrane containing a high proportion of unsaturated lipids?

    • A. It promotes the transition from a fluid state to a gel state
    • B. It stabilizes the fluid membrane structure
    • C. It increases the rate of protein "flip-flop"
    • D. It reduces the number of Van der Waals interactions
    • E. It facilitates the removal of polar head groups
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. It stabilizes the fluid membrane structure

    In a membrane rich in unsaturated (fluid) lipids, cholesterol's rigid rings restrain fatty-acid chain movement and stabilise the fluid structure, preventing it from becoming too leaky. At low temperature it instead prevents gel packing — cholesterol is a fluidity buffer in both directions.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  8. The fluidity of the cell membrane is not merely a structural feature but significantly influences its biological activities. As membrane fluidity increases, what effect is observed on its permeability?

    • A. Permeability to water and small hydrophilic molecules increases
    • B. Permeability to large polar ions decreases proportionally
    • C. Integral proteins become fixed and lose lateral mobility
    • D. The rate of lipid flip-flop decreases to near zero
    • E. The hydrophobic effect becomes the sole regulator of transport
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Permeability to water and small hydrophilic molecules increases

    A more fluid bilayer has looser lipid packing, so water and small hydrophilic molecules cross it more easily — permeability rises with fluidity. Integral proteins also move more freely, not less, in a fluid membrane.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes

  9. The major interaction responsible for stabilizing the plasma membrane is:

    • A. Hydrophobic interactions
    • B. Hydrophilic interactions
    • C. Covalent bonds
    • D. Ionic bonds
    • E. Hydrogen bonds
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Hydrophobic interactions

    The bilayer holds together mainly by hydrophobic interactions: fatty-acid tails hide from water by packing inside, while polar heads face the aqueous phases. No covalent bonds link the lipids, which is why membranes are fluid and self-sealing.

    Source: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Lipids and Membranes
