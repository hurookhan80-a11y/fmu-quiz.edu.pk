Enzymes MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Biochemistry

Home › Biochemistry MCQs › Enzymes

Enzymes are proteins that speed up chemical reactions in the body without being used up themselves. Each enzyme binds specific substrates at its active site and lowers the energy needed for the reaction to proceed. Enzyme activity can be adjusted by temperature, acidity, substrate concentration, and by activators or inhibitors.

  1. A competitive inhibitor has what effect on the kinetic parameters of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction?

    • A. Increases Vmax and increases Km
    • B. Decreases Vmax and decreases Km
    • C. Decreases Vmax and leaves Km unchanged
    • D. Leaves Vmax unchanged and increases the apparent Km
    • E. Leaves both Vmax and Km unchanged
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Leaves Vmax unchanged and increases the apparent Km

    A competitive inhibitor binds reversibly to the active site, so substrate can outcompete it if enough substrate is added. It therefore raises the apparent Km (more substrate is needed for half-maximal velocity) but does not change Vmax. Noncompetitive inhibition mainly lowers Vmax.

  2. A particular enzyme requires pyridoxal phosphate (derived from Vitamin B6) for its activity. In this context, pyridoxal phosphate is a:

    • A. Metal cofactor
    • B. Coenzyme
    • C. Zymogen
    • D. Feedback inhibitor
    • E. Primary substrate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Coenzyme

    Pyridoxal phosphate, derived from vitamin B6, is a small organic non-protein molecule required by enzymes such as transaminases and decarboxylases. Such vitamin-derived organic cofactors are called coenzymes (when tightly bound they may be termed prosthetic groups, but the general classification asked here is coenzyme).

  3. A prosthetic group is distinguished from a coenzyme/cosubstrate by its:

    • A. Metallic nature
    • B. Permanent and tight association with the enzyme
    • C. Ability to be excreted in the urine
    • D. Requirement for ATP hydrolysis
    • E. Location in the nucleus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Permanent and tight association with the enzyme

    A prosthetic group is a coenzyme that is tightly and permanently associated with its enzyme and remains bound throughout catalysis (e.g., FAD in succinate dehydrogenase). Coenzymes/cosubstrates that bind transiently and leave after the reaction are distinguished by this permanent association.

  4. A researcher is studying a multi-subunit enzyme that requires a nonprotein organic molecule to function. If this molecule is permanently associated with the enzyme and returned to its original form after the reaction, it is classified as a:

    • A. Co-substrate
    • B. Metal cofactor
    • C. Prosthetic group
    • D. Apoenzyme
    • E. Zymogen
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Prosthetic group

    A non-protein organic molecule that is permanently (tightly) associated with the enzyme and regenerated in its original form during the reaction is a prosthetic group. A cosubstrate binds and leaves changed; the protein alone without its cofactor is the apoenzyme.

  5. A small numerical value for Km indicates that the enzyme has:

    • A. Low affinity for the substrate
    • B. High affinity for the substrate
    • C. Low catalytic efficiency
    • D. A high turnover number
    • E. A requirement for a prosthetic group
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. High affinity for the substrate

    Km is the substrate concentration at half Vmax. A small Km means half-maximal velocity is reached at low substrate concentration, indicating high affinity of the enzyme for substrate; a large Km indicates low affinity.

  6. A woman with MODY type 2 has a glucokinase mutation. Glucokinase's high Km allows it to:

    • A. Work at maximal velocity at very low glucose
    • B. Be inhibited by low glucose-6-phosphate
    • C. Act as a glucose sensor active when glucose is elevated
    • D. Phosphorylate fructose with high affinity
    • E. Localize exclusively to skeletal muscle
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Act as a glucose sensor active when glucose is elevated

    Glucokinase (hexokinase IV) has a high Km for glucose, so it works substantially only when glucose is high, acting as a glucose sensor in liver and pancreatic beta cells. Unlike other hexokinases it is not inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate, allowing continued glucose phosphorylation after meals.

  7. Allosteric enzymes typically show a curve of which shape when reaction velocity is plotted against substrate concentration?

    • A. Hyperbolic
    • B. Linear
    • C. Sigmoidal (S-shaped)
    • D. Parabolic
    • E. Exponential
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Sigmoidal (S-shaped)

    Allosteric enzymes show a sigmoidal (S-shaped) velocity versus substrate curve because binding of substrate at one subunit increases affinity at other subunits (cooperativity). Simple Michaelis-Menten enzymes give a hyperbolic curve.

  8. An enzyme that catalyzes the cleavage of C–C, C–S, or certain C–N bonds without the addition of water or the transfer of electrons is known as a:

    • A. Hydrolase
    • B. Lyase
    • C. Ligase
    • D. Isomerase
    • E. Transferase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Lyase

    Lyases cleave C-C, C-S, or certain C-N bonds without adding water and without oxidation-reduction, often forming a double bond or ring. Hydrolases use water, ligases join molecules using ATP, and isomerases rearrange atoms within a molecule.

  9. An enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of a phosphate group from ATP to a specific substrate is classified under which major category?

    • A. Oxidoreductases
    • B. Hydrolases
    • C. Transferases
    • D. Isomerases
    • E. Ligases

    Answer: C. Transferases

    Enzymes that transfer a functional group from one molecule to another are transferases. Kinases are transferases that move a phosphate group from ATP to a substrate.

  10. An enzyme that rearranges the structure of its substrate without changing its molecular formula is an:

    • A. Isomerase
    • B. Hydrolase
    • C. Ligase
    • D. Oxidoreductase
    • E. Transferase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Isomerase

    Isomerases catalyze intramolecular rearrangements that convert a molecule into its isomer without changing its molecular formula. Hydrolases break bonds with water and ligases join molecules using ATP.

  11. An enzyme's "Turnover Number" is also known as:

    • A. Km
    • B. Vmax
    • C. kcat
    • D. ΔG
    • E. pH optimum
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. kcat

    The turnover number is kcat: the number of substrate molecules converted to product per enzyme molecule per second at saturation. Km reflects substrate affinity, and Vmax is the maximal velocity for a given enzyme amount.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  12. An inactive enzyme protein that lacks its required nonprotein component is referred to as which of the following?

    • A. Holoenzyme
    • B. Apoenzyme
    • C. Zymogen
    • D. Coenzyme
    • E. Isomer
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Apoenzyme

    The protein part of an enzyme without its required non-protein component is the apoenzyme, which is inactive. Apoenzyme plus cofactor/coenzyme forms the active holoenzyme; a zymogen is an inactive precursor activated by proteolysis.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  13. An inhibitor that binds only to the enzyme-substrate (ES) complex and not to the free enzyme is classified as:

    • A. Competitive
    • B. Noncompetitive
    • C. Uncompetitive
    • D. Allosteric
    • E. Suicide inhibitor
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Uncompetitive

    An uncompetitive inhibitor binds only to the enzyme-substrate (ES) complex, not to free enzyme, forming a dead-end ESI complex. It lowers both Vmax and Km. Competitive inhibitors bind free enzyme at the active site; noncompetitive inhibitors bind both E and ES.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  14. Arsenic poisoning inhibits the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex via lipoic acid. Lipoic acid serves as:

    Lipoic acid (lipoamide) is covalently attached to the E2 subunit of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex and acts as a prosthetic group that swings between active sites carrying the acetyl group. Arsenite binds its sulfhydryl groups and inhibits the complex.

  15. Asparaginase is an example of an enzyme used therapeutically. Its primary clinical application is in the treatment of:

    • A. Blood clots

  16. At very high substrate concentrations ([S] >> Km), the reaction velocity becomes independent of [S]. This state is described as:

    • A. First order
    • B. Mixed order
    • C. Zero order
    • D. Infinite order
    • E. Transition state
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Zero order

    When [S] is much greater than Km, essentially all enzyme is saturated as ES, so velocity equals Vmax and no longer changes with [S]. The reaction is zero order with respect to substrate.

  17. Creatine Kinase (CK) exists as three isoenzymes. Which isoform is specifically found in high proportions in the myocardium?

    • A. CK1 (BB)
    • B. CK2 (MB)
    • C. CK3 (MM)
    • D. CK4 (NN)
    • E. CK5 (LL)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. CK2 (MB)

    Creatine kinase has three isoenzymes: CK-MM (skeletal muscle), CK-BB (brain), and CK-MB (CK2), which is enriched in myocardium. Raised CK-MB is used as a marker of myocardial infarction.

  18. Digestive enzymes like pepsin and trypsin are synthesized as larger, inactive precursors that must be proteolytically cleaved to become active. These precursors are called:

    • A. Apoenzymes
    • B. Holoenzymes
    • C. Zymogens
    • D. Coenzymes
    • E. Modifiers
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Zymogens

    Inactive enzyme precursors that are activated by proteolytic cleavage are called zymogens (proenzymes), e.g., pepsinogen to pepsin and trypsinogen to trypsin. This prevents digestive enzymes from damaging the cells that produce them. An apoenzyme lacks a cofactor, whereas a holoenzyme is the complete active enzyme.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  19. Enzymes belonging to the class "Ligases" are characterized by their ability to:

    • A. Cleave bonds by adding water
    • B. Move groups within the same molecule to form isomers
    • C. Join two molecules together coupled with the hydrolysis of ATP
    • D. Remove groups to form double bonds
    • E. Transfer electrons from a donor to an acceptor
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Join two molecules together coupled with the hydrolysis of ATP

    Ligases join two molecules together (forming C-C, C-O, C-N, or C-S bonds) coupled to hydrolysis of ATP, e.g., DNA ligase sealing nicks. Hydrolases cleave with water, and isomerases rearrange molecules internally.

  20. Ethylene glycol poisoning is treated with ethanol. What is ethanol's kinetic effect on alcohol dehydrogenase acting on ethylene glycol?

    • A. Decreases Vmax, Km unchanged
    • B. Increases Vmax and Km
    • C. Vmax unchanged, apparent Km increased
    • D. Decreases both Vmax and Km
    • E. Binds irreversibly to a regulatory subunit
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Vmax unchanged, apparent Km increased

    Ethanol is a competing substrate for alcohol dehydrogenase, acting as a competitive inhibitor of ethylene glycol metabolism: Vmax is unchanged but apparent Km for ethylene glycol increases. This slows production of toxic metabolites (glycolate, oxalate) while ethylene glycol is excreted.

  21. How can the effects of a competitive inhibitor be overcome in an experimental setting?

    • A. By decreasing the temperature
    • B. By adding more inhibitor
    • C. By increasing the substrate concentration
    • D. By adjusting the pH to the isoelectric point
    • E. By adding a noncompetitive activator
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. By increasing the substrate concentration

    A competitive inhibitor competes with substrate for the active site, so raising substrate concentration outcompetes the inhibitor and restores velocity toward the unchanged Vmax. Adding more inhibitor or changing temperature/pH does not specifically reverse competitive inhibition.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  22. How do enzymes increase the rate of a chemical reaction without altering the equilibrium constant?

    • A. By increasing the free energy of the reactants
    • B. By decreasing the free energy of the products
    • C. By lowering the activation energy of the reaction
    • D. By providing a pathway that bypasses the transition state
    • E. By increasing the total change in free energy (ΔG)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. By lowering the activation energy of the reaction

    Enzymes speed reactions by lowering the activation energy (energy of the transition state), providing an alternative lower-energy pathway. They do not change the free energies of reactants or products, so delta G and the equilibrium constant are unchanged.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  23. How does a noncompetitive inhibitor affect the X-intercept of a Lineweaver-Burk plot?

    • A. It shifts it to the right toward zero.
    • B. It shifts it to the left away from zero.
    • C. It has no effect on the X-intercept.
    • D. It makes the X-intercept positive.
    • E. It removes the X-intercept entirely.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. It has no effect on the X-intercept.

    In noncompetitive inhibition, Km is unchanged while Vmax falls. On a Lineweaver-Burk plot the x-intercept is -1/Km, so it does not change; only the y-intercept (1/Vmax) increases.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  24. If an enzyme follows Michaelis-Menten kinetics, what happens to the velocity (v) if the substrate concentration [S] is tripled while [S] is still much less than Km?

    • A. Velocity is unchanged.
    • B. Velocity is reduced by one-third.
    • C. Velocity approximately triples.
    • D. Velocity reaches Vmax.
    • E. Velocity becomes zero.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Velocity approximately triples.

    When [S] is much less than Km, the enzyme is mostly free and velocity is approximately (Vmax/Km)[S] — first-order and directly proportional to [S]. Tripling [S] in this region therefore approximately triples velocity; Vmax is approached only when [S] >> Km.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  25. If an enzyme is inhibited by its own reaction product, this phenomenon is called:

    • A. Cooperative binding
    • B. Product inhibition
    • C. Zymogen activation
    • D. Positive feedback
    • E. Denaturation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Product inhibition

    When the product of a reaction inhibits the enzyme that produced it, this is product inhibition — a form of feedback regulation preventing over-accumulation of product. It is distinct from zymogen activation, which requires proteolytic cleavage.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  28. In acute pancreatitis, prematurely activated trypsin initiates a cascade by proteolytically cleaving which of the following into its active form?

    • A. Enteropeptidase
    • B. Procolipase
    • C. Pepsinogen
    • D. HMG-CoA reductase
    • E. Glucokinase

    Answer: B. Procolipase

    Enteropeptidase initially activates trypsinogen to trypsin, and trypsin then activates the other pancreatic zymogens — including procolipase to colipase (and chymotrypsinogen and proelastase). In acute pancreatitis, premature intrapancreatic trypsin activation triggers this cascade and autodigestion.

  29. In general acid-base catalysis, which amino acid residue in the active site often gains or loses protons at physiological pH?

    • A. Leucine
    • B. Glycine
    • C. Histidine
    • D. Valine
    • E. Phenylalanine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Histidine

    Histidine has an imidazole side chain with a pKa close to physiological pH, so it can readily accept and donate protons during catalysis. It is therefore the residue most often used for general acid-base catalysis in enzyme active sites.

  30. Most enzymes, being proteins, have which structure?

    • A. Globular with irregular amino acid sequence
    • B. Fibrous with irregular sequence
    • C. Globular with regular repetitive sequence
    • D. Fibrous with regular sequence
    • E. Always quaternary aggregates
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Globular with irregular amino acid sequence

    Most enzymes are globular proteins: they fold into compact, roughly spherical shapes with irregular amino-acid sequences that create a specific active site and allow water solubility. Fibrous proteins with repetitive sequences serve structural roles, and enzymes are not required to be quaternary aggregates — many are single subunits.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  31. Which of the following is NOT a property of enzymes?

    • A. They are regulated by effectors
    • B. They are specific
    • C. They accelerate the reaction
    • D. They change the equilibrium constant
    • E. They complete the reaction without being used up
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. They change the equilibrium constant

    Enzymes accelerate a reaction by lowering activation energy and are themselves regenerated unchanged, but they do not alter the equilibrium constant or the free-energy difference between reactants and products. They are specific and can be regulated by effectors, so the other statements are true enzyme properties.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  32. The induced-fit model of enzyme action explains that:

    • A. The enzyme is rigid and the substrate changes shape
    • B. The enzyme changes its shape after substrate binding
    • C. The substrate is permanently modified before binding
    • D. The product binds first and induces the substrate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The enzyme changes its shape after substrate binding

    In the induced-fit model, substrate binding induces a conformational change in the enzyme that brings catalytic groups into the correct position. This differs from the older rigid lock-and-key model.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  33. Enzymes speed up reactions by:

    • A. Raising the activation energy
    • B. Lowering the activation energy
    • C. Changing the equilibrium constant
    • D. Being consumed in the reaction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Lowering the activation energy

    Enzymes provide an alternative pathway with lower activation energy, so more substrate molecules can react per unit time. They neither change the equilibrium constant nor are used up in the reaction.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  34. Enzymes that catalyse the addition or removal of chemical groups (other than by hydrolysis) are called:

    • A. Ligases
    • B. Transferases
    • C. Lyases
    • D. Isomerases
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Lyases

    Lyases cleave or form bonds by adding/removing groups, often creating double bonds, without hydrolysis. Ligases join two molecules using ATP, and transferases move a group from one molecule to another.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  35. An apoenzyme combined with its cofactor forms a complete, catalytically active enzyme called a:

    • A. Holoenzyme
    • B. Coenzyme
    • C. Zymogen
    • D. Ribozyme
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Holoenzyme

    The protein part alone (apoenzyme) is inactive until it binds its cofactor, and the complete active complex is the holoenzyme. A coenzyme is an organic cofactor itself, not the complete enzyme.

    Source: Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Enzymes

  36. A non-competitive inhibitor of an enzyme characteristically:

    • A. Increases Km with no change in Vmax
    • B. Decreases both Km and Vmax
    • C. Decreases Vmax with no change in Km
    • D. Increases both Km and Vmax
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Decreases Vmax with no change in Km

    A non-competitive inhibitor binds a site distinct from the active site on free enzyme or the ES complex, reducing the amount of functional enzyme, so Vmax falls while Km (affinity of remaining enzyme) is unchanged. Competitive inhibition raises Km with unchanged Vmax; uncompetitive inhibition lowers both Km and Vmax.

    Source: Lippincott Biochemistry — Enzymes
