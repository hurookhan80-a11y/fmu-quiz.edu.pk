Home › Anatomy MCQs › Abdomen

The abdomen is the part of the trunk between the diaphragm and the pelvis. It contains most of the digestive organs, including the stomach, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, and intestines, together with the kidneys and major blood vessels. Its wall is formed by layers of muscle that protect these organs and help breathing and posture.

  1. During endoscopic repair of a femoral hernia, the surgeon must be careful of injury to which artery crossing near the lacunar ligament?

    • A. Accessory obturator artery
    • B. Femoral artery
    • C. Lateral circumflex femoral artery
    • D. Medial circumflex femoral artery
    • E. Superficial epigastric artery
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Accessory obturator artery

    An accessory/aberrant obturator artery arising from inferior epigastric/external iliac may descend close to the lacunar ligament at the medial side of the femoral ring (corona mortis region) and can be cut during femoral hernia repair, causing severe bleeding.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 4: Abdomen

  2. An indirect inguinal hernia passes through the deep inguinal ring, which lies:

    • A. Through the linea alba
    • B. Medial to the inferior epigastric artery
    • C. Lateral to the inferior epigastric artery
    • D. Within the femoral canal
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Lateral to the inferior epigastric artery

    The deep ring is an opening in transversalis fascia lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, so an indirect hernia entering it lies lateral to the artery. A direct hernia pushes through Hesselbach's triangle medial to the inferior epigastric artery, and the femoral canal is below the inguinal ligament.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen

  3. The surface landmark for the base of the appendix (McBurney's point) is located:

    • A. At the midpoint of the inguinal ligament
    • B. At the transpyloric plane
    • C. Midway between the umbilicus and pubic symphysis
    • D. One-third of the way from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. One-third of the way from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus

    McBurney's point lies one-third along the line from the right anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus (two-thirds from the umbilicus), overlying the base of the appendix where appendicitis tenderness is maximal. The midpoint of the inguinal ligament marks the deep ring region, not the appendix.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen

  4. The portal vein is formed behind the neck of the pancreas at the level of L2 by the union of:

    • A. Splenic vein and superior mesenteric vein
    • B. Hepatic veins
    • C. Splenic vein and inferior mesenteric vein alone
    • D. Inferior mesenteric vein and superior mesenteric vein
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Splenic vein and superior mesenteric vein

    The portal vein forms behind the neck of the pancreas at L2 from the splenic and superior mesenteric veins, with the inferior mesenteric vein usually joining the splenic vein. The hepatic veins drain the liver into the inferior vena cava and take no part in portal formation.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen

  5. In a supine patient with intraperitoneal bleeding, blood most dependently collects in the:

    • A. Rectouterine pouch in males
    • B. Hepatorenal recess (Morison's pouch)
    • C. Lesser sac
    • D. Left paracolic gutter only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Hepatorenal recess (Morison's pouch)

    The hepatorenal recess between the liver and right kidney is the most dependent part of the upper peritoneal cavity in the supine position and a common site for free fluid on FAST scanning. The rectovesical (not rectouterine) pouch is dependent when erect in males, and the lesser sac is behind the stomach.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen

  6. The coeliac trunk, the artery of the foregut, arises from the abdominal aorta at vertebral level:

    • A. L1
    • B. L3
    • C. T12
    • D. T10
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. T12

    The coeliac trunk arises just below the aortic hiatus at T12 and trifurcates into left gastric, splenic and common hepatic arteries supplying foregut derivatives. The superior mesenteric artery arises at L1 and the inferior mesenteric at L3.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen

  7. Below the arcuate line, the posterior wall of the rectus sheath is deficient because:

    • A. The peritoneum is absent
    • B. Rectus abdominis splits into two bellies
    • C. Transversus abdominis passes behind rectus
    • D. All aponeuroses pass anterior to rectus abdominis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. All aponeuroses pass anterior to rectus abdominis

    Below the arcuate line (about midway between umbilicus and pubic symphysis) the aponeuroses of all three flat muscles pass anterior to rectus, leaving only transversalis fascia posteriorly — a site of weakness. Above the line the aponeuroses split around the muscle.

    Source: BD Chaurasia's Human Anatomy — Abdomen

  8. The anterior boundary of the epiploic foramen (of Winslow), through which the lesser sac is entered, is formed by:

    • A. Portal triad in the free edge of the lesser omentum
    • B. First part of the duodenum
    • C. Inferior vena cava
    • D. Caudate lobe of the liver
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Portal triad in the free edge of the lesser omentum

    The hepatoduodenal ligament containing the portal triad (bile duct, hepatic artery proper and portal vein) forms the anterior boundary of the epiploic foramen, which is why clamping it (Pringle manoeuvre) controls hepatic inflow. The IVC is the posterior boundary and the caudate lobe the superior boundary.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen

  9. In superior mesenteric artery syndrome, the structure compressed between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery is the:

    • A. First part of duodenum
    • B. Third (horizontal) part of duodenum
    • C. Neck of pancreas
    • D. Left renal vein only, never bowel
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Third (horizontal) part of duodenum

    The third part of the duodenum crosses the midline at L3 in the angle between the aorta posteriorly and the superior mesenteric artery anteriorly, and is compressed when that angle narrows, causing proximal obstruction. The left renal vein can be compressed in the same angle (nutcracker phenomenon), but the bowel structure asked here is the third part of duodenum.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Abdomen
