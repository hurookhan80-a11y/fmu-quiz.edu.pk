Cardiovascular System MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Anatomy

Home › Anatomy MCQs › Cardiovascular System

The cardiovascular system consists of the heart and the blood vessels that carry blood around the body. The heart pumps blood through two circuits: the pulmonary circuit to the lungs and the systemic circuit to the rest of the body. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return it, and capillaries allow exchange with the tissues.

  1. A vessel with a large lumen, thin wall and valves is a:

    • A. Artery
    • B. Capillary
    • C. Vein
    • D. Venule
    • E. Arteriole
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Vein

    A vein is recognised by its large lumen, thin wall, collapsed shape, and presence of valves. Arteries have thicker walls, smaller round lumina, and no valves.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  2. Continuous capillaries differ from fenestrated capillaries by having

    • A. Gaps between endothelial cells
    • B. Tight junctions without pores
    • C. Wide lumen
    • D. Fenestration
    • E. Discontinuous basement membrane
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Tight junctions without pores

    Continuous capillaries have uninterrupted endothelium joined by tight junctions and a continuous basement membrane, with no pores. Fenestrated capillaries have endothelial fenestrations that allow rapid exchange in kidneys and endocrine glands.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  3. On histology a vessel shows a thick tunica media with a prominent elastic lamina. It is most likely a:

    • A. Elastic artery
    • B. Muscular artery
    • C. Arteriole
    • D. Capillary
    • E. Vein
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Muscular artery

    A muscular or distributing artery has a thick tunica media of circular smooth muscle and a prominent internal elastic lamina. Elastic arteries like the aorta have many elastic lamellae in the media instead of a single prominent lamina.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  4. The layer of vessel responsible for vasodilation and vasoconstriction is:

    • A. Tunica intima
    • B. Tunica media
    • C. Tunica adventitia
    • D. Basement membrane
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Tunica media

    Vasoconstriction and vasodilation are produced by smooth muscle in the tunica media contracting or relaxing to change vessel diameter. The tunica intima is only endothelium and subendothelial tissue and cannot contract.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  5. Under light microscope examination, prominent internal elastic lamina and circular smooth muscle are found in:

    • A. Vein
    • B. Venule
    • C. Muscular artery
    • D. Elastic artery
    • E. Capillary
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Muscular artery

    Prominent internal elastic lamina with thick circular smooth muscle in the tunica media identifies a muscular artery under the microscope. Elastic arteries show multiple wavy elastic lamellae throughout the media and a less distinct single lamina.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  6. Vasa vasorum are located in which layer of a blood vessel wall?

    • A. Tunica intima
    • B. Tunica adventitia
    • C. Tunica media
    • D. Endothelium only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Tunica adventitia

    Vasa vasorum, the small vessels supplying the wall of large vessels, lie in the tunica adventitia (and outer media). The intima is the inner endothelial lining and contains no vasa vasorum.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  7. Smooth muscle is present mainly in which layer of a blood vessel wall?

    • A. Tunica intima
    • B. Tunica adventitia
    • C. Tunica media
    • D. Basement membrane
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Tunica media

    The tunica media contains smooth muscle (with elastic fibers in large arteries) and regulates vessel diameter. The adventitia is mainly connective tissue with vasa vasorum and nerves.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  8. Endothelial cells line which layer of a blood vessel?

    • A. Tunica media
    • B. Tunica adventitia
    • C. Tunica albuginea
    • D. Tunica intima
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Tunica intima

    The tunica intima consists of endothelium on a basement membrane with a thin subendothelial layer. Smooth muscle belongs to the media, not the intima.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  9. Capillaries are made up of which cells (single layer)?

    • A. Smooth muscle cells
    • B. Endothelial cells
    • C. Fibroblasts
    • D. Squamous cells of mesothelium only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Endothelial cells

    A capillary wall is a single layer of endothelial cells on a basement membrane, ideal for exchange. It lacks a tunica media, so smooth muscle is absent in true capillaries.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  10. In a fetus, failure of the endocardial cushions to form an intact crux of the heart most characteristically leaves which structure patent?

    • A. Ductus arteriosus
    • B. Ostium primum
    • C. Interventricular foramen
    • D. Foramen ovale
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Ostium primum

    The endocardial cushions form the crux and close the ostium primum as part of the atrioventricular septal complex. Their failure produces an ostium primum atrial septal defect, whereas the foramen ovale is a normal fetal opening with a different mechanism.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System

  11. Smooth muscle is characteristically present in the tunica adventitia of which vessel?

    • A. Pulmonary artery
    • B. Superior vena cava only in its intrapericardial part
    • C. Inferior vena cava
    • D. Aorta
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Inferior vena cava

    Large veins such as the inferior vena cava have longitudinal smooth muscle bundles in a well-developed tunica adventitia. In elastic arteries like the aorta, smooth muscle is concentrated in the tunica media instead.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  12. An aneurysm most commonly results from weakening of which tunic of the arterial wall?

    • A. Tunica media
    • B. Tunica intima
    • C. Tunica adventitia
    • D. Internal elastic lamina alone
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Tunica media

    Loss of smooth muscle and elastic tissue in the tunica media weakens the wall and allows aneurysmal dilatation under arterial pressure. The intima may show atherosclerosis, but medial weakness is the key structural defect.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Cardiovascular System

  13. Descent of the developing heart into the thorax is mainly a result of:

    • A. Fusion of the dorsal aortae
    • B. Craniocaudal (cephalocaudal) folding of the embryo
    • C. Growth of the liver alone
    • D. Lateral folding of the embryo
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Craniocaudal (cephalocaudal) folding of the embryo

    Rapid growth and craniocaudal folding bring the heart tube ventrally and caudally into the thoracic region. Lateral folding mainly forms the body wall and gut tube.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System

  14. Tetralogy of Fallot results from defective development and malalignment of which structure?

    • A. Aorticopulmonary (conotruncal) septum
    • B. Septum primum
    • C. Ductus venosus
    • D. Endocardial cushions only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Aorticopulmonary (conotruncal) septum

    Unequal division and anterior malalignment of the aorticopulmonary (infundibular/conotruncal) septum produce pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta, ventricular septal defect and right ventricular hypertrophy. Endocardial cushion defects instead cause atrioventricular septal defects.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System

  15. The smooth part of the right atrium (sinus venarum) is derived embryologically from the:

    • A. Endocardial cushions
    • B. Primitive atrium
    • C. Sinus venosus (right horn)
    • D. Bulbus cordis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Sinus venosus (right horn)

    The right horn of the sinus venosus is incorporated into the right atrium to form its smooth-walled sinus venarum. The primitive atrium forms the rough, trabeculated part with pectinate muscles.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System

  16. If the conotruncal (aorticopulmonary) septum fails to spiral normally during development, it leads to:

    • A. Tetralogy of Fallot
    • B. Transposition of the great arteries
    • C. Patent ductus arteriosus
    • D. Coarctation of the aorta
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Transposition of the great arteries

    Normal spiralling of the conotruncal septum makes the aorta and pulmonary trunk cross each other and connect to the correct ventricles. Failure to spiral gives transposition, where the aorta arises from the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk from the left ventricle.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System

  17. The vitelline veins primarily drain blood from the:

    • A. Placenta via the connecting stalk
    • B. Yolk sac
    • C. Amnion
    • D. Allantois only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Yolk sac

    Vitelline veins carry blood from the yolk sac to the sinus venosus of the early heart and later contribute to the portal venous system and liver sinusoids. Umbilical veins, in the connecting stalk, drain the placenta.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System

  18. Abnormal (reversed) looping of the heart tube during development leads most directly to:

    • A. Dextrocardia
    • B. Ectopia cordis through the sternum in all cases
    • C. Acyanotic ASD only
    • D. Normal situs with no change
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Dextrocardia

    The heart tube normally loops to the right (D-loop); reversed (L-loop) looping places the heart on the right side - dextrocardia, which may occur alone or as part of situs inversus. Situs inversus is the broader mirror-image arrangement of organs.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Cardiovascular System
