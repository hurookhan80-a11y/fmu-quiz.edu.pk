Respiratory System MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Anatomy

Home › Anatomy MCQs › Respiratory System

The respiratory system brings air into the body and removes carbon dioxide. Air passes through the nose, pharynx, larynx, and trachea into the bronchi and finally the alveoli of the lungs. In the alveoli, oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves it, across a very thin membrane.

  1. Cuboidal cells with lamellar bodies seen in alveoli function in:

    • A. Formation of the blood-air barrier
    • B. Transport of gases
    • C. Production of surfactant
    • D. Phagocytosis
    • E. Support of alveoli
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Production of surfactant

    Cuboidal type II pneumocytes contain lamellar bodies that store and secrete pulmonary surfactant. Type I pneumocytes are flat cells forming the blood-air barrier for gas exchange.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  2. Damage to which type of cell affects the mucociliary clearance mechanism?

    • A. Goblet cells
    • B. Cilia of columnar cells
    • C. Serous glands
    • D. Basal cells
    • E. Elastic fibers
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Cilia of columnar cells

    Mucociliary clearance depends on coordinated beating of cilia on columnar epithelial cells that moves the mucus blanket upward toward the pharynx. Goblet cells produce mucus but do not move it.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  3. If a 1st year MBBS student observes Clara cells in a histology slide which type of bronchiole it is?

    • A. Primary bronchiole
    • B. Secondary bronchiole
    • C. Tertiary bronchiole
    • D. Terminal bronchiole
    • E. Respiratory bronchiole
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Terminal bronchiole

    Clara or club cells are non-ciliated dome-shaped secretory cells characteristic of terminal bronchioles. They protect the bronchiolar epithelium and act as stem cells for its repair.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  4. In newborn respiratory distress syndrome, the cells responsible for surfactant production are:

    • A. Alveolar macrophages
    • B. Clara cells
    • C. Goblet cells
    • D. Type I pneumocytes
    • E. Type II pneumocytes
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. Type II pneumocytes

    Type II pneumocytes secrete surfactant, which lowers alveolar surface tension and prevents alveolar collapse at end-expiration. Deficiency in premature infants causes neonatal respiratory distress syndrome with widespread atelectasis.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  5. It stated that Type 2 pneumocytes are responsible for:

    • A. Gas exchange
    • B. Surfactant production
    • C. Formation of alveolar pores
    • D. Storage of alveolar macrophages
    • E. Providing support
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Surfactant production

    Type II pneumocytes are responsible for surfactant production and also regenerate type I pneumocytes after injury. Gas exchange itself occurs across flat type I pneumocytes.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  6. The lumen of the trachea does not collapse when there is no airflow because of the:

    • A. C-shaped hyaline cartilages
    • B. Smooth muscle fibres
    • C. Mucous glands
    • D. Submucosal connective tissue
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. C-shaped hyaline cartilages

    C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings keep the tracheal lumen patent during breathing while the posterior gap allows oesophageal expansion during swallowing. Loss of cartilage support would let the airway collapse when airflow stops.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  7. The respiratory epithelium lining most of the conducting airways is:

    • A. Simple squamous epithelium
    • B. Stratified squamous epithelium
    • C. Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium
    • D. Simple cuboidal epithelium
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium

    The trachea and bronchi are lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells for mucociliary clearance. Simple squamous epithelium is found in alveoli for gas exchange.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  8. Mucus in the respiratory tract is secreted mainly by which cells?

    • A. Type II pneumocytes
    • B. Goblet cells (and submucosal glands)
    • C. Alveolar macrophages
    • D. Clara cells for surfactant only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Goblet cells (and submucosal glands)

    Goblet cells interspersed in respiratory epithelium secrete mucus that traps inhaled particles for mucociliary clearance. Type II pneumocytes secrete surfactant, not mucus.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  9. In the blood-air barrier of the lung, tight junctions that seal the alveolar lining are present between which cells?

    • A. Type I pneumocytes and endothelial cells across the fused basal lamina
    • B. Adjacent type I pneumocytes
    • C. Endothelial cells and pericytes
    • D. Type I and type II pneumocytes of the same alveolus only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Adjacent type I pneumocytes

    Adjacent type I pneumocytes are joined by tight junctions that prevent tissue fluid from entering the alveolar space. The type I cell and the capillary endothelium are separated by a fused basal lamina, not by a tight junction.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  10. Excessive mucus plugging of an airway epithelium indicates increased activity of which cells?

    • A. Basal cells
    • B. Brush cells
    • C. Ciliated columnar cells
    • D. Goblet (mucus) cells
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Goblet (mucus) cells

    Goblet cells are unicellular glands that secrete mucus, so mucus hypersecretion and plugging reflect their increased activity. Ciliated cells move the mucus layer but do not produce its bulk.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  11. The special secretory cells of terminal bronchioles that help prevent adhesion of the luminal surfaces are:

    • A. Kulchitsky cells
    • B. Type II pneumocytes
    • C. Goblet cells
    • D. Clara (club) cells
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Clara (club) cells

    Clara (club) cells in terminal bronchioles secrete a surfactant-like material and detoxifying enzymes that keep the small airway open and protect its epithelium. Goblet cells largely disappear at this level of the bronchial tree.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  12. Smooth muscle is normally absent from which part of the respiratory tract?

    • A. Bronchi
    • B. Alveoli
    • C. Trachea
    • D. Alveolar ducts
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Alveoli

    Alveolar walls consist of pneumocytes, capillaries and scant connective tissue for gas exchange and contain no smooth muscle. Alveolar ducts and bronchioles do have smooth muscle that regulates airway caliber.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  13. The epithelium and glands of the trachea and bronchi are derived from which germ layer?

    • A. Mesoderm
    • B. Ectoderm
    • C. Neural crest
    • D. Endoderm of the respiratory diverticulum
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Endoderm of the respiratory diverticulum

    The laryngotracheal diverticulum from the foregut endoderm forms the epithelial lining and glands of the lower respiratory tract. Surrounding mesoderm forms cartilage, smooth muscle and connective tissue.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Respiratory System

  14. Cells abundant in the terminal bronchioles are:

    • A. Type I pneumocytes
    • B. Type II pneumocytes
    • C. Clara (club) cells
    • D. Goblet cells
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Clara (club) cells

    Clara (club) cells are non-ciliated secretory cells that become prominent in terminal bronchioles, where goblet cells disappear. They secrete protective components and help detoxify inhaled substances.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  15. Which statement is true about respiratory bronchioles?

    • A. They have cartilage plates in their walls
    • B. Gaseous exchange can occur in them
    • C. They are lined by pseudostratified ciliated epithelium throughout
    • D. They contain submucosal glands
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Gaseous exchange can occur in them

    Respiratory bronchioles have alveoli budding from their walls, so some gaseous exchange occurs in them. Cartilage and glands are features of larger bronchi, not respiratory bronchioles.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System

  16. Surfactant in the lungs is produced at the alveolar level by which cells?

    • A. Type II pneumocytes
    • B. Type I pneumocytes
    • C. Alveolar macrophages
    • D. Club (Clara) cells
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Type II pneumocytes

    Type II pneumocytes contain lamellar bodies and secrete pulmonary surfactant, which lowers alveolar surface tension. Type I pneumocytes are thin cells for gas exchange and do not produce surfactant.

    Source: Laiq Hussain Siddiqui, Medical Histology — Respiratory System
