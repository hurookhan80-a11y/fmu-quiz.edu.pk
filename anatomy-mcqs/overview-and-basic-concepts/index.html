Anatomy is the study of the structure of the human body. Basic concepts include the anatomical position, directional terms such as anterior and posterior, and the planes that divide the body, including sagittal, coronal, and transverse planes. These terms give every structure a precise and agreed location.

  1. A 45 year old man got a fracture in skull. X-ray is done they found a diploe structure in the bone . Which kind of bone can it be ?

    • A. flat
    • B. sesamoid
    • C. irregular
    • D. large
    • E. small
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. flat

    Diploe is the layer of spongy, cancellous bone sandwiched between the inner and outer tables of compact bone in flat bones of the skull. It contains red marrow and diploic veins. Sesamoid, irregular and other bone types do not show this named sandwich arrangement.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  2. Fibrous sheet or band under the skin that also invests muscles is called:

    • A. Fascia
    • B. Aponeurosis
    • C. Tendon
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Fascia

    Fascia is a fibrous sheet or band under the skin and investing muscles — superficial fascia lies under skin and deep fascia invests muscles. A tendon connects muscle to bone, and an aponeurosis is a flat, sheet-like tendon, not the investing layer itself.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  3. Irregular bones are characteristically found in which part of the skeleton?

    • A. Flat bones of the calvaria
    • B. Vertebrae of the spine
    • C. Short bones of the carpus
    • D. Long bones of the limbs
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Vertebrae of the spine

    Vertebrae are the classic example of irregular bones, with complex shapes that do not fit the long, short or flat categories. Skull and facial bones include some irregular bones, but the vertebral column is the standard exam example.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  4. What is the main function of the synovial membrane?

    • A. To produce articular cartilage
    • B. To absorb calcium from joint fluid
    • C. To connect bone to bone
    • D. To produce synovial fluid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. To produce synovial fluid

    The synovial membrane lines the joint capsule and secretes synovial fluid. This fluid lubricates the joint and nourishes the avascular articular cartilage.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  5. What is the function of ligaments at a joint?

    • A. Connect bone to bone and support the joint
    • B. Produce synovial fluid
    • C. Connect muscle to bone
    • D. Store fat around the joint
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Connect bone to bone and support the joint

    Ligaments are dense connective tissue bands that connect bone to bone. They support and stabilise the joint, unlike tendons which connect muscle to bone.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  6. What is a key function of articular cartilage in a synovial joint?

    • A. It provides a smooth surface for opposing bones
    • B. It secretes synovial fluid
    • C. It anchors ligaments inside the joint
    • D. It produces blood cells
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. It provides a smooth surface for opposing bones

    Hyaline articular cartilage covers the opposing bone surfaces in synovial joints. Its smooth, slippery surface reduces friction and helps distribute load during movement.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  7. Which tissue mainly supports structures such as the nose and trachea while allowing some flexibility?

    • A. Elastic tissue
    • B. Stratified squamous epithelium
    • C. Adipose tissue
    • D. Dense bone tissue
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Elastic tissue

    Elastic tissue, rich in elastin fibres, gives support with recoil and flexibility. It helps supporting structures such as the nose and airways keep their shape while still bending.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  8. The joint between the atlas (C1) and axis (C2) is which type of joint?

    • A. Saddle joint
    • B. Pivot joint
    • C. Hinge joint
    • D. Ball-and-socket joint
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Pivot joint

    The median atlanto-axial joint is a pivot joint, where the dens of the axis rotates within the ring of the atlas. This allows the head to turn side to side, as in saying 'no'.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts

  9. A muscle in which the muscle fibres are attached to both sides of a central tendon is called:

    • A. Unipennate
    • B. Multipennate
    • C. Bipennate
    • D. Parallel
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Bipennate

    In a bipennate muscle, fibres arise from both sides of a central tendon, like the two halves of a feather. In a unipennate muscle the fibres attach to only one side.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 1: Overview and Basic Concepts
