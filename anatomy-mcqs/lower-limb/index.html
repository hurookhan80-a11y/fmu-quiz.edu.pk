Home › Anatomy MCQs › Lower Limb

The lower limb consists of the hip, thigh, leg, and foot. Its main bones are the femur, tibia, and fibula, with the patella in front of the knee and the tarsals, metatarsals, and phalanges in the foot. It is supplied mainly by the femoral and sciatic systems of vessels and nerves.

  1. Clinical manifestations of fracture of upper end of fibula:

    • A. Anesthesia on dorsum of foot
    • B. Anesthesia on plantar surface of foot
    • C. Loss of toe extension
    • D. Anaesthesia on medial side of leg
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Anesthesia on dorsum of foot

    The common fibular (peroneal) nerve winds around the neck/upper end of the fibula and is injured by fracture there. Its superficial fibular branch supplies the dorsum of the foot, so anesthesia there is expected. Plantar sensation is tibial nerve territory, and medial leg is saphenous territory.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 6: Lower Limb

  2. fracture of sustantaculum talli .which of the following movements will be lost. ?

    • A. dorsiflexion
    • B. plantar flexion
    • C. imversion
    • D. eversion
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. imversion

    The sustentaculum tali of the calcaneus supports tendons passing below it, including tibialis posterior and flexor digitorum longus, which invert the foot. Fracture here therefore weakens inversion. Dorsiflexion is by anterior leg muscles and eversion by fibularis muscles, which are not supported by this projection.

  3. Jumping is done by

    • A. gastrocnemius
    • B. soleus
    • C. flexor digitorum longus
    • D. flexor hallucis longus
    • E. tibialis posterior

    Answer: A. gastrocnemius

    Jumping and propulsion at take-off need powerful plantarflexion, chiefly by gastrocnemius (with soleus) acting through the calcaneal tendon. Gastrocnemius is the fast, powerful muscle crossing both knee and ankle. The long flexors of toes contribute little to whole-body propulsion.

  6. "Weaver's bottom" is inflammation of the bursa between which structures?

    Weaver's bottom is ischial bursitis: inflammation of the ischiogluteal bursa between gluteus maximus and the ischial tuberosity, caused by prolonged sitting on hard surfaces. The greater trochanter bursa lies laterally and causes lateral hip pain, not sitting pain.

  7. A 10-year-old falls from a tree house, compressing the sole and fracturing the head of the talus. Which structure cannot function normally?

    • A. Medial longitudinal arch
    • B. Lateral longitudinal arch
    • C. Long plantar ligament
    • D. Transverse arch
    • E. Tendon of peroneus longus alone
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Medial longitudinal arch

    The head of the talus is the keystone of the medial longitudinal arch and is supported by the spring ligament; fracture of the talar head collapses this arch. The lateral arch is lower and formed by calcaneus/cuboid, and the transverse arch is at the midfoot level.

  8. A 20-year-old cannot abduct and medially rotate the thigh while running and climbing. Which muscles are most likely damaged?

    • A. Biceps femoris
    • B. Gluteus medius and minimus
    • C. Semimembranosus
    • D. Semitendinosus
    • E. Sartorius
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Gluteus medius and minimus

    Gluteus medius and minimus, supplied by the superior gluteal nerve, are the chief abductors of the thigh and also medially rotate it; their loss impairs running and climbing gait. Hamstrings (semimembranosus/tendinosus, biceps) extend the hip and flex the knee instead.

  9. A 52-year-old woman slipped and fell and now cannot extend her leg at the knee joint. Which muscle is paralysed?

    • A. Gracilis
    • B. Gluteus maximus
    • C. Semimembranosus
    • D. Tibialis anterior
    • E. Quadriceps femoris
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. Quadriceps femoris

    Extension of the leg at the knee is produced only by quadriceps femoris (femoral nerve L2–L4). Its paralysis makes knee extension impossible. Hamstrings and semimembranosus flex the knee, gracilis adducts the thigh, and tibialis anterior dorsiflexes the foot.

    An elderly patient falling with a shortened and laterally (externally) rotated leg has the classic presentation of a fractured neck of femur, with muscles pulling the distal fragment into external rotation and shortening.   14. A dashboard injury causing posterior cruciate ligament damage: the primary function of the PCL is to:

    • B. Prevent posterior displacement of tibia on femur
    • C. Prevent hyperextension of knee
    • D. Prevent medial displacement of tibia
    • E. Prevent lateral displacement of tibia
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Prevent posterior displacement of tibia on femur

    The posterior cruciate ligament prevents posterior displacement of the tibia on the femur (posterior sag), as tested by the posterior drawer sign and injured in dashboard injuries. The ACL does the opposite — it prevents anterior tibial displacement.

  15. A femoral hernia sac lies immediately lateral to which structure (its medial boundary)?

    • A. Femoral nerve
    • B. Femoral artery
    • C. Femoral vein
    • D. Inferior epigastric artery
    • E. Lacunar ligament
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. Lacunar ligament

    The femoral canal, medial to the femoral vein, is bounded medially by the sharp lacunar (Gimbernat) ligament. This rigid medial boundary is why femoral hernias strangulate easily. The femoral nerve/artery/vein lie lateral to the canal in that order (NAVY lateral to medial).

  16. A girl wearing high heels falls and twists (inverts) her ankle. Which ligament is stretched/injured?

    • C. Long plantar ligament

    • E. Short plantar ligament

    Answer: B. Lateral ligament complex

    A forced inversion injury sprains the lateral ligament complex of the ankle, most commonly the anterior talofibular ligament. The strong deltoid (medial) ligament is injured in eversion, and plantar ligaments support the arches rather than resisting inversion.

  17. A high-jump athlete now cannot flex the hip and extend the knee at the same time, with gluteal region pain. Likely diagnosis?

    • B. Neck of femur fracture
    • C. Avulsion of ischial tuberosity
    • D. Avulsion of tibial tuberosity
    • E. Patella fracture

    Answer: C. Avulsion of ischial tuberosity

    The hamstrings take origin from the ischial tuberosity; violent contraction in a young athlete can avulse this apophysis, weakening hip extension combined with knee flexion and causing gluteal pain. Tibial tuberosity avulsion affects quadriceps insertion and knee extension, not this pattern.

  19. A nerve that traverses the adductor canal and then pierces the medial intermuscular septum to become superficial near the knee is the:

    • C. Obturator nerve
    • D. Sciatic nerve

    Answer: B. Saphenous nerve

    The saphenous nerve travels through the adductor canal with the femoral vessels, then pierces the fascia/medial intermuscular region to become superficial at the knee and supply the medial leg/foot.   20. A patient cannot prevent posterior displacement of the femur on the tibia (anterior tibial translation). Which ligament is damaged?

    • A. Oblique popliteal ligament
    • B. Anterior cruciate ligament
    • C. Tibial collateral ligament
    • D. Fibular collateral ligament
    • E. Patellar ligament

    Answer: B. Anterior cruciate ligament

    Posterior displacement of the femur on the tibia is the same relative motion as anterior displacement of the tibia, which is prevented by the anterior cruciate ligament. A torn ACL therefore allows this translation (Lachman/anterior drawer positive). The PCL prevents the opposite, posterior tibial sag.

  24. After hip dislocation and recovery, a patient cannot extend the thigh at the hip joint. Which muscle is solely responsible for this loss?

  25. After pelvic surgery a patient develops a Trendelenburg gait. Which nerve is most likely damaged?

    • A. Superior gluteal nerve
    • B. Inferior gluteal nerve
    • C. Sciatic nerve
    • D. Obturator nerve
    • E. Pudendal nerve

    Trendelenburg gait after pelvic surgery indicates superior gluteal nerve injury paralyzing gluteus medius and minimus, so the pelvis drops on the unsupported (opposite) side during stance. Inferior gluteal injury affects gluteus maximus and hip extension, not pelvic stability in stance.

  26. An athlete has difficulty with flexion of the knee and extension of the hip. Which muscles are likely paralysed?

    • A. Rectus femoris
    • B. Gluteus medius and minimus
    • C. Obturator externus and gemelli
    • D. Semimembranosus and semitendinosus
    • E. Tensor fasciae latae
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Semimembranosus and semitendinosus

    Semimembranosus and semitendinosus are hamstrings: they extend the hip and flex the knee. Paralysis of these medial hamstrings impairs both movements together. Rectus femoris does the opposite (flexes hip, extends knee), and gluteus medius/minimus abduct the hip.

  27. An elderly woman fractures the greater trochanter of the femur. Which muscle would continue to function normally?

    • A. Gluteus medius
    • B. Gluteus minimus
    • C. Gluteus maximus
    • D. Piriformis
    • E. Obturator internus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Gluteus maximus

    Most muscles inserting on the greater trochanter — gluteus medius, minimus, piriformis and obturator internus — lose function when it fractures. Gluteus maximus inserts mainly into the iliotibial tract and gluteal tuberosity of the femur, not the greater trochanter, so it continues to work.

  28. Damage around the greater trochanter affecting abduction of the hip most likely involves which muscle?

    • B. Quadratus femoris
    • C. Adductor longus
    • D. Iliopsoas

  29. Difficulty in climbing (hip extension) due to gluteus maximus paralysis indicates injury to which nerve?

  32. Fracture of the neck of femur causes avascular necrosis of the femoral head mainly due to loss of supply from which artery?

    • B. Profunda femoris artery
    • C. Obturator artery
    • D. Medial circumflex femoral artery
    • E. Superior gluteal artery

    The adult femoral head is supplied mainly by retinacular branches of the medial circumflex femoral artery running up the neck; an intracapsular neck fracture tears them, causing avascular necrosis of the head.   34. Gunshot injury to the lateral side of the upper leg causes inability to dorsiflex the foot. Which fracture is responsible?

    • A. Head of fibula
    • B. Neck of fibula
    • C. Upper end of tibia
    • D. Shaft of tibia

    The common fibular (peroneal) nerve winds superficially around the neck of the fibula; fracture there (or gunshot to lateral upper leg) injures it, paralyzing dorsiflexors (deep fibular) and causing foot drop. Tibial shaft fractures do not selectively hit this nerve at the fibular neck.

  35. In a distal femoral fracture, the fragment enters the popliteal fossa. Which structure, being deepest in the fossa, is most likely injured?

  36. In congenital hip dislocation, which structure deepens the acetabulum and stabilises the hip joint?

  37. In congenital talipes equinovarus (clubfoot), the foot is typically:

    • A. Dorsiflexed and inverted
    • B. Dorsiflexed and everted
    • C. Plantarflexed and inverted
    • D. Plantarflexed and everted
    • E. Neutral and inverted
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Plantarflexed and inverted

    Congenital talipes equinovarus (clubfoot) combines equinus (plantarflexion), varus (inversion of heel), forefoot adduction and cavus — so the foot is plantarflexed and inverted. Dorsiflexed/everted describes calcaneovalgus, the opposite deformity.

  38. In erect posture, the line of gravity falling slightly behind the hip joints is resisted (posture maintained) mainly by which structure?

    • B. Interosseous sacroiliac ligament
    • C. Gluteal muscles alone
    • D. Iliofemoral ligament
    • E. Pubofemoral ligament

    Answer: D. Iliofemoral ligament

    In erect standing, the line of gravity falls slightly behind the hip joints, tending to extend the hip; this is resisted passively by the strong iliofemoral (Y) ligament, allowing standing with little muscle work. Gluteal muscles act when walking/bending, not as the main static check.

  39. In the 'unhappy triad' injury of the knee (medial meniscus + medial collateral ligament injured), which other ligament is damaged?

    • A. Patellar ligament
    • B. Anterior cruciate ligament
    • C. Posterior cruciate ligament
    • D. Deltoid ligament
    • E. Fibular collateral ligament
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Anterior cruciate ligament

    The unhappy triad from a lateral blow to the knee is anterior cruciate ligament + medial (tibial) collateral ligament + medial meniscus injury. The PCL is injured by posterior forces (dashboard), and the patellar ligament is the quadriceps tendon continuation, not part of the triad.

  40. Inflammation involving the superior aspect of the patella (suprapatellar region) is most closely related to which structure?

    • A. Patellar ligament
    • B. Anterior cruciate ligament
    • C. Posterior cruciate ligament
    • D. Quadriceps tendon
    • E. Medial meniscus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Quadriceps tendon

    The quadriceps tendon inserts onto the base/superior pole of the patella, and the suprapatellar bursa lies above it; inflammation/pain in the suprapatellar region therefore relates most closely to the quadriceps tendon insertion. Cruciate ligaments are intra-articular in the knee centre, not suprapatellar.

  41. Intramuscular injection in the inferomedial quadrant of the gluteal region causing foot drop is due to damage of:

    • A. Obturator nerve
    • B. Superior gluteal nerve
    • C. Inferior gluteal nerve
    • D. Sciatic nerve
    • E. Femoral nerve
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Sciatic nerve

    An injection placed in the inferomedial quadrant of the buttock endangers the sciatic nerve, which exits below piriformis medially; injury paralyzes hamstrings and all muscles below the knee, causing foot drop. The safe site is the superolateral quadrant. Superior gluteal nerve injury causes Trendelenburg, not foot drop.

  42. Loss of adductor longus function during sport results in loss of which thigh movement?

    • A. Adduction of thigh
    • B. Abduction of thigh
    • C. Extension of thigh
    • D. Lateral rotation of thigh
    • E. Flexion of knee
