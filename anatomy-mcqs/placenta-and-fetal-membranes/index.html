Placenta and Fetal Membranes MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Anatomy

Home › Anatomy MCQs › Placenta and Fetal Membranes

The placenta is the organ that connects the developing fetus to the wall of the uterus during pregnancy. It allows the exchange of oxygen, nutrients, and waste products between the maternal and fetal blood without the two bloods mixing. The fetal membranes, including the amnion and chorion, enclose the fetus and the amniotic fluid.

  1. Which fetal part forms the fetal component of the placenta?

    • A. Allantois
    • B. Chorion
    • C. Yolk sac
    • D. Amnion
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Chorion

    The fetal part of the placenta develops from the chorion frondosum, formed by the chorion and its villi. The maternal part comes from the decidua basalis of the uterus.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  2. What is the main source of nutrition for the fetus?

    • A. Placenta
    • B. Umbilical vein fat stores
    • C. Amniotic fluid swallowing
    • D. Yolk sac only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Placenta

    The placenta is the main organ of exchange, supplying oxygen and nutrients from the mother to the fetus. Swallowed amniotic fluid contributes little to overall fetal nutrition.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  3. Which structure connects the fetus to the placenta (and so to the mother)?

    • A. Amnion
    • B. Umbilical cord
    • C. Ductus arteriosus
    • D. Chorionic plate only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Umbilical cord

    The umbilical cord connects the fetus to the placenta and contains the umbilical vessels. Through the placenta, exchange with the maternal blood takes place.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  4. If the placenta separates prematurely / ruptures from the uterine wall, which condition will mainly occur?

    • A. Polyhydramnios
    • B. Haemorrhage
    • C. Fetal macrosomia
    • D. Premature rupture of membranes only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Haemorrhage

    Premature separation of the placenta (abruptio placentae) causes bleeding/haemorrhage, which can endanger both mother and fetus. Premature labour is more often linked to rupture of the amniochorionic membranes than to placental separation itself.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  5. Which hormone maintains the corpus luteum during early pregnancy?

    • A. Oxytocin
    • B. Estrogen alone
    • C. FSH
    • D. Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)

    hCG secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast rescues and maintains the corpus luteum so it keeps producing progesterone until the placenta takes over. Without hCG, the corpus luteum regresses and menstruation occurs.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  6. The fetal part of the placenta is formed by the:

    • A. Decidua basalis
    • B. Yolk sac
    • C. Chorion frondosum
    • D. Amnion only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Chorion frondosum

    The placenta has a fetal part formed by the chorion frondosum (villous chorion) and a maternal part formed by the decidua basalis. The amnion surrounds the fetus but does not form the placental exchange tissue.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  7. The main source of nutrition for the fetus is the:

    • A. Amniotic fluid swallowed only
    • B. Yolk sac throughout pregnancy
    • C. Maternal liver directly
    • D. Placenta
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Placenta

    After implantation, the placenta is the main organ of nutrition, gas exchange, and waste removal for the fetus. The yolk sac is important only very early and mainly for blood cell formation and primordial germ cells.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  8. Which structure connects the fetus to the placenta (and thus the mother)?

    • A. Chorionic plate vessels only in the uterus wall
    • B. Vitelline duct
    • C. Umbilical cord
    • D. Ductus arteriosus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Umbilical cord

    The umbilical cord contains two umbilical arteries and one umbilical vein embedded in Wharton's jelly and carries blood between fetus and placenta. The ductus arteriosus is a fetal shunt inside the fetus, between pulmonary trunk and aorta.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes

  9. If the placenta separates prematurely and ruptures its vessels (abruption), the major immediate danger is:

    • A. Hemorrhage
    • B. Fetal macrosomia
    • C. Excess amniotic fluid
    • D. Premature labor only, without bleeding
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Hemorrhage

    Placental abruption tears maternal vessels in the decidua, causing potentially severe maternal and fetal hemorrhage. Premature labor more typically follows rupture of the amniochorionic membranes, as noted in the recall, rather than placental separation itself.

    Source: Moore, The Developing Human — Placenta and Fetal Membranes
