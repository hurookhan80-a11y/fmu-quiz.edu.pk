Neck MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Anatomy

Home › Anatomy MCQs › Neck

The neck connects the head to the trunk and contains structures that pass between them. It holds the larynx, trachea, oesophagus, and thyroid gland, together with major vessels such as the carotid arteries and jugular veins. Its muscles are arranged in layers, and its nerves include branches of the cervical plexus and the cranial nerves.

  1. Axillary sheath is derived from which fascia?

    • A. Superficial fascia of neck
    • B. Investing layer of deep cervical fascia
    • C. Buccopharyngeal fascia
    • D. Clavipectoral fascia
    • E. Prevertebral fascia
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: E. Prevertebral fascia

    The axillary sheath is a fascial tube around the axillary vessels and brachial plexus derived from the prevertebral layer of deep cervical fascia extending from the neck into the axilla. Clavipectoral fascia is a separate anterior axillary wall structure.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  2. In thoracic outlet syndrome, which structures are most likely compressed?

    • A. Subclavian artery and vein together
    • B. Brachiocephalic trunk
    • C. Axillary artery and vein
    • D. Brachial plexus and subclavian artery
    • E. Radial nerve and brachial artery
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Brachial plexus and subclavian artery

    In thoracic outlet syndrome, structures passing through the scalene triangle — the brachial plexus (especially lower trunk) and subclavian artery — are compressed by a cervical rib/scalene. The subclavian vein passes anterior to scalenus anterior, outside the triangle, and is usually spared.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  3. The skin over the acromion and the scapular region is mainly supplied by which nerves?

    • A. Axillary nerve
    • B. Supraclavicular nerves
    • C. Intercostal nerves
    • D. Dorsal rami of upper thoracic nerves only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Supraclavicular nerves

    The supraclavicular nerves from the cervical plexus (C3-C4) descend over the clavicle to supply skin over the acromion and upper scapular region. The axillary nerve supplies skin over the deltoid, a more lateral area.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  4. Phonation (production of voice sounds for speech) is a function of the:

    • A. Larynx
    • B. Pharynx alone
    • C. Nasal cavity alone
    • D. Trachea
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Larynx

    Vocal folds in the larynx vibrate in the expiratory airstream to produce sound, which is then modified by the pharynx, mouth and nasal cavity. The trachea conducts air but does not generate voice.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  5. Infection in the paratracheal (pretracheal) fascial region of the neck can pass downwards into the:

    • A. Superior mediastinum
    • B. Middle ear cavity
    • C. Anterior cranial fossa
    • D. Popliteal fossa
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Superior mediastinum

    The pretracheal fascia encloses the trachea, thyroid and oesophagus and blends below with the fibrous pericardium, so infection can descend into the superior mediastinum. This is a recognised route for spread of deep neck infections.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  6. Infection deep to the prevertebral layer of cervical fascia can spread downwards mainly into the:

    • A. Superior (and posterior) mediastinum via the danger space
    • B. Orbit
    • C. Palmar spaces of the hand
    • D. Peritoneal cavity directly
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Superior (and posterior) mediastinum via the danger space

    The 'danger space' between the alar and prevertebral layers of cervical fascia extends from the skull base into the mediastinum, providing a route for deep neck infection to reach the superior/posterior mediastinum. This is a dangerous pathway for mediastinitis.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  7. Which is an accessory muscle of inspiration?

    • A. Transversus abdominis
    • B. Rectus abdominis
    • C. Internal oblique
    • D. Sternocleidomastoid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Sternocleidomastoid

    Sternocleidomastoid elevates the sternum during forced/deep inspiration and is a classic accessory inspiratory muscle. Abdominal wall muscles are mainly expiratory/accessory for forced expiration.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck

  8. The left recurrent laryngeal nerve recurs (hooks) around:

    • A. Left subclavian artery
    • B. Ligamentum arteriosum (remnant of ductus arteriosus)
    • C. Arch of aorta directly in the adult only, unrelated to ductus
    • D. Left brachiocephalic vein
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Ligamentum arteriosum (remnant of ductus arteriosus)

    The left recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks under the aortic arch at the ligamentum arteriosum, the fibrous remnant of the ductus arteriosus. On the right it hooks around the subclavian artery instead.

    Source: KLM Clinically Oriented Anatomy — Ch. 9: Neck
