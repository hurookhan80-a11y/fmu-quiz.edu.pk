Home › Physiology MCQs › Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

At the neuromuscular junction, a nerve impulse releases acetylcholine, which opens ion channels in the muscle membrane and produces an end-plate potential. That potential triggers an action potential that travels into the fibre through the transverse tubules. The signal releases calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, and calcium starts contraction.

  1. The neurotransmitter released at the skeletal neuromuscular junction is:

    • A. Norepinephrine
    • B. Acetylcholine
    • C. Glutamate
    • D. GABA
    • E. Dopamine
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Acetylcholine

    Motor neurons synthesize ACh from choline and acetyl-CoA (via choline acetyltransferase), store it in synaptic vesicles, and release it into the cleft. ACh then binds nicotinic receptors on the motor end plate. No other transmitter mediates normal voluntary skeletal muscle activation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  2. Acetylcholine is rapidly removed from the synaptic cleft by:

    • A. Choline acetyltransferase
    • B. Acetylcholinesterase
    • C. Monoamine oxidase
    • D. Catechol-O-methyltransferase
    • E. Butyryl synthetase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Acetylcholinesterase

    Acetylcholinesterase, anchored in the synaptic basal lamina, hydrolyzes ACh within ~1 ms, ending the signal crisply so each nerve impulse gives one twitch. Anticholinesterases (neostigmine) prolong ACh action to treat myasthenia; organophosphates poison the same enzyme, causing cholinergic crisis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  3. Release of acetylcholine quanta from the motor nerve terminal is triggered by:

    • A. Na+ influx into the terminal
    • B. Ca2+ influx into the presynaptic terminal
    • C. K+ efflux from the terminal
    • D. Cl- influx
    • E. Passive diffusion of vesicles
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Ca2+ influx into the presynaptic terminal

    The arriving nerve action potential opens presynaptic voltage-gated Ca2+ channels; the Ca2+ microdomain triggers SNARE-mediated vesicle fusion. About 125 vesicles (quanta) release per impulse normally. Lambert-Eaton antibodies destroy these Ca2+ channels, crippling release.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  4. The end plate potential (EPP) at the neuromuscular junction is:

    • A. A hyperpolarizing IPSP
    • B. A graded depolarization from Na+ influx through nicotinic channels
    • C. An all-or-none muscle spike
    • D. A Ca2+ wave
    • E. A K+ equilibrium potential
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. A graded depolarization from Na+ influx through nicotinic channels

    ACh opens nicotinic channels permeable to Na+ (in) and K+ (out); net Na+ entry depolarizes the end plate by ~50-75 mV (the EPP), which electrotonically triggers the muscle action potential in adjacent membrane. The EPP itself is graded, but it normally always reaches threshold — the safety factor.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  5. The 'safety factor' of neuromuscular transmission refers to:

    • A. Excess AChE activity
    • B. ACh release far exceeding what is needed to reach threshold
    • C. Multiple innervation of each fiber
    • D. Presynaptic inhibition
    • E. Postsynaptic Ca2+ spikes
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. ACh release far exceeding what is needed to reach threshold

    Each nerve impulse releases several times more ACh than needed, so the EPP overshoots threshold comfortably — transmission is highly reliable. Diseases (myasthenia) or drugs (curare) erode this margin until some impulses fail, producing fatigable weakness. The safety factor is why NMJ block must be profound before weakness appears.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  6. Curare (tubocurarine) produces flaccid paralysis by:

    • A. Depolarizing the end plate
    • B. Competitive blockade of nicotinic ACh receptors
    • C. Inhibiting ACh synthesis
    • D. Blocking presynaptic Ca2+ channels
    • E. Destroying AChE
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Competitive blockade of nicotinic ACh receptors

    Curare competes with ACh for nicotinic receptors without opening them — a nondepolarizing (competitive) blocker causing flaccid paralysis, reversible by anticholinesterases. It was the original arrow poison. Modern analogues (pancuronium, vecuronium) are used for surgical muscle relaxation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  7. Succinylcholine differs from curare in that it causes muscle paralysis by:

    • A. Competitive receptor blockade
    • B. Persistent end-plate depolarization causing initial fasciculation then paralysis
    • C. AChE inhibition
    • D. Presynaptic ACh depletion
    • E. Blocking T-tubule conduction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Persistent end-plate depolarization causing initial fasciculation then paralysis

    Succinylcholine is two joined ACh molecules: it depolarizes the end plate persistently, firing the fiber once (fasciculation) then leaving it inexcitable (phase I block) because the membrane cannot repolarize. It also triggers malignant hyperthermia in susceptible patients. Unlike curare, anticholinesterases worsen its block.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  8. Neostigmine reverses nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockade by:

    • A. Blocking nicotinic receptors
    • B. Inhibiting acetylcholinesterase, prolonging ACh action
    • C. Stimulating ACh synthesis
    • D. Opening Ca2+ channels
    • E. Desensitizing the end plate
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Inhibiting acetylcholinesterase, prolonging ACh action

    By inhibiting acetylcholinesterase, neostigmine lets each quantum of ACh linger and act repeatedly, boosting the EPP back above threshold in curare poisoning or myasthenia. Pyridostigmine is the longer-acting oral cousin used daily in myasthenia gravis. Atropine is added to block unwanted muscarinic effects.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  9. Botulinum toxin causes flaccid paralysis by:

    • A. Blocking postsynaptic receptors
    • B. Preventing presynaptic ACh release by cleaving SNARE proteins
    • C. Inhibiting AChE
    • D. Depolarizing the end plate
    • E. Blocking voltage-gated Na+ channels
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Preventing presynaptic ACh release by cleaving SNARE proteins

    Botulinum toxin is a protease that cleaves SNARE proteins, so synaptic vesicles cannot fuse — no ACh release, flaccid paralysis (descending, with autonomic features). Minute cosmetic/therapeutic doses exploit this to relax dystonic or spastic muscles. It is the most potent known toxin.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  10. Myasthenia gravis is caused by:

    • A. Presynaptic Ca2+ channel antibodies
    • B. Autoantibodies against postsynaptic nicotinic ACh receptors
    • C. Botulinum-like blockade of release
    • D. Excess acetylcholinesterase
    • E. Motor neuron degeneration
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Autoantibodies against postsynaptic nicotinic ACh receptors

    IgG autoantibodies destroy or block postsynaptic nicotinic receptors, shrinking the safety factor so repeated firing fails — hence fatigable ptosis, diplopia, dysarthria, and limb weakness worsening through the day. Thymoma is associated; thymectomy can help. Anticholinesterases and immunosuppression are mainstays of treatment.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  11. A patient with fluctuating ptosis and diplopia shows dramatic transient improvement after an intravenous drug test. The drug most likely given was:

    • A. Curare
    • B. Edrophonium (Tensilon) or pyridostigmine
    • C. Succinylcholine
    • D. Botulinum toxin
    • E. Hemicholinium
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Edrophonium (Tensilon) or pyridostigmine

    Edrophonium briefly inhibits AChE; the transient EPP boost visibly lifts the ptosis within a minute — a bedside diagnostic test. Longer-acting pyridostigmine is used for daily therapy. Care is needed because excess drug can precipitate cholinergic crisis, which mimics myasthenic crisis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  12. Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome differs from myasthenia gravis in that it involves:

    • A. Postsynaptic receptor antibodies and worsening with repetition
    • B. Presynaptic Ca2+ channel antibodies with strength improving on repeated effort, often paraneoplastic
    • C. Excess AChE activity
    • D. Identical to myasthenia in every respect
    • E. Pure sensory involvement
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Presynaptic Ca2+ channel antibodies with strength improving on repeated effort, often paraneoplastic

    Lambert-Eaton antibodies attack presynaptic P/Q Ca2+ channels, reducing quantal release; repeated stimulation builds residual Ca2+ and transiently improves strength — the opposite of myasthenia's fatigability. It is paraneoplastic, classically with small cell lung carcinoma, and also causes autonomic dryness.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  13. Miniature end plate potentials (MEPPs) recorded at a resting neuromuscular junction represent:

    • A. Full end plate potentials
    • B. Spontaneous release of single ACh vesicles (quanta)
    • C. Muscle action potentials
    • D. T-tubule Ca2+ sparks
    • E. Postsynaptic receptor turnover
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Spontaneous release of single ACh vesicles (quanta)

    Even at rest, single vesicles fuse spontaneously, producing ~0.5 mV MEPPs too small to trigger spikes. Their frequency rises with presynaptic Ca2+. MEPPs proved quantal release theory (Katz) and are reduced in Lambert-Eaton and botulism, increased by hypercalcemia.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  14. In skeletal muscle excitation-contraction coupling, the dihydropyridine (DHP) receptor functions as:

    • A. A Ca2+ release channel of the SR
    • B. A voltage sensor in the T-tubule membrane that opens ryanodine receptors
    • C. An ACh receptor subtype
    • D. The Na+/K+ pump
    • E. A K+ leak channel
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. A voltage sensor in the T-tubule membrane that opens ryanodine receptors

    The DHP receptor is an L-type Ca2+ channel homolog that barely conducts Ca2+ in skeletal muscle; its voltage-sensing S4 segments move with T-tubule depolarization and mechanically tug open the ryanodine receptor. Dihydropyridine drugs (nifedipine) block the cardiac/blood-vessel version, not this skeletal sensor function.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  15. The massive, rapid Ca2+ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum during skeletal muscle activation occurs through:

    • A. DHP receptors
    • B. Ryanodine receptors
    • C. IP3 receptors
    • D. Nicotinic receptors
    • E. SERCA pumps
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Ryanodine receptors

    Ryanodine receptors are the giant SR Ca2+ release channels, opened mechanically by DHP receptors in skeletal muscle (and by Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release in cardiac muscle). Mutations cause malignant hyperthermia and central core disease. Dantrolene treats hyperthermia by closing them.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  16. A 30-year-old woman develops ptosis and diplopia that worsen with prolonged reading and improve after rest. The most likely diagnosis is:

    • A. Lambert-Eaton syndrome
    • B. Myasthenia gravis
    • C. Botulism
    • D. Organophosphate poisoning
    • E. Muscular dystrophy
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Myasthenia gravis

    Drooping eyelids and double vision that fatigue as the day progresses reflect failing neuromuscular transmission in extraocular muscles. The Tensilon test, repetitive nerve stimulation (decremental response), and anti-AChR antibodies confirm myasthenia gravis. Ocular myasthenia may later generalize.

  17. A farmer collapses after spraying crops, with pinpoint pupils, profuse salivation and bronchial secretions, diarrhea, and muscle fasciculations. The toxidrome and its mechanism are:

    • A. Dry mouth, dilated pupils, tachycardia
    • B. SLUDGE syndrome: salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, GI upset, emesis, plus miosis and bronchospasm
    • C. Flaccid descending paralysis without autonomic signs
    • D. Pure motor weakness improving with rest
    • E. Hyperthermia and rigidity
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. SLUDGE syndrome: salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, GI upset, emesis, plus miosis and bronchospasm

    Organophosphates irreversibly inhibit AChE, so ACh floods muscarinic (SLUDGE: salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, GI cramps, emesis; plus miosis, bronchospasm, bradycardia) and nicotinic synapses. Atropine blocks muscarinic effects; pralidoxime reactivates the enzyme if given early. This is the classic pesticide-poisoning emergency.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  18. When acetylcholine opens its receptor channel at the motor end plate:

    • A. Only Na+ enters
    • B. Na+ enters and K+ leaves, with net inward depolarizing current
    • C. Only Cl- enters
    • D. Ca2+ enters exclusively
    • E. K+ enters
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Na+ enters and K+ leaves, with net inward depolarizing current

    The nicotinic ACh receptor is a nonselective cation channel: Na+ rushes in down its steep gradient while some K+ leaves, but the Na+ influx dominates, producing net inward (depolarizing) current. Two ACh molecules must bind to open the channel. This is the molecular basis of the EPP.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  19. The synaptic cleft of the neuromuscular junction characteristically contains:

    • A. Stored ACh vesicles
    • B. Membrane-bound acetylcholinesterase that terminates the signal
    • C. Voltage-gated Na+ channels
    • D. Myelin
    • E. Titin filaments
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Membrane-bound acetylcholinesterase that terminates the signal

    The ~20-30 nm cleft is packed with acetylcholinesterase tethered to collagen, which destroys ACh in about a millisecond — essential for one-to-one nerve-muscle signaling. Without it, the end plate would stay depolarized and block (as in organophosphate poisoning). The narrow gap also speeds diffusion.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  20. Which statement correctly distinguishes calcium's roles at the neuromuscular junction versus inside the muscle fiber?

    • A. Ca2+ binds troponin at the NMJ
    • B. Presynaptic Ca2+ triggers ACh release; sarcoplasmic Ca2+ triggers contraction via troponin C
    • C. Ca2+ opens nicotinic receptors
    • D. Ca2+ is not involved at the NMJ
    • E. Both use the ryanodine receptor
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Presynaptic Ca2+ triggers ACh release; sarcoplasmic Ca2+ triggers contraction via troponin C

    At the NMJ, Ca2+ acts presynaptically to trigger vesicular ACh release; inside the muscle fiber, a separate Ca2+ flood from the SR binds troponin C to start contraction. Two Ca2+ signals, two compartments, one coordinated twitch. This dual role is why Ca2+ channel blockers and ryanodine defects have such distinct phenotypes.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling

  21. At the neuromuscular junction, the small depolarisations produced spontaneously by release of single vesicles of acetylcholine are called:

    • A. Miniature end-plate potentials
    • B. End-plate potentials large enough to always trigger a muscle action potential
    • C. Inhibitory post-synaptic potentials
    • D. Generator potentials of the muscle spindle
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Miniature end-plate potentials

    Single quanta of acetylcholine produce tiny miniature end-plate potentials that summate when many vesicles are released to form the full end-plate potential. A single quantum does not by itself reach threshold, and these potentials are excitatory and localised to the motor end-plate, not spindle generator potentials.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 7: Excitation of Skeletal Muscle: Neuromuscular Transmission and Excitation-Contraction Coupling
