Cardiac Muscle MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Physiology

Home › Physiology MCQs › Cardiac Muscle

Cardiac muscle is found only in the heart. It is striated like skeletal muscle but contracts involuntarily, and its cells are linked by intercalated discs that let electrical signals spread quickly from cell to cell. This arrangement makes the atria and ventricles contract as coordinated chambers rather than as isolated fibres.

  1. The third heart sound (S3) is produced by:

    • A. Closure of the semilunar valves
    • B. Rapid blood flow into the ventricles during rapid filling
    • C. Atrial contraction against a stiff ventricle
    • D. Opening of the mitral valve
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Rapid blood flow into the ventricles during rapid filling

    S3 occurs in early diastole from rapid passive filling and vibration of the ventricular wall as blood rushes in. S4, not S3, is related to atrial contraction; valve closure produces S1 and S2.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  2. Because the semilunar valves are more taut than the AV valves:

    • A. S1 has a higher frequency than S2
    • B. S2 has a higher frequency (sharper sound) than S1
    • C. S2 lasts longer than S1
    • D. The semilunar valves produce S3
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. S2 has a higher frequency (sharper sound) than S1

    The taut semilunar valves close with a sharper, higher-frequency sound, so S2 is higher pitched than the lower, softer S1 produced by the AV valves. S1 is normally longer in duration than S2.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  3. The mitral (AV) valve opens:

    • A. During isovolumetric contraction
    • B. At the end of isovolumetric relaxation, when ventricular pressure falls below atrial pressure
    • C. At the peak of ventricular ejection
    • D. During atrial systole only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. At the end of isovolumetric relaxation, when ventricular pressure falls below atrial pressure

    The AV valves stay closed throughout isovolumetric contraction and ejection; they open when, after isovolumetric relaxation, ventricular pressure drops below atrial pressure and rapid filling begins. During isovolumetric phases all valves are closed and volume is constant.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  4. On the ventricular volume curve, ventricular volume falls during the:

    • A. Rapid filling phase
    • B. Atrial systole
    • C. Ejection phase
    • D. Isovolumetric contraction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Ejection phase

    During ejection, blood leaves the ventricles so ventricular volume steadily decreases. In isovolumetric contraction all valves are closed, so volume does not change.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  5. Normal end-diastolic volume of a ventricle is approximately:

    • A. 40 to 50 mL
    • B. 70 mL
    • C. 110 to 120 mL
    • D. 250 mL
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 110 to 120 mL

    End-diastolic volume is about 110-120 mL; after ejection about 40-50 mL remains as end-systolic volume, giving a stroke volume near 70 mL. The note's 'millimetres' is a typo for millilitres.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  6. The plateau phase (phase 2) of the ventricular muscle action potential is mainly due to:

    • A. Rapid Na+ influx
    • B. Opening of slow Ca++ (Ca++-Na+) channels with decreased K+ efflux
    • C. Massive Cl- influx
    • D. Opening of fast K+ channels only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Opening of slow Ca++ (Ca++-Na+) channels with decreased K+ efflux

    Guyton describes slow calcium-sodium channels opening in the plateau, keeping the membrane depolarised while K+ permeability is reduced. Rapid Na+ influx causes the phase 0 upstroke, not the plateau.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  7. The absolute refractory period of ventricular cardiac muscle lasts approximately:

    • A. Only during the upstroke (1 ms)
    • B. Through the plateau, until about the middle of contraction (roughly 0.25-0.30 s)
    • C. For the whole of diastole
    • D. Until the next T wave ends in all cases
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Through the plateau, until about the middle of contraction (roughly 0.25-0.30 s)

    The long absolute refractory period of ventricular muscle extends through the plateau until about half of contraction is complete, preventing tetanus and premature re-excitation. Skeletal muscle, by contrast, has a very short refractory period.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle

  8. Approximately how much of ventricular filling has already occurred before the atria contract?

    • A. 20%
    • B. 50%
    • C. 80%
    • D. 100%
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 80%

    About 80% of ventricular filling is passive during rapid and slow filling (diastasis); atrial contraction adds only the final ~20%. This is why atrial fibrillation is tolerated at rest but matters at high heart rates.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle
