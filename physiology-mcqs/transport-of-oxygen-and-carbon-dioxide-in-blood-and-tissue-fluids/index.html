Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Physiology

Home › Physiology MCQs › Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

Oxygen is carried in the blood mainly bound to haemoglobin inside red blood cells, with only a small amount dissolved in plasma. Carbon dioxide is carried mainly as bicarbonate in the plasma, with smaller amounts bound to haemoglobin and dissolved. In the tissues the gases move by diffusion down their partial pressure gradients.

  1. A 25 year old athlete is running a 400-meter sprint. In his actively contracting muscles, hemoglobin releases more oxygen than at rest. Which mechanism best explains this phenomenon?

    • A. Increased CO2 and H+ lower hemoglobin's affinity for O2
    • B. Decreased temperature in exercising muscle facilitates O2 unloading
    • C. Oxygenation of hemoglobin reduces its capacity to bind CO2
    • D. Reduced 2,3-BPG in RBCs enhances oxygen delivery to tissues
    • E. Alkalosis in tissues shifts the O-Hb dissociation curve to the right
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Increased CO2 and H+ lower hemoglobin's affinity for O2

    Exercising muscle produces CO2, H+, and heat, all of which reduce haemoglobin's affinity for O2 and shift the oxyhaemoglobin curve to the right (Bohr effect), promoting O2 unloading where it is most needed. Decreased temperature would shift the curve left, alkalosis would also shift it left, and reduced 2,3-BPG would increase affinity and impair unloading. Oxygenation itself reduces CO2 binding (Haldane effect), which is a different phenomenon.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  2. A 25 year old athlete running a marathon shows increased CO2 production. Which phenomenon best explains the greater amount of CO2 carried from tissues to lungs for a given PCO2?

    • A. Chloride shift
    • B. Haldane effect
    • C. Bohr effect
    • D. Increased oxygen affinity of hemoglobin
    • E. Reduced carbamino formation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Haldane effect

    The Haldane effect states that deoxygenated haemoglobin binds more CO2 (as carbamino compounds and by buffering H+) than oxygenated haemoglobin. Thus as O2 is unloaded in tissues, CO2 loading is favoured for the same PCO2, and in the lungs oxygenation promotes CO2 release. The Bohr effect is the reciprocal influence of CO2/H+ on O2 affinity, while the chloride shift maintains electrical neutrality during bicarbonate transport.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  3. A 45-year-old man with COPD retains CO2. Which mechanism carries the largest fraction of CO2 in blood?

    • A. Dissolved in plasma
    • B. Carbaminohemoglobin (CO2 bound to Hb)
    • C. As bicarbonate ion in plasma
    • D. As carbonic acid
    • E. As carbamino compounds with plasma proteins
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. As bicarbonate ion in plasma

    About 70% of CO2 transported in blood is as bicarbonate ion in plasma: CO2 enters red cells, carbonic anhydrase forms carbonic acid which dissociates to H+ and HCO3-, and HCO3- exits to plasma in exchange for Cl- (chloride shift). Only about 23% travels as carbaminohaemoglobin and about 7% dissolved. Carbonic acid itself is a transient intermediate, not a transport form.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  4. When venous blood passes through systemic capillaries, which event best explains the chloride shift

    • A. Entry of Cl- into RBCs in exchange for HCO3- moving out
    • B. Efflux of Cl- from RBCs to maintain osmotic equilibrium
    • C. Influx of HCO3- into RBCs to buffer rising CO2
    • D. Outward diffusion of H ions with inward movement of Cl-
    • E. Active transport of Cl- against its electrochemical gradient
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Entry of Cl- into RBCs in exchange for HCO3- moving out

    In systemic capillaries CO2 enters red cells and is converted by carbonic anhydrase to H+ and HCO3-. HCO3- leaves the cell to plasma down its gradient in exchange for Cl- entry via the band 3 exchanger (chloride or Hamburger shift), maintaining electroneutrality. In the lungs the shift reverses. Cl- therefore enters red cells in tissues, not leaves them.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  5. Which statement best describes the Haldane effect in blood gas transport?

    • A. Oxygenation of hemoglobin decreases its capacity to carry CO2
    • B. Deoxygenated hemoglobin carries less CO2 than oxygenated hemoglobin
    • C. Oxygenation of hemoglobin increases formation of carbamino compounds
    • D. Deoxygenated hemoglobin has reduced buffering capacity for H ions
    • E. Oxygenation of hemoglobin promotes CO2 binding at tissue level
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Oxygenation of hemoglobin decreases its capacity to carry CO2

    The Haldane effect is that oxygenation of haemoglobin in the lungs reduces its capacity to carry CO2 (both carbamino binding falls and H+ buffering decreases), promoting CO2 unloading. Conversely deoxyhaemoglobin in tissues carries more CO2. This is correctly stated as oxygenation decreasing CO2 capacity; it is distinct from the Bohr effect on O2 affinity.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  6. Which cells are primarily responsible for bicarbonate production in the body for CO2 transport?

    • A. Red blood cells
    • B. Alveolar macrophages
    • C. Hepatocytes
    • D. Platelets
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Red blood cells

    Carbonic anhydrase inside red blood cells rapidly converts CO2 and water to carbonic acid, which dissociates to H+ and bicarbonate. Most CO2 is therefore carried in plasma as bicarbonate (the chloride shift maintains neutrality).

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  7. Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning affects hemoglobin by:

    • A. Increasing O2 affinity of hemoglobin (left shift) while reducing O2-carrying capacity
    • B. Decreasing O2 affinity only, with no effect on capacity
    • C. Converting all hemoglobin to methemoglobin
    • D. Increasing O2-carrying capacity above normal
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Increasing O2 affinity of hemoglobin (left shift) while reducing O2-carrying capacity

    CO binds hemoglobin with ~240 times the affinity of O2, forming carboxyhemoglobin, reducing carrying capacity and shifting the dissociation curve left so tissues cannot unload O2. Guyton emphasizes this impaired release as the tissue-toxic effect, correcting the loose recall wording.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  8. Which condition causes a right shift of the hemoglobin-oxygen dissociation curve (favoring O2 release)?

    • A. Alkalosis
    • B. Exercise (increased CO2, H+, temperature and 2,3-BPG in tissues)
    • C. Hypothermia
    • D. Carbon monoxide poisoning
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Exercise (increased CO2, H+, temperature and 2,3-BPG in tissues)

    Exercise raises local CO2, hydrogen ions, temperature and 2,3-BPG, shifting the curve right (Bohr effect) to unload more O2 to active muscle. Alkalosis and hypothermia shift the curve left.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  9. Cyanosis (bluish discoloration) appears when there is an increased amount of:

    • A. Deoxygenated (reduced) hemoglobin in capillary blood
    • B. Oxygenated hemoglobin
    • C. Carboxyhemoglobin cherry-red pigment
    • D. Bilirubin
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Deoxygenated (reduced) hemoglobin in capillary blood

    Cyanosis becomes visible when reduced (deoxygenated) hemoglobin exceeds about 5 g/dL in capillary blood. Carboxyhemoglobin gives a cherry-red color, and bilirubin causes yellow jaundice, not cyanosis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  10. Most carbon dioxide (CO2) is transported in blood in which form?

    • A. Bicarbonate (HCO3-) ions in plasma
    • B. Dissolved CO2 only
    • C. Carbamino compounds only
    • D. Carbonic acid stored in RBCs only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Bicarbonate (HCO3-) ions in plasma

    About 70% of CO2 is carried as bicarbonate formed in RBCs by carbonic anhydrase and exchanged for chloride. Dissolved CO2 (~7%) and carbamino compounds (~23%) carry smaller fractions.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids

  11. Most oxygen transported in arterial blood is carried:

    • A. Dissolved freely in plasma
    • B. Bound to albumin as a protein complex
    • C. As bicarbonate ions in plasma
    • D. Bound to haemoglobin inside red blood cells
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Bound to haemoglobin inside red blood cells

    About 97% of oxygen is carried reversibly bound to haemoglobin, which raises blood oxygen capacity far above what plasma can dissolve (only about 3% is dissolved, though dissolved oxygen determines PO2). Bicarbonate is the main form for carbon dioxide transport, not oxygen, and albumin is not a significant oxygen carrier.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood and Tissue Fluids
