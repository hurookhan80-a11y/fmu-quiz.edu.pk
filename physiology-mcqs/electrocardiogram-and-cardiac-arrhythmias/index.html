The electrocardiogram is a recording of the heart's electrical activity from the body surface, shown as a series of waves. Normal rhythm begins in the sinoatrial node and spreads in an orderly way through the heart. An arrhythmia is any disturbance of this pattern, seen as an abnormal rate, rhythm, or wave shape.

  1. A 22-year-old man presents with recurrent episodes of sudden-onset palpitations that terminate spontaneously. During sinus rhythm, his ECG demonstrates a short PR interval, a delta wave, and a widened QRS complex. Which interpretation best explains both the ECG findings and the patient's clinical presentation?

    • A. Delayed conduction through the AV node causes physiological shortening of ventricular depolarization.
    • B. An accessory atrioventricular pathway bypasses the AV node, predisposing the patient to re-entrant tachyarrhythmias.
    • C. Complete atrioventricular block results in premature ventricular activation through the His–Purkinje system.
    • D. Increased vagal tone accelerates AV nodal conduction, producing ventricular pre-excitation.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. An accessory atrioventricular pathway bypasses the AV node, predisposing the patient to re-entrant tachyarrhythmias.

    A short PR interval with a slurred upstroke (delta wave) and wide QRS in sinus rhythm with paroxysmal palpitations is Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome. An accessory AV pathway (bundle of Kent) bypasses the AV nodal delay, pre-exciting the ventricles and providing a limb for re-entrant tachycardia. AV nodal delay shortening or complete heart block cannot produce a delta wave, and increased vagal tone would slow, not bypass, AV conduction.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  2. A 24-year-old healthy medical student experiences occasional palpitations during examination week. ECG demonstrates isolated premature broad QRS complexes without preceding P waves, followed by a full compensatory pause. Physical examination and echocardiography are normal. Which is the most appropriate interpretation?

    • A. Benign physiological ventricular ectopic beats precipitated by increased sympathetic activity
    • B. Ventricular tachycardia requiring immediate defibrillation
    • C. Acute myocardial infarction with ventricular irritability
    • D. Complete atrioventricular block with ventricular escape rhythm
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Benign physiological ventricular ectopic beats precipitated by increased sympathetic activity

    Isolated premature wide QRS complexes without a preceding P wave followed by a full compensatory pause are premature ventricular contractions (PVCs). In a young healthy person with a normal heart and triggers such as examination stress, caffeine, or sympathetic drive, occasional PVCs are benign. There is no sustained ventricular tachycardia, infarction, or complete heart block pattern.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  3. A 55-year-old man presents with sudden-onset palpitations. ECG shows a regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 180 beats/min. Electrophysiological study confirms a re-entry circuit. Which condition is essential for maintenance of a re-entry arrhythmia?

    • A. Uniform conduction velocity in all pathways
    • B. Two pathways with different conduction velocities and refractory periods
    • C. Simultaneous activation of both ventricles
    • D. Complete atrioventricular block
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Two pathways with different conduction velocities and refractory periods

    Maintenance of re-entry (circus movement) requires two pathways with different conduction velocities and refractory periods plus an area of unidirectional block, so the impulse repeatedly re-excites tissue that has recovered. Uniform conduction, simultaneous biventricular activation, or complete AV block cannot sustain a circus circuit. This is the mechanism of most paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardias in Guyton's account.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  4. A 58-year-old man develops retrosternal chest discomfort while climbing stairs. His ECG demonstrates 1 mm horizontal ST-segment depression in leads V4–V6, which returns to baseline after rest. Cardiac enzymes are normal. Which conclusion is most appropriate regarding his ECG findings?

    • A. The ECG changes most likely represent a benign physiological variant requiring no further evaluation.
    • B. The findings support subendocardial myocardial ischaemia and warrant further assessment for coronary artery disease.
    • C. The ECG is diagnostic of complete atrioventricular block because ST depression indicates impaired conduction.
    • D. The ECG findings confirm atrial fibrillation as the cause of exertional chest pain.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The findings support subendocardial myocardial ischaemia and warrant further assessment for coronary artery disease.

    Exertional retrosternal pain with transient 1 mm horizontal ST depression in V4-V6 that normalises with rest and normal cardiac enzymes is classic stable angina due to subendocardial ischaemia. The subendocardium is most vulnerable to ischaemia, and reversible ST depression demands assessment for coronary artery disease. It is not a benign variant needing no evaluation, nor does ST depression indicate AV block or atrial fibrillation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  5. A 58-year-old man presents with severe central chest pain radiating to his left arm. His ECG is suspected to show an acute anterior wall myocardial infarction. Which ECG leads are most likely to demonstrate ST-segment elevation?

    • A. II, III, aVF
    • B. I, aVL, V5, V6
    • C. V1–V4
    • D. aVR and V1
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. V1–V4

    The precordial leads V1-V4 overlie the anterior wall of the left ventricle supplied by the left anterior descending artery, so anterior myocardial infarction shows ST elevation there. Leads II, III, and aVF view the inferior wall, and I, aVL, V5, V6 view the lateral wall. Lead aVR is rarely diagnostic for anterior infarction.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  6. A 60-year-old man presents with severe crushing chest pain for 2 hours. His ECG shows ST-segment elevation in leads V2–V4. According to the current of injury concept, the ST-segment elevation occurs because:

    • A. Ischemic myocardial cells repolarize more rapidly than normal cells.
    • B. Partial continuous depolarization of ischemic myocardium creates an injury current.
    • C. Ventricular hypertrophy increases the amplitude of the QRS complex.
    • D. Increased sympathetic activity accelerates ventricular repolarization.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Partial continuous depolarization of ischemic myocardium creates an injury current.

    Ischaemic myocardium cannot maintain its resting membrane potential and remains partly depolarised during diastole. A current of injury therefore flows between injured and normal tissue, shifting the electrical baseline so that the ST segment appears elevated in leads overlying the injury during the TP segment correction. Rapid repolarisation, hypertrophy, or sympathetic effects do not explain localised ST elevation by this mechanism.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  7. A 62-year-old man with a previous myocardial infarction has recurrent episodes of sustained ventricular tachycardia. An implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) is inserted to prevent sudden cardiac death. The ICD prevents sudden cardiac death primarily by:

    • A. Increasing coronary blood flow through coronary vasodilation
    • B. Delivering an electrical shock to restore normal sinus rhythm
    • C. Blocking sympathetic stimulation of the myocardium
    • D. Increasing myocardial contractility by continuous ventricular pacing
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Delivering an electrical shock to restore normal sinus rhythm

    An implantable cardioverter-defibrillator continuously monitors rhythm and, on detecting sustained ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation, delivers a strong internal electrical shock that depolarises the myocardium simultaneously and allows the SA node to resume control. It does not act by vasodilation, sympathetic blockade, or continuous pacing to increase contractility, although some devices can pace.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  8. A 64-year-old man with end-stage chronic kidney disease presents with generalized weakness and palpitations. His ECG demonstrates diffuse tall, narrow, symmetrical T waves with mild ST-segment elevation. Which is the most likely diagnosis?

    • A. Hyperkalemia syndrome secondary to advanced renal failure
    • B. Hyperacute ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)
    • C. Acute uremic pericarditis
    • D. Benign early repolarization syndrome
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Hyperkalemia syndrome secondary to advanced renal failure

    Diffuse tall, narrow, symmetrically peaked T waves in a patient with end-stage renal disease and weakness are the classic ECG manifestation of hyperkalaemia. Raised extracellular K+ accelerates ventricular repolarisation and, if untreated, can progress to widened QRS, loss of P waves, and sine-wave arrest. This is not STEMI (no localised ST elevation pattern), uraemic pericarditis (which shows diffuse ST elevation with PR depression), or benign early repolarisation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  9. A 68-year-old man presents with palpitations and dizziness. His ECG rhythm strip records 12 QRS complexes over 10 seconds, with markedly irregular R–R intervals. Two students estimate the heart rate using the 300 rule and obtain different values. Which approach should the consultant recommend as the most reliable estimate of the patient's heart rate?

    • A. Use the 300 rule because it provides the most accurate estimate regardless of rhythm regularity.
    • B. Count the number of QRS complexes in the 10-second rhythm strip and multiply by 6, as this method is most appropriate for irregular rhythms.
    • C. Calculate heart rate using 1500 divided by the number of small squares, because irregularity does not affect this method.
    • D. Count the number of P waves over 10 seconds and multiply by 6 to estimate ventricular rate.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Count the number of QRS complexes in the 10-second rhythm strip and multiply by 6, as this method is most appropriate for irregular rhythms.

    When rhythm is irregular the 300/1500 large- and small-square methods, which assume a constant R-R interval, are invalid. The reliable method is to count QRS complexes in a known time (e.g. 10 seconds, 50 large squares at 25 mm/s) and multiply appropriately (by 6 for 10 s) to obtain average rate. Guyton-based teaching stresses this for atrial fibrillation and other irregular rhythms.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  10. A 68-year-old man presents with recurrent presyncope. A doctor reviews his ECG and reports Mobitz type II atrioventricular block. On consultant review, the ECG demonstrates progressive prolongation of the PR interval before a non-conducted P wave, followed by the shortest PR interval after the dropped beat. Which finding most convincingly refutes the first doctor's diagnosis?

    • A. The PR interval progressively lengthens before the dropped QRS complex.
    • B. The ventricular rate is slower than the atrial rate.
    • C. The QRS complexes are narrow throughout the tracing.
    • D. The rhythm demonstrates intermittent non-conducted P waves.
    • E. The atrial rhythm remains regular despite dropped ventricular beats.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. The PR interval progressively lengthens before the dropped QRS complex.

    The description given is actually Mobitz type I (Wenckebach), not Mobitz type II. In Wenckebach the PR interval progressively lengthens until a QRS is dropped, and the cycle repeats with the shortest PR after the pause. True Mobitz type II shows a constant PR interval in conducted beats with sudden, unpredictable non-conducted P waves, usually due to infranodal (His-Purkinje) disease and more likely to progress to complete block.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  11. A 72-year-old man presents with recurrent dizziness and near-syncope. His ECG demonstrates regular P waves with a constant PR interval. After every two conducted beats, one P wave is not followed by a QRS complex (3:2 conduction). Which is the most appropriate diagnosis?

    • A. First-degree atrioventricular (AV) block
    • B. Second-degree AV block, Mobitz type I (Wenckebach)
    • C. Second-degree AV block, Mobitz type II
    • D. Complete (third-degree) AV block
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Second-degree AV block, Mobitz type II

    Sudden failure of a P wave to conduct with an unchanged, constant PR interval in all conducted beats defines second-degree AV block, Mobitz type II. Wenckebach (Mobitz I) shows progressive PR prolongation before the dropped beat. Mobitz II usually reflects His-Purkinje disease, carries a high risk of complete block and syncope, and often needs pacing.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  12. A 74-year-old man presents with palpitations. An emergency physician diagnoses atrial fibrillation based on an irregular rhythm. During the cardiology review, the ECG is re-examined. Which ECG finding would most strongly invalidate the diagnosis of atrial fibrillation and require reconsideration of the rhythm diagnosis?

    • A. Irregularly irregular R–R intervals throughout the tracing
    • B. Narrow QRS complexes with a ventricular rate of 120 beats/min
    • C. A uniform P wave consistently preceding every QRS complex with a fixed PR interval
    • D. Absence of discrete P waves with a fibrillatory baseline
    • E. Beat-to-beat variation in ventricular cycle length
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. A uniform P wave consistently preceding every QRS complex with a fixed PR interval

    Atrial fibrillation is characterised by an irregularly irregular rhythm with absent discrete P waves and chaotic fibrillatory baseline activity. A uniform P wave preceding every QRS with a fixed PR interval would instead indicate sinus rhythm or a regular supraventricular rhythm and is incompatible with AF. Guyton explains AF as multiple chaotic re-entrant wavelets in the atria with irregular AV conduction.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  13. A pharmacological agent markedly shortens the ventricular refractory period. Which clinical consequence is most likely?

    • A. Reduced risk of ventricular arrhythmias
    • B. Increased susceptibility to re-entry tachyarrhythmias
    • C. Improved ventricular filling
    • D. Increased cardiac efficiency
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Increased susceptibility to re-entry tachyarrhythmias

    The normally long ventricular refractory period (250-300 ms) prevents re-entry and tetanic contraction. Markedly shortening it allows an impulse to re-enter and re-excite tissue that has already recovered, predisposing to circus-movement re-entrant tachyarrhythmias and fibrillation. A longer refractory period would be protective, not pro-arrhythmic.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias

  14. Which limb lead represents the main electrical axis of the heart?

    • A. Lead I
    • B. Lead II
    • C. Lead III
    • D. Lead aVR
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Lead II

    The normal mean QRS electrical axis is about +59 to +60 degrees, which lies closest to the axis of lead II (+60 degrees). Lead II therefore normally shows the largest positive QRS deflection and is the standard rhythm-monitoring lead. It represents the main axis better than leads I (0 degrees) or III (+120 degrees).

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Electrocardiogram and Cardiac Arrhythmias
