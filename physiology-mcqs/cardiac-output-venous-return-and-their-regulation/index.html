Home › Physiology MCQs › Cardiac Output, Venous Return, and Their Regulation

Cardiac output is the volume of blood the heart pumps each minute, equal to the heart rate multiplied by the stroke volume. Venous return is the volume of blood returning to the heart each minute, and in a steady state it equals cardiac output. The body regulates both, mainly through heart rate, contractility, and the filling pressure of the heart.

  1. A 45-year-old man receives an injection norepinephrine. His mean arterial pressure rises, but his cardiac output falls below normal within minutes. Which mechanism best explains the fall in cardiac output?

    • A. Decreased venous return due to increased afterload
    • B. Increased stroke volume due to higher arterial pressure
    • C. Increased venous compliance leading to blood pooling
    • D. Decreased heart rate due to sympathetic activation
    • E. Increased contractility due to higher peripheral resistance
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Decreased venous return due to increased afterload

    Norepinephrine is a powerful alpha-1 vasoconstrictor that markedly raises total peripheral resistance and afterload. The left ventricle must eject against much higher pressure, and with reflex vagal bradycardia, stroke volume and cardiac output can actually fall despite higher arterial pressure. Venous return is not primarily reduced by pooling, and sympathetic activation would increase, not decrease, contractility and heart rate.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 20: Cardiac Output, Venous Return, and Their Regulation

  2. A 52-year-old man with morbid obesity undergoes haemodynamic assessment. His cardiac output is 6.0 L/min, which is within the normal range. However, his cardiac index is below the reference range. Which interpretation best explains these findings?

    • A. Reduced stroke volume despite a normal cardiac output
    • B. Increased body surface area lowers the cardiac index despite a normal cardiac output
    • C. Decreased myocardial contractility despite preserved cardiac output
    • D. Increased systemic vascular resistance reduces the cardiac index independently of body size
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Increased body surface area lowers the cardiac index despite a normal cardiac output

    Cardiac index is cardiac output divided by body surface area (normal about 3.2 L/min/m2). In morbid obesity surface area is greatly increased, so even a normal absolute output of 6.0 L/min yields a low index. Stroke volume, contractility, and systemic resistance are not implied to be abnormal by these numbers alone; the finding is a size-normalisation effect described by Guyton.

  3. A 72-year-old man suddenly loses consciousness for 20 seconds while walking. He recovers spontaneously without confusion. His pulse is 30 beats/min, and the ECG shows complete atrioventricular (AV) block. What is the most likely cause of his transient loss of consciousness?

    • A. Reduced coronary blood flow due to coronary artery spasm
    • B. Transient cerebral ischemia caused by a sudden fall in cardiac output due to complete AV block
    • C. Increased intracranial pressure due to cerebral edema
    • D. Reflex vasodilation resulting from baroreceptor stimulation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Transient cerebral ischemia caused by a sudden fall in cardiac output due to complete AV block

    Sudden transient loss of consciousness with rapid spontaneous recovery while walking, due to abrupt fall in cardiac output and cerebral perfusion, is a Stokes-Adams attack, classically in complete heart block when the idioventricular rate (about 30/min) is too slow or pauses occur. Cerebral ischaemia, not primary cerebral disease, causes the syncope until an escape rhythm or pacing restores output.

  4. A healthy 24-year-old athlete completes 30 minutes of brisk walking. His blood volume, myocardial contractility, and heart rate remain essentially unchanged, yet his cardiac output increases. New equilibrium point on the cardiac output–venous return curves is explained by:

    • A. Enhanced skeletal muscle pump increases venous return, shifting the operating point to a higher cardiac output without altering blood volume.
    • B. Increased blood volume shifts the venous return curve to the right, producing the higher cardiac output.
    • C. Decreased venous tone lowers mean systemic filling pressure, thereby increasing venous return.
    • D. Closure of venous valves increases venous resistance, causing a rise in cardiac output.
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Enhanced skeletal muscle pump increases venous return, shifting the operating point to a higher cardiac output without altering blood volume.

    Rhythmic contraction of skeletal muscles during walking compresses deep veins; with competent venous valves blood is milked toward the heart (muscle pump), increasing venous return and shifting the operating point to higher cardiac output without change in blood volume or contractility. This is a Guyton venous return mechanism distinct from sympathetic vasoconstriction or respiratory pump effects alone.

  5. A healthy athlete suddenly shifts from lying down to standing. Which change is the most immediate?

    • A. Increased venous return
    • B. Decreased cardiac output
    • C. Increased stroke volume
    • D. Increased right atrial pressure
    • E. Immediate rise in left ventricular end-diastolic volume
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Decreased cardiac output

    On suddenly standing, gravity pools 500-700 mL of blood in leg veins, venous return falls transiently, and stroke volume and cardiac output decrease before baroreflex compensation. Cerebral perfusion may dip causing light-headedness. Heart rate and sympathetic activity rise secondarily, but the immediate change is reduced, not increased, cardiac output.

  6. After myocardial infarction with reduced cardiac output, cardiac output is restored toward normal primarily by:

    • A. Heart pumping becomes normal in 24 hours
    • B. Sympathetic system increases cardiac output permanently
    • C. Kidney conserves fluid
    • D. Renal increase in fluid excretion
    • E. Parasympathetic system decreases cardiac output
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Kidney conserves fluid

    After myocardial infarction with reduced cardiac output, the kidneys sense reduced perfusion and conserve salt and water (reduced excretion, RAAS activation), expanding blood volume and mean systemic filling pressure. By Guyton's analysis this raises venous return and restores cardiac output toward normal along the Frank-Starling curve. Immediate sympathetic vasoconstriction helps pressure but sustained restoration is renal volume retention.

  7. In an experiment, sympathetic stimulation of the heart increases cardiac output. Which mechanism contributes most?

    • A. Decreased preload
    • B. Increased afterload
    • C. Increased heart rate and contractility
    • D. Decreased venous return
    • E. Improved efficiency despite reduced oxygen delivery
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Increased heart rate and contractility

    Sympathetic stimulation increases cardiac output by positive chronotropy (faster SA nodal firing) and positive inotropy (stronger contraction via beta-1 receptors), raising both heart rate and stroke volume. Increased venous return contributes via venoconstriction, but the direct cardiac mechanisms stated are rate and contractility. Afterload increase alone would tend to reduce output.

  8. Mean circulatory filling pressure (MCFP) equals 7 mmHg when blood flow stops. How does Mean systemic filling pressure (MSFP) differ Mean circulatory filling pressure?

    • B. Slightly higher, measured in systemic circulation
    • C. Always lower due to pulmonary compliance
    • D. Only reflects venous return slope
    • E. Present only when arterial pressure is zero
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Slightly higher, measured in systemic circulation

    Mean circulatory filling pressure is the pressure in the entire circulation when flow stops and pressures equilibrate (about 7 mmHg). Mean systemic filling pressure, measured for the systemic circulation alone, is slightly higher because the pulmonary circulation (more compliant, lower pressure) is excluded. The two are related Guyton concepts for analysing venous return.

  9. When heart pumping ability falls below about 40% of normal, fluid retention becomes harmful mainly because:

    • A. Increased venous return exceeds the Frank-Starling limit
    • B. Kidney excretes too much sodium and water
    • C. Increased blood volume decreases preload
    • D. Heart can pump excess blood efficiently
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Increased venous return exceeds the Frank-Starling limit

    When cardiac pumping falls below about 40% of normal, renal fluid retention expands volume to raise venous return, but beyond the plateau (limit) of the Frank-Starling (cardiac output) curve extra volume cannot increase output. It only raises right atrial and venous pressures, worsening oedema and congestion without benefit. Retention is helpful only while the heart can still respond to increased filling.

  12. Stroke volume is increased when venous compliance decreases because:

    • A. Decreased compliance always reduces venous return
    • B. It directly blocks aortic outflow
    • C. Decreased venous compliance increases venous return (and end-diastolic volume)
    • D. It stops atrial contraction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Decreased venous compliance increases venous return (and end-diastolic volume)

  14. Venous return to the heart is increased by:

    • B. Decreased skeletal muscle pump activity
    • C. Increased venous compliance
    • D. Venodilatation with pooling of blood
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Constriction of veins (venoconstriction)

  17. Stroke volume is increased by:

    • A. Decreased venous tone
    • B. Decreased end-diastolic volume

    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Increased venous tone and venous return

  19. Cardiac index (cardiac output per square meter of body surface area) is used instead of raw cardiac output because:

    • A. It is easier to measure
    • B. It eliminates the effect of body size, allowing comparison between individuals
    • C. It measures contractility directly
    • D. It is independent of heart rate
    • E. It predicts blood pressure
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. It eliminates the effect of body size, allowing comparison between individuals

    A 50-kg woman and a 100-kg man have very different absolute cardiac outputs but similar tissue perfusion needs per unit mass.   20. The Frank-Starling law of the heart states that:

    • A. Heart rate increases with exercise
    • B. Contractility is independent of preload
    • C. Increased end-diastolic volume (stretch) increases the force of contraction and stroke volume
    • D. Afterload determines heart rate
    • E. The right heart pumps more than the left
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Increased end-diastolic volume (stretch) increases the force of contraction and stroke volume

    Stretching cardiac muscle fibers toward their optimal length increases actin-myosin overlap and calcium sensitivity, so a fuller ventricle contracts more forcefully and ejects the extra volume. This intrinsic mechanism automatically matches left and right ventricular outputs and lets the heart handle increased venous return without neural input.

  21. Venous return to the heart is determined by the pressure gradient between the mean systemic filling pressure and the right atrial pressure, divided by the resistance to venous return. If mean systemic filling pressure is 7 mmHg, right atrial pressure is 2 mmHg, and resistance is 1 mmHg.min/L, venous return is:

    • A. 9 L/min
    • B. 5 L/min
    • C. 3.5 L/min
    • D. 7 L/min
    • E. 2 L/min
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. 5 L/min

    Venous return = (MSFP - RAP) / RVR = (7 - 2) / 1 = 5 L/min, equal to resting cardiac output (in steady state, venous return must equal cardiac output). Raising MSFP (transfusion, venoconstriction) or lowering RAP increases return; raising RAP (as in heart failure) reduces the gradient and impedes return.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 20: Cardiac Output, Venous Return, and Their Regulation

  22. Mean systemic filling pressure (normally about 7 mmHg) is increased by:

    • A. Blood transfusion and sympathetic venoconstriction
    • B. Hemorrhage
    • C. Venodilation
    • D. Decreased blood volume
    • E. Right heart failure
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Blood transfusion and sympathetic venoconstriction

    MSFP reflects the fullness of the circulation: adding volume (transfusion) or constricting the venous reservoir (sympathetic venoconstriction) raises it, increasing the driving force for venous return. Hemorrhage and venodilation lower MSFP, reducing venous return — the fundamental circulatory defect in hypovolemic shock.

  23. On the Guyton cardiac output curve, when right atrial pressure rises above about 4 mmHg, further increases in atrial pressure produce little additional cardiac output because:

    • A. The heart stops beating
    • B. Venous return becomes zero
    • C. The ventricle is already maximally stretched and the Frank-Starling mechanism plateaus
    • D. Arterial pressure falls to zero
    • E. The pericardium ruptures
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. The ventricle is already maximally stretched and the Frank-Starling mechanism plateaus

    The normal cardiac output curve plateaus at high filling pressures: once sarcomeres are stretched to optimal length, extra filling yields diminishing additional stroke volume. In heart failure the curve is flattened and depressed, so the same filling pressure produces far less output — the graphical signature of a failing pump.

  24. Maximal sympathetic stimulation shifts the cardiac output curve:

    • A. Downward and rightward
    • B. It has no effect on the curve
    • C. Leftward only
    • D. Upward, so the heart pumps more at any given right atrial pressure
    • E. It flattens the venous return curve
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Upward, so the heart pumps more at any given right atrial pressure

    Sympathetic activation (norepinephrine, epinephrine) increases both heart rate and contractility, shifting the entire cardiac output curve upward: at any filling pressure the heart ejects more. Parasympathetic stimulation does the reverse. These curve shifts, combined with venous return curves, determine the operating point of the circulation.

  28. In cardiac tamponade, fluid in the pericardial sac compresses the heart. The characteristic hemodynamic finding is:

    • A. Low right atrial pressure
    • B. Equalization of diastolic pressures (RAP = RVDP = LVDP) with reduced cardiac output
    • C. Increased pulse pressure
    • D. High cardiac output
    • E. Isolated left atrial hypertension
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Equalization of diastolic pressures (RAP = RVDP = LVDP) with reduced cardiac output

    The rigid pericardial fluid compresses all chambers equally, so diastolic pressures equalize across the heart while filling — and therefore stroke volume and cardiac output — fall. The compensatory tachycardia cannot overcome the restricted filling, producing hypotension with distended neck veins: Beck's triad.

  29. At extremely high heart rates (e.g., 220 beats/min), cardiac output may paradoxically fall because:

    • A. Contractility decreases
    • B. Blood pressure rises too high
    • C. Diastole shortens so much that ventricular filling time becomes inadequate
    • D. The AV node blocks all impulses
    • E. Venous return increases excessively
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Diastole shortens so much that ventricular filling time becomes inadequate

    Cardiac output is rate x stroke volume, but at very high rates diastole — when filling occurs — is abbreviated disproportionately. Stroke volume collapses from inadequate filling, and the product falls despite the rapid rate. This is why rapid tachyarrhythmias cause hypotension and syncope.

  31. During normal inspiration, venous return to the right heart transiently increases because:

    • A. The heart beats faster
    • B. Arterial pressure rises
    • C. Intrathoracic pressure falls, expanding thoracic veins and lowering right atrial pressure
    • D. The diaphragm compresses the vena cava
    • E. Sympathetic tone increases
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Intrathoracic pressure falls, expanding thoracic veins and lowering right atrial pressure

    Inspiration lowers intrathoracic (and thus right atrial) pressure while raising abdominal pressure, steepening the pressure gradient driving blood centrally — the respiratory pump.   32. During walking, venous return from the legs is augmented by the skeletal muscle pump. This mechanism requires:

    • A. Arterial pulsation only
    • B. Rhythmic muscle contraction compressing veins equipped with one-way valves
    • C. Deep breathing only
    • D. Sympathetic activation
    • E. Increased cardiac contractility
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Rhythmic muscle contraction compressing veins equipped with one-way valves

    Contracting calf muscles squeeze deep veins; one-way venous valves ensure the blood moves only toward the heart. Between contractions the veins refill from below. Valve incompetence (varicose veins) defeats this pump, causing venous pooling, edema, and skin changes — and explains why prolonged standing without walking is so poorly tolerated.

  33. Rapid transfusion of 1 liter of blood in a normovolemic patient will:

    • A. Raise mean systemic filling pressure and increase venous return
    • B. Lower right atrial pressure
    • C. Decrease cardiac output
    • D. Constrict arterioles
    • E. Have no circulatory effect
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Raise mean systemic filling pressure and increase venous return

    Added volume distends the venous reservoir, raising mean systemic filling pressure and the gradient for venous return.   34. Positive inotropic agents such as catecholamines increase myocardial contractility mainly by:

    • A. Decreasing heart rate
    • B. Blocking calcium channels
    • C. Increasing intracellular calcium availability to the contractile machinery
    • D. Reducing preload
    • E. Dilating coronary arteries only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Increasing intracellular calcium availability to the contractile machinery

    Contractile force is determined by calcium binding to troponin C. Beta-1 stimulation raises cAMP, enhancing calcium entry and sarcoplasmic reticulum release, so more cross-bridges form per beat. Digitalis works differently — inhibiting Na+/K+ ATPase to raise intracellular sodium, which slows Na+/Ca2+ exchange and traps calcium inside.

  35. In compensated hemorrhage, venous return is defended by all of the following EXCEPT:

    • B. Arteriolar constriction maintaining arterial pressure
    • C. Tachycardia and increased contractility
    • D. Profound systemic vasodilation and bradycardia
    • E. Transcapillary refill from the interstitium
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Profound systemic vasodilation and bradycardia

    Compensated hemorrhage is a sympathetic triumph: venoconstriction (restoring MSFP), arteriolar constriction (defending pressure), tachycardia/inotropy (maintaining output), and transcapillary refill (plasma volume restoration). Profound vasodilation with bradycardia is the vasovagal pattern — the opposite response, which would be catastrophic in hemorrhage.

  37. In a patient with a low cardiac output but normal arterial oxygen content, tissue oxygen extraction:

    • A. Increases, widening the arteriovenous oxygen difference
    • B. Decreases
    • C. Remains fixed
    • D. Becomes zero
    • E. Reverses direction

  38. A resting adult has a heart rate of 70 beats/min and a stroke volume of 70 ml. What is his approximate cardiac output?

    • A. 2.5 L/min
    • B. 7.0 L/min
    • C. 4.9 L/min
    • D. 10.0 L/min
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. 4.9 L/min

    Cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume: 70 x 70 ml is about 4900 ml/min, or roughly 5 L/min, the normal resting value. Values near 2.5 L/min would imply severe pump failure at rest, while 7–10 L/min are typical of exercise, not rest with these numbers.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 20: Cardiac Output, Venous Return, and Their Regulation
