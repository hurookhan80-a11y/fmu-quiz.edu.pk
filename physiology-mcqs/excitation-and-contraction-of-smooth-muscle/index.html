Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Physiology

Home › Physiology MCQs › Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

Smooth muscle is found in the walls of hollow organs and blood vessels, and it contracts without conscious control. Excitation raises calcium inside the cell, and calcium then activates the contractile machinery through a different mechanism from skeletal muscle. Contraction is slower and more sustained, which suits organs that must maintain tone for long periods.

  1. Single-unit (visceral) smooth muscle is characterized by:

    • A. Each fiber is separately innervated with few gap junctions
    • B. Fibers connect via gap junctions and contract as a single syncytium
    • C. It cannot contract spontaneously
    • D. It lacks actin and myosin
    • E. It is under voluntary control
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Fibers connect via gap junctions and contract as a single syncytium

    Single-unit (visceral) smooth muscle — gut, uterus, ureter, small vessels — is stitched together by gap junctions so an impulse spreads to the whole sheet, which contracts as one syncytium. This suits organs that must squeeze contents along. Multiunit muscle (iris, piloerector, large airways) instead has separately innervated fibers for fine control.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  2. Multiunit smooth muscle differs from single-unit smooth muscle in that multiunit muscle shows:

    • A. Gap junctions throughout
    • B. Independent innervation of individual fibers with few gap junctions
    • C. Syncytial contraction
    • D. Spontaneous pacemaker activity
    • E. Stretch-induced contraction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Independent innervation of individual fibers with few gap junctions

    In multiunit smooth muscle (ciliary muscle, iris, piloerector), each fiber has its own nerve ending and gap junctions are rare, so contraction is graded and localized — like skeletal motor units. This design serves precision tasks such as focusing the lens, unlike the mass squeezing of visceral muscle.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  3. In smooth muscle, the calcium sensor that initiates contraction (analogous to troponin C in skeletal muscle) is:

    • A. Troponin C
    • B. Calmodulin activating myosin light chain kinase
    • C. Tropomyosin shift
    • D. DHP receptors
    • E. Calsequestrin
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Calmodulin activating myosin light chain kinase

    Smooth muscle has no troponin; instead Ca2+ binds calmodulin, and the complex activates myosin light chain kinase (MLCK). MLCK phosphorylates the myosin regulatory light chain, permitting cross-bridge cycling. This calmodulin-MLCK axis is the smooth muscle equivalent of the troponin switch.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  4. Myosin light chain kinase (MLCK) initiates smooth muscle contraction by phosphorylating:

    • A. Troponin I
    • B. The regulatory light chain of myosin
    • C. Tropomyosin
    • D. Actin
    • E. Caldesmon only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The regulatory light chain of myosin

    MLCK transfers phosphate to the 20-kDa regulatory light chain of myosin, which swings the myosin head into its active conformation. Dephosphorylation by myosin phosphatase reverses it. Drugs that raise cGMP (nitrates) or cAMP ultimately reduce MLCK activity, relaxing vascular smooth muscle.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  5. The 'latch mechanism' of smooth muscle allows:

    • A. Rapid cross-bridge cycling
    • B. Dephosphorylated cross-bridges staying attached with very slow cycling and low ATP cost
    • C. Rigor from ATP depletion
    • D. Tetanus
    • E. Increased MLCK activity
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Dephosphorylated cross-bridges staying attached with very slow cycling and low ATP cost

    In the latch state, dephosphorylated myosin heads remain attached to actin, cycling extremely slowly — tension is maintained for hours with minimal ATP use. This is how sphincters, the uterus, and vascular tone stay contracted economically. Skeletal muscle has no equivalent and would fatigue rapidly.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  6. Slow waves in gastrointestinal smooth muscle:

    • A. They are action potentials causing immediate contraction
    • B. They are rhythmic depolarizations that set the timing; superimposed spikes cause contraction
    • C. They hyperpolarize the muscle
    • D. They occur only in multiunit muscle
    • E. They replace gap junctions
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. They are rhythmic depolarizations that set the timing; superimposed spikes cause contraction

    GI smooth muscle membranes oscillate rhythmically (slow waves); when a wave's peak crosses threshold, Ca2+ spike potentials fire and the muscle contracts — so slow waves set the rhythm (e.g., 12/min duodenum, 3/min stomach) while spikes set the force. Hormones and nerves modulate both, tuning motility.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  7. Stress-relaxation of the bladder wall during filling (pressure returning toward normal despite increasing volume) is due to:

    • A. Active pumping of Ca2+ out
    • B. Rearrangement of contractile filaments to the new length (plasticity)
    • C. Increased MLCK activity
    • D. Sympathetic vasoconstriction
    • E. Gap junction closure
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Rearrangement of contractile filaments to the new length (plasticity)

    When the bladder fills, its wall first tenses then yields — the contractile elements rearrange to the new length, so pressure falls back despite the larger volume (Laplace accommodation). This plasticity lets hollow organs store large volumes at low pressure. It reverses when the organ empties.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  8. Sudden stretch of visceral (single-unit) smooth muscle typically causes:

    • A. Relaxation
    • B. Contraction (myogenic response)
    • C. Immediate fatigue
    • D. Conversion to skeletal muscle
    • E. Apoptosis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Contraction (myogenic response)

    Stretch opens mechanosensitive channels, depolarizing visceral smooth muscle and triggering contraction — the myogenic response. It underlies intestinal peristalsis after a bolus, ureteral peristalsis, and the Bayliss effect in arterioles. No nerves are required, though autonomic input modulates it.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  9. Dense bodies in smooth muscle are functionally analogous to:

    • A. T-tubules
    • B. Z discs of skeletal muscle
    • C. Sarcoplasmic reticulum
    • D. Mitochondria
    • E. Synaptic vesicles
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Z discs of skeletal muscle

    Dense bodies — alpha-actinin-rich plaques on the membrane and in the cytoplasm — anchor actin filaments, transmitting cross-bridge force to the cell surface, exactly as Z discs do in striated muscle. Between them, intermediate filaments (desmin/vimentin) form the cytoskeletal lattice.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  10. Caveolae in smooth muscle cells are:

    • A. Store glycogen
    • B. Invaginations of the surface membrane involved in Ca2+ signaling (T-tubule analogues)
    • C. Synthesize ATP
    • D. Anchor to bone
    • E. Release ACh
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Invaginations of the surface membrane involved in Ca2+ signaling (T-tubule analogues)

    Caveolae are flask-shaped membrane invaginations (the smooth muscle substitute for T-tubules) that cluster signaling molecules and may shuttle extracellular Ca2+ inward. They also organize receptors and eNOS signaling in vascular smooth muscle. Their exact contractile role is still being defined.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  11. Calcium for smooth muscle contraction comes from:

    • A. Only the sarcoplasmic reticulum
    • B. Both SR release and extracellular Ca2+ influx
    • C. Only mitochondria
    • D. The nucleus
    • E. T-tubule stores
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Both SR release and extracellular Ca2+ influx

    Unlike skeletal muscle, which relies almost entirely on SR Ca2+, smooth muscle draws on both SR release (IP3/ryanodine) and influx across the surface membrane through voltage-gated and receptor-operated Ca2+ channels. Ca2+ channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem) exploit this dependence to relax vascular smooth muscle.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  12. Myosin light chain phosphatase promotes smooth muscle relaxation by:

    • A. Phosphorylates the light chain
    • B. Dephosphorylates the myosin light chain, promoting relaxation
    • C. Releases Ca2+ from the SR
    • D. Opens gap junctions
    • E. Synthesizes cAMP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Dephosphorylates the myosin light chain, promoting relaxation

    Myosin phosphatase strips the phosphate from the regulatory light chain, inactivating the cross-bridge (except latched ones) — relaxation. Its activity is tuned by Rho-kinase and by cGMP/cAMP pathways, which is how nitrates and beta-agonists relax smooth muscle. The MLCK/phosphatase balance sets tone.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  13. Autonomic nerve fibers innervate smooth muscle through:

    • A. Motor end plates
    • B. Varicosities releasing transmitter diffusely to many fibers
    • C. Neuromuscular spindles
    • D. Tight junctions
    • E. Intercalated discs
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Varicosities releasing transmitter diffusely to many fibers

    Autonomic axons do not form discrete end plates on smooth muscle; instead, beaded varicosities along the axon release transmitter into the interstitium to bathe many fibers — diffuse, graded control suited to tone rather than twitches. One varicosity influences multiple cells via gap-junction spread.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  14. Sympathetic stimulation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle. The receptor mediating this is the:

    • A. Alpha-1 receptors causing contraction
    • B. Beta-2 receptors causing relaxation
    • C. Muscarinic receptors causing contraction
    • D. Nicotinic receptors
    • E. Dopamine receptors
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Beta-2 receptors causing relaxation

    Bronchial smooth muscle carries inhibitory beta-2 receptors: sympathetic (or salbutamol) stimulation raises cAMP, which inhibits MLCK and relaxes the airways — the basis of bronchodilator therapy in asthma. The same transmitter constricts skin vessels via alpha-1 receptors, showing receptor — not transmitter — dictates the response.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  15. Compared with skeletal muscle contraction, smooth muscle contraction is:

    • A. Faster and more forceful
    • B. Slower to develop, far longer-lasting, and much more energy-efficient
    • C. Identical in speed and energetics
    • D. Dependent on troponin
    • E. Always tetanic
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Slower to develop, far longer-lasting, and much more energy-efficient

    Smooth muscle myosin ATPase cycles far slower than skeletal, and the latch mechanism sustains force almost for free — ideal for sphincters, posture of hollow organs, and vascular tone. A skeletal muscle holding the same tension would exhaust its ATP and fatigue within minutes.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  16. Gap junctions between smooth muscle cells permit:

    • A. Mechanical adhesion only
    • B. Electrical coupling, spreading depolarization between cells
    • C. Ca2+ storage
    • D. Protein synthesis
    • E. Hormone secretion
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Electrical coupling, spreading depolarization between cells

    Gap junctions are low-resistance channels (connexins) letting ions and small molecules flow cell to cell, so depolarization spreads through the whole visceral sheet — electrical syncytium behavior. Block them and coordinated peristalsis or uterine labor contractions fragment into chaos.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  17. Nitric oxide (and nitroglycerin) relaxes vascular smooth muscle by:

    • A. Opening L-type Ca2+ channels
    • B. Raising cGMP, which activates myosin phosphatase and lowers cytosolic Ca2+
    • C. Stimulating MLCK
    • D. Blocking K+ channels
    • E. Increasing SR Ca2+ release
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Raising cGMP, which activates myosin phosphatase and lowers cytosolic Ca2+

    Nitric oxide from endothelium diffuses into vascular smooth muscle, activating guanylate cyclase; cGMP activates PKG, which boosts myosin phosphatase and lowers cytosolic Ca2+ — profound relaxation and vasodilation. Nitroglycerin donates NO to relieve angina by this pathway; sildenafil amplifies it by blocking cGMP breakdown.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  18. Oxytocin strengthens uterine contractions during labor by:

    • A. Blocking gap junctions
    • B. Raising intracellular Ca2+ and increasing gap-junction coupling at term
    • C. Inhibiting MLCK directly
    • D. Relaxing via beta-2 receptors
    • E. Depleting ATP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Raising intracellular Ca2+ and increasing gap-junction coupling at term

    Oxytocin raises uterine smooth muscle Ca2+ (via IP3 and influx) and, near term, upregulates gap junctions and oxytocin receptors — converting the uterus into an effective single unit for coordinated labor. Synthetic oxytocin (Pitocin) induces or augments labor by the same mechanism.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  19. The remarkable endurance of tonic smooth muscle contraction (e.g., sphincters) with minimal energy use is explained by:

    • A. Faster cross-bridge cycling
    • B. Slow myosin ATPase activity plus the latch mechanism
    • C. Continuous tetanus
    • D. Larger motor units
    • E. Anaerobic metabolism
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Slow myosin ATPase activity plus the latch mechanism

    Smooth muscle myosin hydrolyzes ATP an order of magnitude more slowly than skeletal myosin, and latch bridges hold tension without cycling — so a contracted sphincter sips energy. This economy is why vascular tone and gut motility can run all day, every day, without fatigue.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 8: Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle

  20. Fine, graded control is possible in multiunit smooth muscle (e.g., iris) because:

    • A. Gap junctions synchronize all fibers
    • B. Each fiber is independently innervated, allowing graded recruitment
    • C. Slow waves pace everything
    • D. Stretch activates all fibers equally
    • E. Hormones bathe all fibers
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Each fiber is independently innervated, allowing graded recruitment

    Because each fiber in multiunit muscle receives its own axon terminal, the CNS can recruit precise numbers of fibers — like skeletal motor units — giving graded, localized responses (pupil diameter, lens focus, piloerection). Single-unit muscle instead fires en masse through gap junctions.
