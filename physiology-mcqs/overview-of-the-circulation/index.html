Overview of the Circulation MCQs for FMU 1st Year MBBS Physiology

Home › Physiology MCQs › Overview of the Circulation

The circulation carries blood from the heart to the tissues and back again in a continuous loop. The systemic circulation supplies the body, the pulmonary circulation carries blood through the lungs, and the two circuits are arranged in series. This arrangement means that all blood passes through the lungs before returning to the tissues.

  1. Epinephrine causes vasoconstriction in some vascular beds and vasodilation in others mainly because:

    • A. Different types of adrenergic receptors are present in different blood vessels
    • B. Different amounts of acetylcholine are released
    • C. Different muscarinic receptors are present
    • D. Different calcium concentrations are present
    • E. Different venous anatomy
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Different types of adrenergic receptors are present in different blood vessels

    Epinephrine has opposite vascular effects because different beds express different adrenergic receptors: alpha-1 receptors on most skin and splanchnic vessels cause vasoconstriction, whereas beta-2 receptors on skeletal muscle and coronary vessels cause vasodilation. The effect depends on receptor subtype and downstream signalling, not on acetylcholine, muscarinic receptors, calcium concentration alone, or venous anatomy.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  2. Which receptor and second messenger are mainly responsible for epinephrine-induced vasoconstriction?

    • A. Beta-2 receptor with increased cAMP
    • B. Alpha-1 receptor with increased IP3/DAG
    • C. Beta-1 receptor with increased cGMP
    • D. Alpha-2 receptor with increased cAMP
    • E. Muscarinic receptor with IP3
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Alpha-1 receptor with increased IP3/DAG

    Epinephrine-induced vasoconstriction is mediated mainly by alpha-1 adrenergic receptors coupled to Gq, which raises inositol trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG), increases cytosolic Ca2+, and contracts vascular smooth muscle. Beta-2/cAMP signalling causes vasodilation, while beta-1, alpha-2/cAMP inhibition, and muscarinic pathways are not the main constrictor mechanism.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  3. Which receptor and second messenger are mainly responsible for epinephrine-induced vasodilation in skeletal muscle vessels?

    • A. Alpha-1 receptor with increased IP3/DAG
    • B. Beta-2 receptor with increased cAMP
    • C. Beta-1 receptor with increased cGMP
    • D. Alpha-2 receptor with decreased cAMP
    • E. Alpha-1 receptor with cAMP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Beta-2 receptor with increased cAMP

    In skeletal muscle vessels, epinephrine acts predominantly on beta-2 receptors coupled to Gs, raising cyclic AMP, which relaxes vascular smooth muscle and produces vasodilation. This permits increased muscle blood flow during exercise and stress. Alpha-1/IP3 signalling in the same vessels would cause constriction, and cGMP is mainly the nitric oxide pathway.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  4. A 60 year old man with long-standing hypertension undergoes Angiography. The reduced ability of his arteries to buffer pulsatile pressure is due to:

    • A. Decreased vascular compliance
    • B. Increased venous compliance
    • C. Increased arterial elastance
    • D. Enhanced baroreceptor sensitivity
    • E. Decreased total peripheral resistance
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Decreased vascular compliance

    Arterial compliance (distensibility) allows arteries to accept stroke volume with only a modest pressure rise. In chronic hypertension arteries stiffen (arteriosclerosis), compliance falls, pulsatile pressure is poorly buffered, and pulse pressure widens. Venous compliance, baroreceptor sensitivity, and peripheral resistance do not explain loss of arterial buffering.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  5. Cerebral blood flow is best preserved during hypotension due to:

    • A. Myogenic autoregulation
    • B. Sympathetic vasoconstriction
    • C. Parasympathetic vasodilation
    • D. Increased venous return
    • E. Enhanced collateral circulation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Myogenic autoregulation

    Cerebral blood flow is autoregulated between about 60-150 mmHg mean arterial pressure mainly by myogenic responses of cerebral arterioles: they dilate when pressure falls and constrict when it rises, keeping flow nearly constant. Metabolic and neurogenic mechanisms contribute, but the rapid pressure buffering in hypotension is myogenic autoregulation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  6. During aerobic exercise, shear stress on vascular endothelial cells increases. Which physiological response is most directly mediated by nitric oxide release?

    • A. Vasoconstriction of resistance vessels
    • B. Vasodilation of arterioles
    • C. Enhanced platelet aggregation
    • D. Increased systemic vascular resistance
    • E. Coronary blood flow reduction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Vasodilation of arterioles

    Increased shear stress on endothelium during exercise stimulates endothelial nitric oxide synthase to release nitric oxide, which relaxes vascular smooth muscle and produces flow-mediated vasodilation of arterioles and conduit vessels. Vasoconstriction would oppose the increased flow, and shear stress does not cause platelet aggregation as its physiological response.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  7. During strenuous exercise, skeletal muscle blood flow increases dramatically. Which factor is the most important mediator?

    • A. Increased sympathetic tone
    • B. Metabolic vasodilation (adenosine, K+, CO2)
    • C. Increased arterial blood pressure
    • D. Parasympathetic activation
    • E. B-adrenergic vasodilation
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Metabolic vasodilation (adenosine, K+, CO2)

    During strenuous exercise local metabolites accumulating in active muscle (adenosine, K+, CO2, H+, and falling PO2) cause intense metabolic vasodilation (active hyperaemia), increasing muscle blood flow 15-25 fold. Sympathetic vasoconstriction is overridden locally, and neural or pressure changes alone cannot account for the magnitude. This matching of flow to metabolism is the dominant mechanism.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  8. Korotkoff sounds are due to:

    • A. Closure of AV valves
    • B. Closure of semilunar valves
    • C. Turbulent blood flow in the artery
    • D. Elastic recoil of arteries
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Turbulent blood flow in the artery

    Korotkoff sounds are produced by turbulent blood flow in the brachial artery when cuff pressure partially occludes it between systolic and diastolic pressures. Blood spurts through the narrowed segment during systole, creating tapping sounds; laminar flow when the cuff is fully released is silent. They are not laminar flow, valve sounds, or venous sounds.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  9. Which organ can tolerate the greatest resistance to blood flow?

    • A. Skin
    • B. Heart
    • C. Brain
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Skin

    Skin blood flow subserves temperature regulation rather than local metabolic need and can vary from near zero in cold (intense sympathetic vasoconstriction) to very high in heat without skin injury. Skin therefore tolerates the greatest imposed resistance (flow reduction) of any organ. Brain, heart, and kidney cannot tolerate prolonged near-zero flow.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  10. Vascular compliance (distensibility) is best defined as:

    • A. The total blood volume contained in the veins
    • B. The resistance offered to blood flow
    • C. The change in pressure for a given change in flow
    • D. The increase in volume per unit increase in pressure
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. The increase in volume per unit increase in pressure

    Compliance equals change in volume divided by change in pressure and describes how easily a vessel distends. Resistance relates pressure to flow and is a different vascular property.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  11. Which part of the circulation acts as the main reservoir of blood, normally containing about 60% of the total blood volume?

    • A. Arteries
    • B. Capillaries
    • C. Arterioles
    • D. Veins
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Veins

    Veins are highly compliant capacitance vessels and normally hold about 60% of the blood volume as a mobilizable reservoir. Arteries hold a much smaller fraction at high pressure.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  12. Blood flow through a vessel is:

    • A. Independent of resistance
    • B. Directly proportional to resistance and inversely proportional to pressure difference
    • C. Determined by vessel length alone
    • D. Directly proportional to pressure difference and inversely proportional to resistance
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Directly proportional to pressure difference and inversely proportional to resistance

    Flow equals pressure difference divided by resistance, so it rises with driving pressure and falls as resistance increases. This is the hydraulic equivalent of Ohm's law for the circulation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  13. Local control of blood flow means that blood flow is adjusted mainly by:

    • A. Central venous pressure alone
    • B. Hormones only, with no local factors
    • C. Baroreceptor reflexes alone
    • D. Changes in the tissues themselves according to their metabolic demand
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Changes in the tissues themselves according to their metabolic demand

    Local mechanisms such as metabolic vasodilatation match perfusion to tissue oxygen and nutrient needs independently of systemic pressure control. Baroreceptors regulate overall arterial pressure, not the distribution to individual tissues.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  14. Highest vascular compliance is found in the:

    • A. Arteries
    • B. Arterioles
    • C. Veins
    • D. Capillaries
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Veins

    Veins are the most distensible vessels and act as the main blood reservoir, holding the largest share of blood volume. Arteries and arterioles have much thicker, less compliant walls.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  15. Damping of the pressure pulse as it travels towards the arterioles and capillaries can be decreased by:

    • A. Decreasing total peripheral resistance
    • B. Increasing total peripheral resistance
    • C. Increasing arterial compliance
    • D. Decreasing blood viscosity to zero
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Increasing total peripheral resistance

    Damping (progressive loss of pulsation) is reduced when peripheral resistance is high, because more of the pressure pulse is reflected/transmitted rather than absorbed. Greater compliance increases damping by absorbing the pulse.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  16. Pulse pressure is defined as the:

    • A. Mean arterial pressure
    • B. Difference between systolic and diastolic pressure
    • C. Product of stroke volume and heart rate
    • D. Pressure at the dicrotic notch
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Difference between systolic and diastolic pressure

    Pulse pressure = systolic - diastolic pressure (normally about 40 mmHg) and mainly reflects stroke volume and arterial compliance. Mean arterial pressure is diastolic plus one-third of the pulse pressure.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  17. Korotkoff sounds heard during blood-pressure measurement are due to:

    • A. Closure of semilunar valves
    • B. Closure of AV valves
    • C. Turbulent blood flow in the compressed artery
    • D. Elastic recoil of the aorta
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Turbulent blood flow in the compressed artery

    Cuff pressure between systolic and diastolic levels makes flow through the brachial artery turbulent, producing Korotkoff sounds. Valve closures produce heart sounds, which are different in origin and timing.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation

  18. The most important factor determining blood flow to a particular area is:

    • A. Venous compliance alone
    • B. Vascular resistance of that area
    • C. Haematocrit alone
    • D. Blood viscosity alone
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Vascular resistance of that area

    By the flow equation Q = pressure gradient / resistance, local vascular resistance—set mainly by arteriolar radius—controls distribution of flow to each tissue. Viscosity and compliance affect flow/pressure but resistance is the dominant regulator.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 14: Overview of the Circulation
