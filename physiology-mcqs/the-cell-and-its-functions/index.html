Home › Physiology MCQs › The Cell and Its Functions

The cell is the basic unit of the body, and each cell is bounded by a membrane that controls what enters and leaves. Inside, the cytoplasm contains organelles such as the nucleus, mitochondria, and endoplasmic reticulum, each with a specialised job. Together these parts obtain energy, build new molecules, and allow the cell to respond to its surroundings.

  1. The basic structural framework of the cell membrane is the:

    • A. Protein monolayer
    • B. Phospholipid bilayer
    • C. Cholesterol crystal lattice
    • D. Glycoprotein mesh
    • E. Lipopolysaccharide sheet
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Phospholipid bilayer

    Two layers of phospholipids arrange tail-to-tail, with hydrophobic fatty acid tails inward and hydrophilic phosphate heads facing the water on both sides. This bilayer is a fluid barrier to water-soluble substances. Embedded proteins then provide channels, carriers, receptors, and enzymes.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  2. Transmembrane (integral) proteins of the cell membrane function chiefly as:

    • A. Energy storage molecules
    • B. Channels, carriers, receptors, and enzymes
    • C. Calcium buffers
    • D. DNA templates
    • E. Structural cement only
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Channels, carriers, receptors, and enzymes

    Proteins spanning the bilayer form selective pores (channels), shuttle molecules across (carriers), bind hormones and transmitters (receptors), and catalyze reactions (enzymes). Peripheral proteins attach to one surface and often anchor the cytoskeleton. Together they give each membrane its functional identity.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  3. The loose carbohydrate coat on the outer surface of the cell membrane is called the:

    • A. Glycocalyx
    • B. Basal lamina
    • C. Periplasm
    • D. Pellicle
    • E. Zona pellucida
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Glycocalyx

    The glycocalyx is a fuzzy layer of carbohydrate chains attached to membrane proteins and lipids. It protects against physical injury, provides cell-identity markers, mediates cell-cell adhesion, and serves as the binding site for many hormones. Its negative charge also repels other negatively charged cells.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  4. Cholesterol-rich LDL particles are taken into cells by:

    • A. Simple diffusion through the bilayer
    • B. Receptor-mediated endocytosis via clathrin-coated pits
    • C. Phagocytosis by pseudopods
    • D. Facilitated diffusion through GLUT carriers
    • E. Osmosis through aquaporins
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Receptor-mediated endocytosis via clathrin-coated pits

    LDL binds specific surface receptors that cluster in clathrin-coated pits, which pinch off as coated vesicles. Inside, the LDL is delivered to lysosomes and its cholesterol released. Defective LDL receptors cause familial hypercholesterolemia — a direct clinical consequence of failed receptor-mediated endocytosis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  5. The organelle that contains acid hydrolases for digesting ingested bacteria and worn-out organelles is the:

    • A. Mitochondrion
    • B. Peroxisome
    • C. Lysosome
    • D. Ribosome
    • E. Centrosome
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Lysosome

    Lysosomes are membrane-bound sacs of digestive enzymes that work best at acid pH. They digest phagocytosed microbes, recycle damaged organelles (autophagy), and remove obsolete tissue. Leakage of these enzymes causes cell injury, and their inherited deficiencies produce lysosomal storage diseases such as Tay-Sachs.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  6. ATP is generated in largest amounts by which organelle?

    • A. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
    • B. Golgi apparatus
    • C. Mitochondrion
    • D. Lysosome
    • E. Nucleolus
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Mitochondrion

    Mitochondria carry out oxidative phosphorylation: the electron transport chain on the inner membrane pumps protons, and ATP synthase harnesses the gradient to make ATP — about 34 per glucose versus 2 from glycolysis. They contain their own DNA and ribosomes, a legacy of their bacterial origin.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  7. Rough endoplasmic reticulum is specialized for:

    • A. Steroid hormone synthesis
    • B. Protein synthesis for secretion or membrane insertion
    • C. Calcium storage in muscle
    • D. Lipid digestion
    • E. DNA replication
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Protein synthesis for secretion or membrane insertion

    Ribosomes studding the rough ER translate proteins destined for secretion, lysosomes, or the membrane itself. Smooth ER instead synthesizes lipids and steroids and detoxifies drugs. Cells with heavy secretory loads — pancreatic acinar cells, plasma cells — are packed with rough ER.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  8. The Golgi apparatus functions mainly in:

    • A. ATP synthesis
    • B. Post-translational modification, packaging, and sorting of proteins
    • C. Phagocytosis of bacteria
    • D. Cholesterol synthesis
    • E. Muscle contraction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Post-translational modification, packaging, and sorting of proteins

    Proteins arriving from the rough ER are glycosylated, trimmed, and sorted in the Golgi stacks, then shipped in vesicles to lysosomes, secretory granules, or the plasma membrane. It is the cell's post office. Defects in Golgi glycosylation cause congenital disorders of glycosylation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  9. Microtubules are essential for all of the following EXCEPT:

    • A. Formation of the mitotic spindle
    • B. Beating of cilia and flagella
    • C. Intracellular vesicle transport
    • D. Contraction of skeletal muscle sarcomeres
    • E. Maintenance of cell shape
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Contraction of skeletal muscle sarcomeres

    Microtubules (tubulin polymers) build the spindle, cilia/flagella (with dynein), and intracellular highways for vesicles. Skeletal muscle contraction uses actin microfilaments and myosin, not microtubules. Colchicine and vincristine act by disrupting microtubules, arresting mitosis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  10. Actin microfilaments are directly involved in:

    • A. Oxidative phosphorylation
    • B. Muscle contraction and amoeboid cell movement
    • C. DNA transcription
    • D. Lysosomal digestion
    • E. Steroid synthesis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Muscle contraction and amoeboid cell movement

    Thin actin filaments slide against myosin in muscle and power crawling movements of white blood cells and fibroblasts. Together with intermediate filaments and microtubules they form the cytoskeleton. Cytochalasins, which cap actin filaments, paralyze cell motility — demonstrating actin's mechanical role.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  11. During mitosis, sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles in:

    • A. Prophase
    • B. Metaphase
    • C. Anaphase
    • D. Telophase
    • E. Interphase
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Anaphase

    In anaphase, centromeres split and spindle microtubules pull sister chromatids apart. Metaphase aligns chromosomes on the equatorial plate, prophase condenses them, and telophase rebuilds the nuclear envelopes. Nondisjunction in anaphase causes trisomies such as Down syndrome.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  12. Chromosomes line up on the equatorial plate of the cell during:

    • A. Prophase
    • B. Metaphase
    • C. Anaphase
    • D. Telophase
    • E. Cytokinesis
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Metaphase

    Metaphase is the alignment checkpoint: spindle fibers attach to kinetochores and arrange chromosomes at the cell equator. Only when every kinetochore is attached does anaphase begin. This checkpoint prevents unequal chromosome distribution to daughter cells.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  13. Cellular ATP is required for all of the following EXCEPT:

    • A. Active transport across membranes
    • B. Skeletal muscle contraction
    • C. Glandular secretion
    • D. Simple diffusion of oxygen
    • E. Conduction of nerve impulses
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Simple diffusion of oxygen

    Simple diffusion is passive — molecules move down their concentration gradient with no energy input. ATP powers active transport, cross-bridge cycling, exocytosis of secretory products, and the Na+/K+ pump that maintains the gradients behind nerve impulses. About 95% of ATP comes from mitochondria.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  14. Exocytosis is best described as:

    • A. Engulfment of large particles by pseudopods
    • B. Fusion of intracellular vesicles with the plasma membrane, releasing their contents outside
    • C. Uptake of fluid droplets into pinocytotic vesicles
    • D. Digestion of organelles by lysosomes
    • E. Diffusion of lipid-soluble hormones into the cell
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Fusion of intracellular vesicles with the plasma membrane, releasing their contents outside

    In exocytosis, secretory vesicles dock and fuse with the surface membrane, expelling hormones, enzymes, or neurotransmitters. It is the mirror of endocytosis and requires Ca2+ and ATP. Synaptic transmitter release and insulin secretion are classic examples.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  15. Phagocytosis differs from pinocytosis in that phagocytosis:

    • A. Takes up fluid and dissolved solutes
    • B. Ingests large particles such as bacteria and cell debris
    • C. Requires clathrin-coated pits
    • D. Occurs only in liver cells
    • E. Needs no ATP
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Ingests large particles such as bacteria and cell debris

    Phagocytosis ('cell eating') extends pseudopods around large targets — bacteria, dead cells — forming phagosomes that fuse with lysosomes. Pinocytosis ('cell drinking') internalizes extracellular fluid in small vesicles and occurs in most cells. Both are energy-requiring forms of endocytosis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  16. Nuclear pores permit the passage of:

    • A. Whole ribosomes into the nucleus
    • B. mRNA out to the cytoplasm and proteins into the nucleus
    • C. DNA out to the mitochondria
    • D. Lysosomal enzymes into the nucleus
    • E. Cholesterol into chromatin
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. mRNA out to the cytoplasm and proteins into the nucleus

    The nuclear envelope is perforated by pores that selectively transport macromolecules: messenger RNA exits for translation while histones, polymerases, and ribosomal proteins enter. Small molecules diffuse freely. This regulated traffic couples nuclear gene expression to cytoplasmic protein synthesis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  17. Peroxisomes are chiefly involved in:

    • A. Protein glycosylation
    • B. Oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids and detoxification reactions
    • C. ATP synthesis
    • D. Calcium storage
    • E. Ribosome assembly
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids and detoxification reactions

    Peroxisomes use oxidases that generate hydrogen peroxide, then immediately destroy it with catalase. They shorten very-long-chain fatty acids before mitochondria finish the job and detoxify alcohol and other compounds. Their failure causes Zellweger syndrome, with severe neurological impairment.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  18. The cytosol is best defined as:

    • A. The entire cell contents including organelles
    • B. The fluid portion of the cytoplasm excluding organelles
    • C. The fluid inside mitochondria
    • D. The nucleoplasm
    • E. The extracellular fluid
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The fluid portion of the cytoplasm excluding organelles

    Cytoplasm = cytosol + organelles; cytosol is the aqueous gel in which organelles are suspended and where glycolysis and many metabolic reactions occur. Keeping the terms distinct matters because enzymes and substrates partition between cytosolic and organelle compartments.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  19. Clathrin-coated pits on the cell surface are specialized for:

    • A. Simple diffusion of gases
    • B. Receptor-mediated endocytosis
    • C. Active transport of sodium
    • D. Osmotic water movement
    • E. Gap junction communication
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Receptor-mediated endocytosis

    Clathrin triskelions assemble into a basket-like coat that deforms the membrane into a pit, concentrating hormone, lipoprotein, or nutrient receptors. The pit pinches off as a coated vesicle, then sheds clathrin for reuse. This is how cells internalize LDL, transferrin, and many peptide hormones.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions

  20. A child presents with chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, situs inversus, and male infertility. The underlying defect is most likely in:

    • A. Lysosomal acid hydrolases
    • B. Ciliary dynein (microtubule motor protein)
    • C. Mitochondrial DNA
    • D. Nuclear pore complexes
    • E. Ribosomal RNA
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Ciliary dynein (microtubule motor protein)

    This is primary ciliary dyskinesia (Kartagener syndrome): defective dynein arms paralyze cilia, so mucus cannot be cleared (respiratory infections, bronchiectasis), sperm cannot swim (infertility), and embryonic nodal cilia fail to establish left-right asymmetry (situs inversus). Cilia are microtubule-based, so the defect is cytoskeletal.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed. — Ch. 2: The Cell and Its Functions
