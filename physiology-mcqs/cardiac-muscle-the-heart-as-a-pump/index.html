Home › Physiology MCQs › Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

Cardiac muscle is the involuntary, striated muscle of the heart wall, and its cells are connected so the whole heart contracts together. The heart works as a pump with two sides: the right side drives blood through the lungs and the left side drives it through the body. Valves keep blood flowing in one direction only.

  1. Aortic stenosis most directly leads to which change in left ventricular workload?

    • A. Decreased left ventricular contractility as the primary change
    • B. Increased left ventricular afterload
    • C. Increased venous return to the left atrium
    • D. Decreased left ventricular preload
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Increased left ventricular afterload

    A narrowed aortic valve increases the pressure the left ventricle must generate to eject blood, which is an increase in afterload. Preload refers to end-diastolic stretch and is not the primary change in aortic stenosis.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  2. What is the main function of the atrioventricular valves?

    • A. To prevent backflow from the aorta into the left ventricle
    • B. To generate the first heart sound by opening
    • C. To conduct impulses from atria to ventricles
    • D. To prevent backflow from the ventricles into the atria
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. To prevent backflow from the ventricles into the atria

    The mitral and tricuspid valves close during ventricular systole to prevent regurgitation into the atria. Prevention of backflow from the great arteries into the ventricles is the function of the semilunar valves.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  3. Closure of the atrioventricular valves at the start of ventricular systole produces which heart sound?

    • A. S3
    • B. S4
    • C. S1
    • D. S2
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. S1

    S1 is produced by closure of the mitral and tricuspid valves when ventricular pressure exceeds atrial pressure. S2 is produced later by closure of the aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  4. Closure of the semilunar valves at the end of ventricular systole produces which heart sound?

    • A. Opening snap
    • B. S2
    • C. S1
    • D. S3
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. S2

    When ventricular pressure falls below arterial pressure, the aortic and pulmonary valves close and produce S2. S1 marks the earlier closure of the AV valves.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  5. The plateau phase of the cardiac muscle action potential is sustained mainly by:

    • A. Cl- influx
    • B. Rapid Na+ influx through fast sodium channels
    • C. K+ efflux through inward rectifier channels
    • D. Ca++ influx through L-type calcium channels
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Ca++ influx through L-type calcium channels

    Slow L-type Ca++ channel opening maintains the plateau and triggers calcium-induced calcium release for contraction. Fast Na+ influx causes only the initial upstroke.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  6. The ability of cardiac muscle (nodal tissue) to generate its own spontaneous rhythm is called:

    • A. Conductivity
    • B. Frank-Starling mechanism
    • C. Autorhythmicity
    • D. Refractoriness
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Autorhythmicity

    Autorhythmicity is the self-excitation of pacemaker tissue due to gradual diastolic depolarization, especially in the SA node. The Frank-Starling mechanism relates stretch to force of contraction, not to impulse generation.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  7. In cardiac muscle, the main function of the transverse (T) tubules is to:

    • A. Store the bulk of calcium for contraction
    • B. Rapidly propagate the action potential into the interior of the cell
    • C. Produce ATP for contraction
    • D. Anchor the cell at intercalated discs
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Rapidly propagate the action potential into the interior of the cell

    T tubules carry the action potential deep into the cardiac myocyte so that calcium channels open synchronously throughout the cell. Calcium is stored mainly in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, not in the T tubules.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  8. Most transfer of blood from the atria into the ventricles during diastole occurs during which phase?

    • A. Atrial systole alone
    • B. Isovolumetric relaxation
    • C. Isovolumetric contraction
    • D. Rapid ventricular filling (early diastole)
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Rapid ventricular filling (early diastole)

    When the AV valves open, blood that accumulated in the atria rushes into the relaxed ventricles during rapid filling, providing most of ventricular filling. Atrial contraction adds only the final smaller fraction.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  9. Intercalated discs in cardiac muscle provide:

    • A. Only mechanical attachment with no electrical role
    • B. Storage of glycogen
    • C. Production of pacemaker impulses
    • D. Electrical conductance between cells and mechanical connection
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Electrical conductance between cells and mechanical connection

    Gap junctions in intercalated discs allow ions and action potentials to pass from cell to cell, while desmosomes and fascia adherens provide mechanical coupling. This makes cardiac muscle function as an electrical syncytium.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  10. End-diastolic volume is best defined as:

    • A. The volume in the atrium at the end of systole
    • B. The volume in the ventricle just before it contracts (at the end of diastole)
    • C. The volume ejected per beat
    • D. The volume remaining after systole
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. The volume in the ventricle just before it contracts (at the end of diastole)

    End-diastolic volume is the filled volume in the ventricle immediately before contraction and determines preload and stroke volume via the Frank-Starling mechanism. The volume remaining after ejection is end-systolic volume.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  11. What is the approximate duration of ventricular systole at resting heart rate?

    • A. 0.1 second
    • B. 0.8 second
    • C. 0.5 second
    • D. 0.3 second
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. 0.3 second

    At ~72 beats/min the cardiac cycle is ~0.8 s, with ventricular systole about 0.3 s and diastole about 0.5 s. Atrial systole alone is ~0.1 s, while 0.8 s is the whole cycle.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  12. The ventricular volume curve falls most steeply during which phase?

    • A. Ejection phase
    • B. Isovolumetric relaxation
    • C. Rapid passive filling
    • D. Isovolumetric contraction
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: A. Ejection phase

    Ventricular volume only falls when blood is ejected into the aorta and pulmonary artery during the ejection phase. During isovolumetric phases volume is constant, and during filling it rises.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  13. What does parasympathetic (vagal) stimulation do to the heart?

    • A. Increases heart rate only
    • B. Decreases heart rate and decreases contractility, mainly atrial
    • C. Increases both heart rate and contractility
    • D. Has no effect on the heart
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Decreases heart rate and decreases contractility, mainly atrial

    Acetylcholine from the vagus slows SA-node firing and AV conduction and modestly reduces contractility, with the strongest inotropic effect in atria and little direct ventricular effect. Sympathetic stimulation does the opposite.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  14. Cardiac efficiency is best defined as:

    • A. Stroke volume divided by end-diastolic volume only
    • B. Cardiac output multiplied by heart rate
    • C. Mean arterial pressure divided by cardiac output
    • D. Cardiac work output divided by energy (oxygen) input
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Cardiac work output divided by energy (oxygen) input

    Cardiac efficiency = external work output / metabolic energy input, normally ~20-25%. Output/energy wording captures this ratio, unlike ejection fraction (SV/EDV) or resistance (pressure/output).

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  15. Opening of the mitral valve initiates which phase?

    • A. Ejection phase
    • B. Isovolumetric contraction
    • C. Rapid passive ventricular filling
    • D. Atrial systole
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: C. Rapid passive ventricular filling

    When ventricular pressure falls below atrial pressure the mitral valve opens and rapid passive ventricular filling begins. Atrial systole later tops up filling, while ejection requires AV valves to be closed.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  16. Immediately after the QRS complex, the ventricles enter:

    • A. Rapid passive filling
    • B. Isovolumetric contraction
    • C. Isovolumetric relaxation
    • D. Atrial systole
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Isovolumetric contraction

    Ventricular depolarisation (QRS) triggers ventricular contraction; with all valves briefly closed this starts as isovolumetric contraction until semilunar valves open. Relaxation and filling occur after ejection, around/after the T wave.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  17. Afterload is best described as:

    • A. Venous return
    • B. Peripheral (arterial) resistance / arterial pressure against which ventricle ejects
    • C. Heart rate
    • D. End-diastolic volume
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: B. Peripheral (arterial) resistance / arterial pressure against which ventricle ejects

    Afterload is the load against which the ventricle contracts, determined mainly by arterial pressure/peripheral resistance. End-diastolic volume defines preload, not afterload.

    Source: Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump

  18. When heart rate increases from 72 to 180 beats/min, which phase of the cardiac cycle is shortened proportionally the most?

    • A. Ventricular systole
    • B. Atrial systole
    • C. Isovolumetric contraction
    • D. Ventricular diastole
    Show answer and explanation

    Answer: D. Ventricular diastole

    As heart rate rises, the whole cycle shortens, but ventricular diastole shortens far more than systole. This is why very fast rates reduce ventricular filling time and coronary perfusion time.

    Source: Guyton and Hall — Ch. 9: Cardiac Muscle; The Heart as a Pump
